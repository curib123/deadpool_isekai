# Volume 1 Chapter Roadmap — Greywake Arc

**Status:** CURRENT CANON ROADMAP — 2026-10-03  
**Range:** CH001–CH026  
**POV:** Distant third-person limited — Luck Everhart  
**Power:** Fortune Distortion only  
**Architecture:** `volumes/VOLUME-001-GREYWAKE-ARCHITECTURE.md`

This roadmap describes current chapter function. Manuscript canon controls line-level execution.

# Movement I — The Undefined Stranger

## CH-001 — Wrong Forest, Wrong World
**Objective:** survive the Hollow March and find civilization.  
**Conflict:** unknown forest, predator, damaged memory, no local knowledge.  
**Luck engine:** multiple pre-established environmental accidents create survival openings.  
**Character function:** establish theatrical instinct before reputation exists.  
**Structural change:** he chooses the name **Luck Everhart** after surviving.  
**Ending pull:** smoke/road signs point toward civilization while another coincidence follows him.

## CH-002 — Undefined
**Objective:** enter Greywake and obtain food/shelter without indefinite detention.  
**Conflict:** gate/registry systems cannot classify him.  
**Luck engine:** minimal; administrative pressure owns the chapter.  
**Misunderstanding:** some officials read composure as concealment rather than confusion.  
**Change:** Luck becomes an Unverified Entrant instead of a normal traveler.  
**Ending pull:** the anomaly needs independent verification.

## CH-003 — The Things They Can Measure
**Objective:** determine whether the identification failure is Greywake's problem or his.  
**Conflict:** physical measurements work while identity data does not.  
**Supporting owner:** Maelis owns measurement/evidence.  
**Luck engine:** none required.  
**Change:** repeatability proves the anomaly is not one broken gate tool.  
**Ending pull:** Luck still needs a practical way to earn money without normal registration.

# Movement II — Frontier Work

## CH-004 — Off the Books
**Objective:** secure money and supplies.  
**Conflict:** Hesk offers useful work without standard protections.  
**Character function:** Luck knowingly accepts a bad-but-useful arrangement.  
**Luck engine:** minor reputation side-effect from ordinary cart/equipment failure.  
**Change:** Luck becomes irregular Road Guild labor.  
**Ending pull:** first field assignment.

## CH-005 — A Better Hammer
**Objective:** help complete road/bridge work and get paid.  
**Conflict:** damaged equipment, dangerous worksite, frontier pressure.  
**Luck engine:** existing defects/tools/terrain produce an improbable practical opening; no conjured object.  
**Supporting owner:** workers/foreman still solve the job through actual craft.  
**Change:** workers begin reading Luck's improvised timing as hidden expertise.  
**Ending pull:** his usefulness creates more work and more attention.

## CH-006 — The Road That Works
**Objective:** complete caravan work on the maintained route.  
**Conflict:** Tavian sees animal movement that contradicts seasonal expectations.  
**Luck engine:** action uses positioning and existing road geometry, not magic construction.  
**Supporting owner:** Tavian owns ecological interpretation.  
**Change:** the story establishes that the road works while the animals do not.  
**Ending pull:** abnormal movement needs investigation.

## CH-007 — You Should Be Dead
**Objective:** survive a displaced predator attack and get injured people back.  
**Conflict:** real wounds, rescue pressure, bad assumptions about where danger should be.  
**Luck engine:** Fortune Distortion reduces catastrophe but does not erase injury.  
**Supporting owner:** Sela owns treatment.  
**Change:** Luck's mortality becomes undeniable; rumors about survival intensify.  
**Ending pull:** repeated displacement no longer looks isolated.

## CH-008 — Outside the Lanterns
**Objective:** help on an outer route.  
**Conflict:** poor warnings, weak maintenance, scarce protection.  
**Supporting owner:** Kellan owns engineering/community reality.  
**Luck engine:** minor only if needed; work competence remains human.  
**Change:** Luck sees that outer communities bear costs the main road hides.  
**Ending pull:** disparity becomes part of the mystery.

# Movement III — The Road Is Lying

## CH-009 — Wrong Tracks
**Objective:** verify Tavian's migration pattern.  
**Conflict:** field evidence contradicts Guild maps.  
**Supporting owner:** Tavian.  
**Luck engine:** chance may expose a useful track or timing window, but cannot interpret it.  
**Change:** incompatible species routes form a pattern.  
**Ending pull:** records must be compared with field reality.

## CH-010 — The Ledger Doesn't Balance
**Objective:** reconcile casualties, contracts, and missing workers.  
**Conflict:** Guild paperwork explains away too many losses.  
**Supporting owner:** Maelis; Jessa holds partial operational knowledge.  
**Luck engine:** none required.  
**Change:** Luck realizes his own off-book status makes him easy to erase too.  
**Ending pull:** evidence points toward an abandoned route.

## CH-011 — Broken North
**Objective:** verify physical manipulation on a closed road.  
**Conflict:** unstable terrain, ridge-hounds, recent maintenance where none should exist.  
**Luck engine:** visible structural failures create survival openings without impossible effects.  
**Supporting owners:** Kellan/Tavian/Maelis each own engineering, ecology, documentation.  
**Change:** hidden Guild-manufactured lure equipment is discovered.  
**Ending pull:** one stake implies a network.

## CH-012 — Someone Put This Here
**Objective:** determine what the lure does and whether it is isolated.  
**Conflict:** evidence is politically dangerous and technically incomplete.  
**Luck engine:** no knowledge shortcut.  
**Change:** deliberate long-term intervention becomes the leading explanation.  
**Ending pull:** the pattern must be confronted institutionally.

## CH-013 — Where They Die
**Objective:** put field evidence, casualty patterns, and route records in one room.  
**Conflict:** Hesk frames the system as frontier necessity.  
**Luck engine:** none.  
**Change:** risk is shown to fall repeatedly on politically weak places.  
**Ending pull:** Greywake must decide whether ending the policy is survivable.

# Movement IV — Necessary Sacrifices

## CH-014 — Necessary Losses
**Objective:** decide what can safely change before migration worsens.  
**Conflict:** Hesk's strongest argument is partly true: blind shutdown can redirect danger.  
**Supporting owner:** Tavian owns the uncertainty.  
**Change:** no-change / no-secret-control temporary position is established.  
**Ending pull:** predator pressure appears near eastern homesteads.

## CH-015 — One Stake Too Many
**Objective:** respond to immediate outer-homestead danger.  
**Conflict:** waiting risks civilians; acting risks destabilizing the network.  
**Character function:** Luck chooses intervention from anger, autonomy, and urgency.  
**Luck engine:** may help him survive the local action but cannot make the decision correct.  
**Change:** a lure site is disrupted.  
**Ending pull:** pressure moves somewhere unexpected.

## CH-016 — Backflow
**Objective:** evacuate people and contain the redirected surge.  
**Conflict:** multiple species move through newly opened corridors.  
**Luck engine:** rescue openings only; specialists coordinate routes.  
**Change:** Luck's intervention is confirmed as part of the causal chain.  
**Ending pull:** casualties/damage create political leverage for Hesk.

## CH-017 — The Price of Being Right
**Objective:** stabilize casualties and decide whether to restore the sacrificial system.  
**Conflict:** Hesk uses the backflow as proof that his policy was necessary.  
**Supporting owners:** Sela handles bodies; Maelis/Tavian handle evidence.  
**Luck engine:** none required.  
**Change:** Luck accepts factual responsibility without accepting Hesk's conclusion.  
**Ending pull:** a third option is now necessary.

# Movement V — No Acceptable Loss

## CH-018 — No Acceptable Loss
**Objective:** design a third option before the main surge arrives.  
**Conflict:** damaged roads, limited labor, incomplete maps, distrust.  
**Luck engine:** none required in planning.  
**Change:** specialists combine knowledge instead of treating Luck as the solution.  
**Ending pull:** they choose to make a new/old corridor usable.

## CH-019 — Build the Wrong Road
**Objective:** prepare Broken North as a safer migration corridor.  
**Conflict:** time, material shortages, terrain, local predator pressure.  
**Supporting owners:** Kellan/work crews/Tavian.  
**Luck engine:** existing material failures and timing may create openings; no magical construction.  
**Change:** the corridor becomes plausible but fragile.  
**Ending pull:** Hesk still has lure stock/control options.

## CH-020 — The Last Lure
**Objective:** secure remaining lure material and records before Hesk restores the sacrificial route.  
**Conflict:** Hesk loyalists, evidence risk, Jessa's employment/family stakes.  
**Luck engine:** minor pursuit/obstruction coincidence only if causally established.  
**Change:** Jessa's evidence becomes actionable and Hesk loses covert freedom.  
**Ending pull:** migration arrives before politics can fully settle.

# Movement VI — Greywake Holds

## CH-021 — Before the Herd
**Objective:** evacuate exposed people and position the plan.  
**Conflict:** limited carts, injured residents, damaged routes, fear.  
**Luck engine:** restrained/minor.  
**Change:** everyone owns a defined job; Luck does not own the whole plan.  
**Ending pull:** prey mass appears beyond the lantern line.

## CH-022 — First Wave
**Objective:** keep the prepared corridor open during initial migration pressure.  
**Conflict:** barricades fail, animals scatter, predators exploit confusion.  
**Luck engine:** chance creates openings inside the prepared defense; it does not replace it.  
**Change:** the route holds but the largest territorial threat emerges.  
**Ending pull:** infrastructure starts failing under peak pressure.

## CH-023 — Greywake Holds
**Objective:** hold the choke point long enough for the migration body to commit.  
**Conflict:** major predator + failing route infrastructure.  
**Luck engine:** a visible chain of timing/terrain failures creates one decisive opening.  
**Supporting owners:** militia/work crews/Tavian.  
**Change:** physical line holds.  
**Ending pull:** Hesk's final lure threatens to redirect the herd again.

## CH-024 — No One Gets Chosen
**Objective:** stop the final sacrificial redirection without losing the migration corridor.  
**Conflict:** Hesk, immediate civilians, route geometry, Luck's desire to confront him personally.  
**Character function:** Luck chooses the action that preserves the collective plan over the most satisfying confrontation.  
**Luck engine:** opening only; no omnipotent solution.  
**Change:** migration commits to the prepared route and Hesk loses operational control.  
**Ending pull:** survival gives way to accountability.

## CH-025 — What Greywake Counts
**Objective:** count casualties, damage, evidence, and responsibility.  
**Conflict:** some residents still believe Hesk's policy was necessary.  
**Luck engine:** none.  
**Change:** Guild authority is restructured/limited and outer communities gain enforceable representation in route decisions.  
**Ending pull:** Luck's growing local role becomes its own threat to his freedom.

## CH-026 — Before They Decide What I Am
**Objective:** leave Greywake on his own terms.  
**Conflict:** staying means becoming hero, anomaly, employee, patient, weapon, or symbol according to somebody else's needs.  
**Luck engine:** minimal; departure is character-driven.  
**Change:** Luck leaves with money, wounds in recovery, reputation, and unresolved identity.  
**Ending pull:** wider world, no named next destination required in the chapter itself.
