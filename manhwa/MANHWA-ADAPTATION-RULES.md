# Manhwa Adaptation Rules

## Source Lock

Every episode begins by naming the exact canonical manuscript/chapter and the supporting scene, continuity, character, world, system, and mystery files required for that episode.

## Allowed Adaptation

- shorten prose where the image carries the same information;
- convert narration into acting, expressions, staging, environment, or SFX;
- split one prose beat into multiple panels for timing;
- combine minor prose beats when no information or consequence is lost;
- shorten dialogue while preserving intent, characterization, factual meaning, and outcome;
- use vertical whitespace for suspense, comedy timing, scale, and reveals.

## Forbidden Adaptation

- inventing powers because they look cool;
- moving mystery reveals earlier;
- changing who knows what;
- changing injuries or recovery timing;
- changing character motives or moral choices;
- adding romance, relationships, lore, factions, items, or abilities not authorized upstream;
- changing a destination's culture or architecture merely for aesthetics;
- making comedy erase consequences.

## First-Person Voice

Red Jackal remains the first-person storyteller. Narration boxes, reader-facing remarks, dialogue, and visual acting may share the work, but the adaptation must preserve his established deadpan/meta voice.

## Visual Compression Rule

When prose is removed, record what visual element now communicates the same story fact in the episode adaptation breakdown.

## Canon Escalation

If an episode genuinely needs a story change, stop adaptation at that point and revise canon through the novel pipeline first.
