# Chapter 6 — The Road That Works

Luck chose a place at the front of the caravan, where the morning light gave his coat a respectable outline.

The lead guard sent him to the third wagon.

"I'm wasted back there."

"You're assigned back there."

Luck moved. Five wagons left Greywake, and the world continued without appreciating the composition it had lost.

A charged speech token traveled in the lead wagon. Luck stayed close enough to understand the shouted instructions as the drivers kept their spacing and two Guild guards rotated along the line. He had left his broken post secured with the third wagon's cargo, available when his ankle needed it.

Yesterday's payment had bought a bed. Today's arrangement had bought him another day of watching the road. Apparently surviving a repair qualified a man to stand near other expensive things while they moved.

The road itself was less careless than the people praising him. Gravel filled the low places. Drainage cuts carried water clear of the wheels. Brush had been cut back far enough that anything large would have to cross open ground to reach the wagons.

Every few hundred paces, Luck saw something somebody had repaired.

At the toll marker, a stranger was crouched beside the drainage channel. A polearm lay within reach of his hand. Luck approached to discover what could make mud so interesting.

"Don't step there."

He stopped with one boot raised. The stranger pointed at a faint print almost beneath it, then returned his attention to a deeper track crossing the ditch.

The lead guard came over. "We're on schedule."

"Then give me a moment to tell you whether that's useful."

The guard glanced at the insignia near the stranger's shoulder. "Luck Everhart, irregular Guild labor. Tavian Rook, Crownspine Warden liaison."

Tavian looked at Luck briefly, then at the mud again. Luck found the order of priority offensive.

"What are we looking at?"

"A browser crossing from the southeast. A predator crossing later from the northwest."

"Animals crossing a road."

"Animals crossing a road they don't usually need."

Tavian indicated the separate directions. The browser should have been moving farther east toward wetter ground; the predator normally ranged north before the late rains. Weather might account for it. So might a chase.

"But I've found six crossings like this in three days," he said.

The guard uncrossed his arms. "Attacks?"

"Crossings. Don't turn one observation into another."

When the toll workers waved the caravan forward, Tavian picked up his polearm and walked beside the road. Luck matched his pace until the guard pointed him back toward the third wagon.

"You're joining us?" Luck called.

"My next survey point is the rest post."

"That sounds like joining us."

"It isn't."

Luck decided the man was going to make ordinary conversation expensive.

Before noon they reached the guarded rest post. Drivers watered the teams beneath an awning. Fresh timber stood beside a repair shed, and the well had a roof better maintained than several buildings Luck had passed inside Greywake.

A narrow side road branched west. It had none of those advantages. Grass grew between its ruts, one ditch had collapsed, and brush leaned close to the wheels.

Tavian crouched near its entrance. This time Luck stopped behind him without stepping on anything important.

"Small farms and wood camps," Tavian said when he asked what lay down the spur.

"Lower priority?"

"Lower traffic," the nearby guard corrected.

Luck looked from the fresh timber to the collapsed ditch. "Those aren't always the same thing."

"No," the guard said. He didn't offer another answer.

Tavian showed Luck layered hoofprints and claw marks. Older animals had moved west, newer ones northeast; hunters had passed south. Different directions, repeated use of the same narrow approach.

"Why?" Luck asked.

"That's what I'm trying to find out."

The guard called them back before Luck could improve the question. They left the rest post with watered teams, checked tags, and a route nobody had officially closed.

An hour later, Tavian stopped walking.

He raised a hand. The lead guard saw it and called a halt just as brush crashed ahead of the first wagon.

A horned animal burst onto the road. Mud streaked its flank. It struck the drainage lip, stumbled, and swerved toward the gap between the second and third wagons.

A predator came out behind it.

Suddenly every team wanted to move. Drivers fought the reins while passengers began climbing down into the same gaps the animals were trying to use.

"Hold the wagons!" the lead guard shouted.

"Passengers behind the line," Tavian said. "Keep the ditch side open."

Luck stepped into the gap ahead of the third wagon. A clear space, a charging creature, a coat that would have looked excellent from the road.

"Move right," Tavian snapped.

"I have the gap."

"You're closing its escape."

Luck looked again. The horned animal was already turning away from him—toward a passenger crouched beside the wagon step.

He moved right.

Hooves struck gravel where he had been standing. The browser found the open ditch and went through, missing the passenger without knowing she existed. Luck caught the wagon rail as mud spattered his boots.

The predator came next. Its prey had escaped, but there were people between it and the road back.

Tavian moved to open that route. The lead guard held his spear toward the animal without advancing. Behind Luck, the second guard gathered the last passengers into cover.

Luck wanted a better weapon than his tiny knife. There was no time to arrange one.

The predator darted left. He shifted to keep it away from the wagon, matching the guard rather than inventing an angle of his own. For two breaths, they left it an open retreat and no useful way forward.

It bared its teeth.

Luck kicked gravel toward its face. The creature recoiled; the guard drove his spear point into the road with a hard crack. The predator turned into the gap Tavian had left and ran.

Nobody chased it.

The passenger beside the step began shaking after she stood. Tavian checked that no one had been struck before he went back to the tracks.

Luck stayed by the wagon until his hands stopped gripping the rail.

"That was worse than mine," he said when Tavian approached.

"Your what?"

"First day. Forest animal. Bigger than that one. Rotten post, bad footing, a vine. Half a tree nearly landed on us."

"What stopped it?"

"The branch. Something howled. It reconsidered."

He omitted how nearly his knees had failed during the final stare. Tavian looked at the bandage on his forearm, but another call took him to the ditch before he asked anything else.

Beneath the fresh chase marks lay older tracks. Not the same animals, Tavian said: different weights and strides. The lead guard crouched beside him and checked the overlapping routes.

"The caravan got through," the guard said.

"Yes."

"We can continue."

"Yes. Keep the spacing."

Luck waited until the guard returned to the lead wagon. "So the road is safe?"

Tavian stood. "We got these people past one chase. That's what happened."

No reassuring story. Just the size of the thing they had actually done.

Luck returned to the third wagon—the place the guard had needed him, not the place he had selected for the light. As the wheels began turning, he watched Tavian take one last look at the old tracks beneath the new ones.

The road had worked. The guards had worked. One word from a man who understood animals had stopped Luck from driving a frightened creature into a passenger.

Next time, he might not hear the word in time.
