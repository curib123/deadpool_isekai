The predator stepped into Broken North.

For one second, nobody moved.

Too large for the road.

Too close to frightened prey.

Too territorial to care.

Then the herd behind it surged.

"Do not close on it!" Tavian shouted.

Three militia had started inward.

They stopped.

"North line back two steps," Brynn ordered. "Spears low. Workers off the edge."

The formation changed around the animal.

Fast enough.

The predator hit a guide frame with one shoulder.

Timber exploded sideways.

Prey split around it and compressed again.

I had answers.

Too many.

A wall.

A trench.

A cage.

A hole where the animal used to be.

The dangerous part was not whether I could end the predator.

I could.

The dangerous part was ending it in a way that made several hundred frightened animals choose a worse direction.

"Not yet," Tavian said.

He watched the animal.

"It wants space."

Brynn kept her eyes on the line.

"Where?"

"Not here. Not while the front is crossing."

Every escape route the animal tested was currently another body, worker, slope, or prey line.

If I solved only the predator, I would choose where the rest of the disaster moved.

A work crew near the lower repair point got a gap.

"Move!" Brynn called.

Four workers ran with timber and rope.

The predator turned.

"Back!"

Three reached the marked line.

The fourth slipped beside the road shelf.

The predator moved.

I was already there.

I pulled the worker upright.

The predator lunged.

This time the game stopped being interesting.

I caught it.

One hand against the side of its head.

One against the shoulder ridge.

The charge drove my boots backward through gravel.

Then stopped.

The predator pushed.

It got nowhere.

For one second the militia line forgot to breathe.

I looked at the animal.

"No."

I turned.

Several hundred kilograms of bad judgment changed direction because I decided it would.

It stumbled sideways, recovered, and stared at me.

There were easier ways to finish this.

Tavian shouted:

"Do not kill it!"

"I wasn't—"

"You were thinking about it."

Fair.

He pointed toward the upper brush.

"It wants that service cut. Clear it."

Brynn moved the crew immediately.

"Upper team! Clear the service cut. Lower shields keep it off the workers."

Good.

A solution that did not require making the migration react to a corpse, explosion, or theatrical crater.

Then Broken North made a worse sound.

Stone grinding.

The inner shelf dropped.

Fill spilled into the drainage.

One timber support shifted.

The usable lane narrowed while prey were still crossing.

"Brace slipped!" the foreman shouted.

Brynn looked at him.

"Can you reset?"

"Not with this load!"

The road moved again.

A browser stumbled and shoved smaller animals outward.

Tavian looked from road to herd.

"That shelf goes, we lose the inside line."

Then he pointed.

"Hold that."

Specific.

Good.

I looked at the failing shelf.

I could have replaced the entire road.

Perfect stone.

Wide corridor.

Strong enough for anything.

But a sudden perfect road would change animal choices in ways Tavian had not planned.

So I gave him what he asked for.

At first.

Pale-grey support appeared under the shelf.

Workers moved.

Then the crack spread farther than the original failure point.

The foreman went pale.

"Whole inner lip!"

Workers backed away.

The road started to go.

I looked at it.

"No."

The same instruction expanded.

Hold the road.

Pale-grey support extended beneath the failing lip along the old retaining line.

Not a new power.

Not an upgrade.

Not a desperate higher stage.

I had always been capable of more.

I had simply been choosing small shapes because small shapes were usually enough.

Now more road needed holding.

So more road was held.

The entire failing edge stopped falling.

Instantly.

Brynn recovered first.

"Repair crew! Move!"

Workers crawled under the protected side with timber, wedges, stone, and rope.

I stepped away from the support.

It remained.

The foreman looked at me.

He had assumed I needed to stand there concentrating.

Reasonable.

Wrong.

The predator charged toward the workers.

I met it before they did.

This time I did not let it touch me.

I caught the ridge of its skull and put it into the road hard enough to shake loose dust.

Not dead.

Tavian had asked for an exit.

The animal came up furious.

I stepped close.

"Wrong direction."

I shoved its head north.

It resisted.

Briefly.

Militia backed away.

Smart.

Tavian pointed toward the service cut.

"Almost open! Keep it facing north!"

"I am."

"You are antagonizing it."

"Also north-facing."

No one appreciated the distinction.

Behind us:

"First timber set!"

"Second brace coming!"

Another prey group entered.

Brynn turned a ridge-hound away with the flat of her sword while a militia spear blocked the other angle.

Nobody needed me.

Good.

The large predator circled.

I circled with it.

It started looking confused.

Understandable.

Tavian watched the prey.

"Not yet."

The service cut was open enough for the predator.

Not enough for the smaller animals crossing in front of it.

So we waited.

The predator and I stood three paces apart while a migration passed between us.

Then the foreman shouted:

"Load test!"

Workers eased weight onto permanent timber.

The road settled.

My support stayed where it was.

"Again!"

More load.

Timber creaked.

"Primary holds!"

The foreman crawled out.

"Release the outer third."

Specific again.

I withdrew part of the support.

Real timber took the load.

"Next third."

I removed more.

Still held.

He pointed at the worst crack.

"Keep that."

Then Tavian raised one hand.

The prey line thinned.

He looked at the service cut.

"Now."

Brynn shouted.

"Upper line open! Lower shields forward! Give it the lane!"

The predator saw space.

It lunged north.

Nobody blocked it.

It tore through the service cut and vanished uphill.

One militia man started after it.

Brynn's voice stopped him.

"No chase!"

Correct.

The predator was not the objective.

Greywake was.

The next wave entered.

The road held.

Barely.

The foreman checked his braces.

"Release."

I withdrew the final impossible support.

Ordinary timber, stone, rope, and old masonry took the road back.

It settled.

Stopped.

Then the upper guide failed.

Prey spilled toward a work crew.

Brynn shouted.

"Left line!"

I looked at Tavian.

He was already pointing.

"Do nothing."

My hands tightened.

The obvious answer was available.

"If you put a wall there, they bunch," he said.

"I know."

"If you move them, the rear follows the new visual line."

"I know."

"Then trust the workers."

I hated that sentence.

The workers cut the failed rope and dragged the broken panel flat.

They opened space instead of closing it.

The herd spread.

Pressure dropped.

Tavian was right.

Again.

Power had never been the missing information.

The next minutes became movement.

Militia shifted.

Workers replaced markers only during gaps.

Brynn sent runners before people asked.

Tavian changed guide angles as behavior changed.

I carried broken timber and pulled one worker upright.

Then a ridge-hound broke toward an injured militia man.

I was closer.

A pale-grey platform appeared under the hound's chest and lifted it just high enough that its legs stopped touching ground.

The animal twisted in outrage.

Tavian stared.

"Put it down north."

"Obviously."

The platform moved three paces.

Lowered.

Vanished.

The hound landed and ran after the herd.

Brynn looked at me.

"Necessary?"

"No."

"Useful?"

"Yes."

She considered.

"Fine."

I accepted the ruling.

By degrees, the physical line stabilized.

The main body committed more strongly to Broken North.

Prey stopped testing every side opening.

Ridge-hounds followed movement instead of workers.

The repaired shelf held.

Then Tavian looked toward the lower fork.

His face changed.

I followed his sightline.

A trailing branch of prey was bending outward.

Toward the outer homestead route.

Another group followed.

Then another.

The physical line was holding.

Greywake had done its part.

Hesk's lure was pulling them away from it again.
