# Chapter 24 — No One Gets Chosen

**Status:** DRAFT — GATE 9 NOT YET RUN  
**Word Count:** PENDING

The physical line was holding.

That should have felt like victory.

Instead, Tavian pointed toward the lower fork.

The branch pulling away from Broken North had grown since the first turn. It was not the main body, but it was no longer a few frightened animals testing open ground either. Browsers at the front kept taking the same angle. Smaller prey followed them. The path they were choosing ran toward the outer homestead route.

Brynn saw it.

“How long?”

Tavian watched three groups move through the split before answering.

“Not long enough.”

He looked back toward Broken North. The main corridor was still carrying most of the migration. Workers and militia were holding the damaged choke without us standing over them. The ordinary reinforcement under the inner shelf was still taking weight.

Then Tavian looked at the wrong branch again.

“That pull is the old-service line.”

Brynn’s face hardened.

“The vulnerable site.”

“Yes.”

My side hurt every time I breathed. The wound from the predator had mostly stopped bleeding, but the rest of me had not received the message that we were done. My hands were shaking. My leg felt unreliable. The pressure behind my eyes had become part of the landscape.

I still started walking.

Brynn did too.

The service path cut away from Broken North through lower brush and old roadwork. We had not gone far when two figures came from the opposite approach.

Maelis carried a wrapped packet against her chest.

Jessa carried a copied route sheet and looked like she had spent the last hour expecting to be fired, trampled, or both.

Brynn did not waste time asking why they were there.

“Site?”

Jessa pointed downslope.

“Same housing. Same sector code from the storehouse records.”

Maelis lifted the packet.

“Copies of the custody entries, service references, and Jessa’s signed explanation. Originals are still secured.”

Good.

Nobody had brought the only proof into the middle of a migration.

Tavian took one look at the route sheet Jessa held.

“Show me the physical line.”

She pointed to the old road fork.

He compared it with the movement beyond the trees.

“That is it.”

Brynn drew her sword.

Not dramatically.

Just because there was finally a person at the end of this problem.

We reached the site from the upper side.

The old-service housing sat inside a shallow stone recess beside the fork. I recognized the same family of equipment we had been dealing with for days: fitted stake, vented housing, service plate, reservoir assembly.

Nothing new.

Nothing mystical.

Hesk stood beside it.

The Roadmaster field-service key was already in the housing.

He looked tired.

Not defeated.

That would have been easier.

The vent assembly was open farther than the coalition setting had allowed. One directional plate had been turned toward the outer road.

Jessa saw it immediately.

“He changed the output direction.”

Tavian looked through the brush toward the moving branch.

“Yes.”

Brynn stepped forward.

“Hands away from the housing.”

Hesk looked at her.

“Captain.”

“Now.”

He did not move.

Maelis came to the edge of the recess.

“We have the reserve records. We have the issue entries. We have witness statements. Destroying this site will not erase what happened.”

“I am not destroying it.”

“No,” she said. “You are using it.”

Hesk’s eyes moved to the migration below.

“Because your replacement is failing.”

“The line is holding,” Brynn said.

“For now.”

Tavian answered before she could.

“The main body is committed far enough that the corridor can carry it if you stop pulling the trailing branch away.”

Hesk finally looked at him.

“And if Broken North collapses?”

“Then we deal with the collapse we actually have.”

Hesk’s jaw tightened.

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the only honest one.”

The animals below shifted.

A new group took the outer angle.

Hesk pointed toward them.

“You see a branch. I see what happens after Greywake loses the main road. I have seen it before.”

Nobody interrupted him.

Maybe because we already knew the story.

Winter isolation.

Missing caravans.

People starving while distant officials discussed whether Greywake was worth saving.

Hesk looked at Brynn.

“You know what the road carries.”

“I do.”

“Food. Medicine. Metal. Relief. Work. Everything this settlement cannot grow or make alone.”

“I know.”

“And you are gambling that on a damaged road nobody trusted a week ago.”

Brynn’s expression did not change.

“We are refusing to route the cost through inhabited farms.”

Hesk looked at me.

“You understand risk better than they do.”

I almost laughed.

There was nothing funny in it.

He continued.

“You have survived injuries that would stop any person here. You make decisions because you can afford consequences other people cannot. Do not pretend you are different from me.”

That landed closer than I wanted.

He knew it.

I looked at the housing.

Then at the outer road.

The problem with a good accusation was that denying it did not make it wrong.

I had made choices like that.

I had acted because I could survive what happened next.

Other people had paid for parts of it.

But there was a difference.

I looked back at him.

“I don’t get to choose them either.”

Hesk’s hand moved.

Jessa saw it first.

“He’s changing the plate again.”

Hesk turned the service key.

The vent shifted.

Tavian looked toward the migration.

“Outer pull is strengthening.”

Brynn moved.

“Hesk. Step away.”

He pulled the key free.

Then he stepped backward from the recess.

Not toward us.

Toward the lower path.

Brynn cut him off.

He changed direction.

I was closer.

I moved in front of him.

Hesk stopped.

For a second, neither of us did anything.

He looked at my hands.

Still shaking.

Then at the blood dried along my coat.

Then at my face.

“You can barely stand.”

“Yes.”

“You cannot stop the migration.”

“No.”

“You cannot hold Greywake together.”

“No.”

His eyes narrowed.

“Then move.”

I did not.

Behind me, Tavian called out.

“Red.”

Not a warning.

A direction.

I looked.

The outer branch had widened again.

Hesk saw me look.

That was the choice.

Stay here.

Keep him in front of me.

Finish the part that felt personal.

Or turn away while he was still standing.

Brynn was already there.

She did not need me to become her jailer.

I stepped aside.

Not for Hesk.

For her.

Brynn took his wrist.

He resisted once.

She twisted the arm behind him and drove him against the stone edge of the service recess.

The key fell.

Jessa picked it up.

Hesk tried to turn.

Brynn held him.

“You are relieved of emergency road authority.”

“You do not have the council vote.”

“I have a live emergency, an unauthorized active lure, and your key in our hands.”

Maelis said, “And witnesses.”

Hesk looked at her.

She did not look away.

Tavian shouted again.

“Fork!”

I turned my back on Hesk.

That felt better than hitting him would have.

The split between Broken North and the outer road had become a moving line of bodies.

Tavian ran ahead of me and stopped near a low stone shoulder where the paths divided.

“Here.”

I looked at the ground.

He pointed.

“Not across the road. Angle them north. Only the front.”

The pressure behind my eyes answered before I did.

My hands shook harder.

I had one useful thing left.

Maybe.

I focused.

The shape came slowly.

Too slowly.

Pale-grey material pushed up along the fork.

Low.

Angled.

The near end formed crooked, then cracked before the far end was finished.

I kept it simple.

Not wall.

Not fence.

One direction.

The guide leaned toward Broken North.

The first browser reached it and turned.

The next hit the cracked edge with one shoulder.

The manifestation shuddered.

Held.

Three more animals followed the first turn.

Then five.

The branch hesitated.

“Now!” Tavian shouted.

Behind me, Jessa dropped beside the old-service housing.

Brynn still had Hesk under control.

Maelis stood near the recess with the copied records and watched every hand that touched the site.

Jessa inserted the Roadmaster key.

Her hands shook too.

She turned it once.

Stopped.

“Vent first.”

Tavian looked at the animals.

“Wait.”

The outer branch pressed against my guide.

The construct cracked wider.

My side pulled with every breath.

My leg wanted to fold.

I kept both hands down because raising them did not make the thing stronger.

It only made me feel like I was doing something.

Tavian watched the lead animals.

“Now.”

Jessa closed the vent assembly.

The sound was small.

Metal against metal.

No flash.

No dramatic collapse of invisible power.

The animals did not instantly turn.

They kept moving on momentum.

My guide took another impact.

The outer face split.

Jessa moved to the service plate.

“This returns it neutral.”

Maelis said, “Do it.”

Jessa rotated the plate back through the service stop.

The keyed housing clicked.

Tavian watched the route.

Nothing happened.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Hesk spoke from behind Brynn.

“You think that fixes years of pressure?”

Tavian did not answer him.

He was watching the herd.

The first change was small.

One animal at the outer edge stopped following the lower path and turned after the bodies already moving through Broken North.

Then another.

The lead branch hit my guide again.

The pale-grey edge collapsed halfway.

I held the idea.

One direction.

That was all.

A second cluster chose north.

Then a third.

Tavian raised one hand.

“Keep the plate neutral.”

Jessa kept it there.

Brynn looked at Maelis.

“Can it be secured?”

“Yes,” Jessa answered. “Key out after the service catch. Then pin the housing closed.”

“There is a spare locking pin in the side kit,” Maelis said.

She had read the inventory.

Jessa found it.

Ordinary metal.

Ordinary procedure.

She set the service catch, removed the key, and passed it to Brynn.

Then she drove the pin through the housing lock.

The lure stopped being Hesk’s decision.

My construct broke.

The far half vanished first.

The near half lasted another second, then disappeared under the next group of prey.

I did not try again.

I could not have made anything useful if I wanted to.

The herd kept moving.

Not perfectly.

Not all at once.

But the branch was no longer being pulled outward.

The animals at the front followed the line already worn into Broken North.

The groups behind followed them.

The outer route emptied by degrees.

Tavian stood at the fork and watched until enough bodies had passed that there was no longer a meaningful choice at the split.

Then he lowered his hand.

“Committed.”

Brynn exhaled once.

Maelis looked at the secured housing.

Jessa looked at the key in Brynn’s hand.

Hesk said nothing.

He did not apologize.

He did not need to.

Brynn bound his wrists with a militia restraint cord from her belt.

“You are under emergency detention pending council and Ledger review.”

Hesk looked at the migration.

The main body was moving through Broken North.

The damaged road held.

The outer homestead route did not receive the redirected mass he had tried to send there.

His plan was over.

Not because Greywake had found a way to save everything without cost.

The road was damaged.

People were injured.

Trade would suffer.

Nothing about the day had become easy.

But the cost had stopped being one man’s private decision.

I leaned against the old stone beside the fork.

My hands were still shaking.

My side still hurt.

The last thing I had made was already gone.

That was fine.

Broken North remained full of moving animals.

The outer road remained clear.

Hesk was under guard.

And no one had been chosen to lose.
