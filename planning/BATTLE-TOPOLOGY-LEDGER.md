## 1. Purpose

Track action topology, objectives, terrain, supporting-cast ownership, and consequences across destination arcs. The ledger prevents repetitive action design and prevents Red's overwhelming power from being disguised as a numerical power ladder.

An action scene exists because the situation creates it. No chapter receives combat merely to satisfy a quota.

## 3. Topology Vocabulary

Valid forms include 1v1, 2v1, 1v2, 1v4/1vMany, 2v2, uneven team, rotating opponents, split-party, escort, defensive hold, pursuit/interception, rescue under attack, three-sided conflict, environmental action, and multi-front action.

The chosen topology must change tactics, movement, information, priorities, or consequence. Variety is not a novelty checklist.

## 7. Red and Supporting-Cast Rules

Red is already overwhelmingly powerful. If he does not end a conflict immediately, the manuscript must make the reason legible: incomplete information, multiple objectives, collateral risk, hostages, evidence, timing, curiosity, theatrics, restraint, reputation, or preserving another character's agency.

When Red becomes serious, restraint drops; a new power tier does not appear. Regeneration does not erase the cost of injury, and Play Logic does not become a finite stamina bar.

Supporting characters must retain their own expertise and causal role. Enemies must have concrete objectives, limited knowledge, adaptive behavior, and credible retreat, surrender, reposition, escalation, or death conditions.
