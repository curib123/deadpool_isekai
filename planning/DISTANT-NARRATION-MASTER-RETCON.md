# Distant Narration Master Retcon

**Project:** This Isekai Has a Bug. Unfortunately, It's Me.  
**Status:** LOCKED AUTHORIAL RETCON  
**Effective:** 2026-09-26  
**Priority:** Overrides all conflicting close-first-person / first-person-narrator rules in older files.

# 1. Locked POV

All standard Red Jackal chapters use:

**DISTANT THIRD-PERSON LIMITED**

Narration uses:
- Red / he / him / his;
- external scene description;
- observable behavior;
- selective brief Red thought;
- no head-hopping.

Red is the focal character, but not the narrator.

The narrator should remain outside Red while still staying close enough to his scene for the prose to feel immediate, readable, and alive.

# 2. Lively Web-Novel Presentation — LOCKED

Distant narration must **not** become dry, detached, or report-like.

The default scene rhythm is:

**visible action / setting → lively dialogue → physical reaction or silence → optional short Red thought → consequence / next move**

Use:
- short, mobile-friendly paragraphs;
- clear physical movement;
- expressive faces and gestures;
- interruptions;
- teasing;
- unfinished replies;
- dry reactions;
- arguments that move while people speak;
- dialogue with subtext rather than exposition dumps;
- occasional one-line impact paragraphs;
- selective private thought when the exact wording adds comedy, tension, contradiction, or personality.

Avoid:
- distant narration that reads like a summary;
- dialogue where everyone politely waits their turn;
- long blocks explaining what a character already showed;
- constant narrator interpretation of Red's feelings;
- turning every joke into internal monologue.

# 3. Internal Thought

Direct Red self-thought is allowed and encouraged **selectively**.

Use it when:
- the exact wording is funny;
- Red privately contradicts what he says aloud;
- a choice becomes sharper when the reader gets one quick thought;
- action or dialogue cannot efficiently convey the beat;
- Jackal Luck produces a suspicious coincidence and Red privately notices it;
- Red's theatrical confidence hides a simpler private reaction.

Preferred:

Red stared at the locked door.

*Could break it.*

He tried the handle instead.

Or:

The guard dropped the only key directly beside Red's boot.

Red looked down.

*That was offensively convenient.*

He picked it up and smiled.

"Exactly as planned."

Do not turn third-person narration into disguised first-person thought-stream prose.

Self-thought should normally stay short: a line, a phrase, or one brief paragraph.

# 4. Dialogue

Dialogue carries a major share of Red's personality.

Red's:
- humor;
- arrogance;
- cluelessness about his ceiling;
- theatricality;
- meta-awareness;
- selfishness;
- accidental-mastermind energy

should usually be visible through what he says and does.

Supporting characters must also keep distinct rhythms. They should interrupt Red, ignore jokes, push back, misunderstand him, or continue working while he performs.

A scene should rarely become two talking heads exchanging exposition.

# 5. Narration Distance

The narrator may describe Red's visible mood or simple immediate inference, but should not constantly translate his mind for the reader.

Preferred:

The room held one desk, three chairs, and shelves packed to the ceiling.

Red looked around once.

"Paperwork won."

Optional thought:

*Tragic.*

This keeps the narration distant while preserving Red's internal flavor.

# 6. Power Presentation in Prose

Red's current power identity is governed by `planning/RED-JACKAL-PLAY-LOGIC-LUCK-RETCON.md`:

**PLAY LOGIC + JACKAL LUCK + FOURTH-WALL PAUSE**

There is **no separate regeneration power**.

When Red's body recovers from impossible injury, prose should present it as the same impossible reality behavior associated with Play Logic, without announcing a skill activation or using game-like healing terminology.

Jackal Luck should normally be shown as suspicious coincidence, timing, probability, or absurd convenience rather than labeled in-scene.

A good Red beat is:

**coincidence helps → Red notices privately → Red shamelessly takes public credit → witnesses assume foresight**

# 7. Fourth-Wall Pause

Fourth-Wall Pause remains canon from CH-001.

A genuine reader address is a deliberate POV exception:
- local Veyran time freezes;
- Red may directly address the external audience in first person because he is literally speaking;
- narration resumes in third person when the pause ends.

# 8. Preservation

This retcon changes narration style, not:
- plot;
- chapter order;
- world;
- mystery truth;
- cast motives;
- battles;
- consequences;
- Greywake outcome;
- Merrowgate plan.

Power presentation follows the separate current power retcon and may revise old regeneration terminology into Play Logic body restoration while preserving the event itself.

1. distant-third-person narration;
2. lively dialogue appropriate to the scene;
3. selective Red self-thought where useful;

5. current Play Logic + Jackal Luck power presentation;
6. character voice check;
7. continuity check;
8. Gate 9 revalidation;
