# 0. Series Opening Lock

CH001 is the single official beginning of the story.

It contains the material that previously existed as a separate prologue:
- Earth explosion;
- memory damage;
- first arrival in Veyr;
- theatrical instinct before fame;
- first visible Fortune Distortion timing;
- forest survival;
- self-chosen name Luck Everhart.

Do not recreate a standalone prologue unless the author explicitly reverses this lock.

# 1. Core Story Engine

Luck Everhart is a physically mortal outsider whose only supernatural ability is passive **Fortune Distortion**. He never identifies it as his own supernatural power.

The recurring engine is:

**ordinary desire → concrete obstacle → Luck improvises or performs → staged ordinary variables align improbably → somebody still has to act → witnesses infer deliberate mastery → Luck preserves the useful mystique → the misunderstanding creates a later consequence**

This engine is a tool, not a mandatory beat in every scene.

# 2. Scene Structure — Hard Rule

Every scene must answer:

1. **Goal:** What does the viewpoint character want right now?
2. **Obstacle:** What concretely prevents it?
3. **Attempt:** What action is taken?
4. **Turn:** What new information, failure, arrival, or causal event changes the situation?
5. **Change:** What is different when the scene ends?
6. **Carryover:** What consequence survives into a later scene?

A scene fails structurally if it only explains lore, repeats a conclusion already reached, or ends in the same practical state in which it began.

# 3. Staging — Hard Rule

Before action uses an element, establish enough of it for the reader to picture the event:
- participants;
- important objects;
- exits;
- hazards;
- useful distances;
- terrain;
- moving machinery/animals;
- who is responsible for what.

Do not reveal a perfectly useful rope, loose beam, cart, window, lever, or escape route at the exact instant Luck needs it unless its prior absence is itself explained.

# 4. Chapter Structure

Every chapter must contain:

- an immediate objective/problem/question within the opening movement;
- at least one meaningful obstacle;
- at least one choice that belongs specifically to Luck or another focal character;
- a state change that cannot be removed without affecting the next chapter;
- consequence continuity;
- a concrete forward pull.

A chapter may be action, investigation, politics, travel, work, aftermath, or relationship conflict. It does not need a battle.

A chapter fails if it is only:
- exposition;
- travel without pressure/change;
- discussion repeating known facts;
- admiration of Luck;
- setup whose payoff has no cost;
- aftermath that resets injuries, money, trust, or politics.

# 5. Fortune Distortion Causality

Every major lucky outcome must be reconstructable.

Before the payoff, establish at least some of:
- bad footing;
- weather;
- material fatigue;
- equipment condition;
- crowd movement;
- animal behavior;
- timing windows;
- another character's independent plan;
- bureaucracy/procedure;
- social assumptions;
- route geometry.

Then show:
**variable → shift → opening → action → result → consequence**

Fortune Distortion may bias probability. It may not create the variable from nothing.

Luck never:
- activates luck;
- tests luck as a power;
- plans around guaranteed luck;
- commands an exact result;
- calls Fortune Distortion his ability.

# 6. Misunderstanding Variety

Do not repeat “Luck accidentally wins, witness calls him a genius” as the only pattern.

Rotate among:
- accident interpreted as strategy;
- failed cool pose interpreted as intimidation;
- vague line interpreted as prediction;
- another character succeeds because Luck accidentally opened the route;
- Luck retreats and observers read it as a deliberate lure;
- Luck is blamed for something he did not plan;
- Luck receives an obligation because people believe he promised more than he did;
- an enemy overreacts to his reputation;
- a practical character refuses the legend and forces Luck to do ordinary work;
- the misunderstanding harms his legal/social position;
- Luck's performance simply fails and nobody cares.

Every major misunderstanding must alter a decision, expectation, relationship, threat, payment, investigation, or future obligation.

# 7. Supporting Character Independence

Supporting characters must:
- have goals not centered on Luck;
- possess expertise Luck lacks;
- sometimes disagree with him correctly;
- solve parts of problems without him;
- suffer or benefit according to their own choices;
- never become incompetent merely to magnify Luck.

Luck can steal presentation. He cannot steal everyone else's causality.

# 8. Dialogue Structure

Dialogue must perform work:
- persuade;
- refuse;
- negotiate;
- investigate;
- hide;
- threaten;
- test;
- accuse;
- coordinate;
- repair;
- reveal a value conflict.

Cut dialogue that only restates exposition both speakers already know.

Characters must not share the same rhythm. Practical specialists speak differently from politicians, clerks, workers, and performers.

# 9. Luck Character Lock

Luck remains:
- dry;
- theatrical;
- opportunistic;
- freedom-seeking;
- curious;
- vain about presentation;
- privately less certain than he looks;
- capable of fear and seriousness;
- willing to accept useful credit;
- unwilling to knowingly sacrifice someone merely to preserve his image.

He does not become a solemn chosen hero.

Growth is allowed in:
- ordinary competence;
- relationships;
- knowledge of Veyr;
- judgment;
- consequences remembered;
- ability to use openings once they exist.

Growth is not allowed in:
- supernatural power tiers;
- conscious luck control;
- regeneration;
- time manipulation;
- hidden second abilities.

# 10. Consequence Ledger — Hard Rule

Track across chapters:
- injuries and recovery;
- spent money;
- broken/lost equipment;
- damaged locations;
- deaths;
- promises;
- debts;
- legal status;
- rumors;
- political authority;
- relationships/trust;
- evidence;
- travel time;
- unresolved mysteries.

Comedy never resets consequence.

# 11. Seriousness and Comedy

Comedy may exist beside danger, but not erase it.

After:
- death;
- severe injury;
- betrayal;
- irreversible loss;
- public disaster;

allow the emotional consequence to land before returning to comedy.

Luck's serious mode is quieter, not a transformation.

# 12. Mystery Discipline

A mystery advances only when:
- new evidence appears;
- an old fact gains a new interpretation;
- a character gains information they did not have;
- a false theory becomes less viable;
- a protected clue is deliberately planted.

Do not advance cosmology simply because a local arc needs a dramatic line.

# 13. Arc Structure

Each destination arc must have:
- a local identity independent of Luck;
- a local problem that existed before him;
- a local antagonist/faction with credible incentives;
- a reason Luck becomes involved that fits his immediate desires;
- midpoint change;
- major reversal with cost;
- climax requiring multiple competencies;
- local resolution;
- surviving consequences;
- departure or durable status change.

Luck may reroute the local story. He does not erase it.

# 14. Greywake Structural Spine

Greywake's current spine is:

**arrival/identity failure → irregular work → route disparity → migration contradiction → hidden lure evidence → political conflict → premature intervention/backflow → third-option planning → migration crisis → final lure confrontation → institutional aftermath → voluntary departure**

This order is preserved unless a chapter-level audit finds a direct causality break.

# 15. Merrowgate Structural Spine

Merrowgate's current spine is:

**arrival/cost pressure → identity liability → reputation economy → paid dock integration → Cassian's credible security argument → suspicious off-manifest work → sabotage pattern → profit/authority midpoint → evidence links Luck to the bad job → deadly reversal → Luck chooses to stay → coalition plan → harbor crisis → evidence and authority climax → responsibility/repair → witnessed-contract workaround → departure**

No legacy manifestation or regeneration beat is required to preserve this structure.

# 16. Chapter Revision Standard

When auditing an existing chapter:

**KEEP** — structure and prose already satisfy current authority.  
**TARGETED FIX** — local staging, dialogue, continuity, luck chain, or ending issue.  
**SCENE REWRITE** — one or more scenes lack goal/obstacle/change or use legacy power logic.  
**CHAPTER REWRITE** — chapter premise/execution conflicts with current structure or cannot be repaired locally.

Do not rewrite a strong chapter merely to make it different.

# 17. Final Structural QA

A chapter passes only if all are true:

- goal is identifiable;
- obstacle is concrete;
- scene geography is understandable where action requires it;
- at least one meaningful choice/attempt occurs;
- chapter state changes;
- ending pulls forward;
- Fortune Distortion, if present, uses visible causal variables;
- Luck does not consciously use luck;
- no second power/regeneration/time-stop/matter creation appears;
- supporting cast retain competence;
- misunderstanding is not repetitive or consequence-free;
- injuries/money/evidence/politics continue correctly;
- dialogue has purpose;
- POV remains Luck-limited unless an explicitly authorized POV change exists;
- no protected mystery leaks through narration.

# Final Lock

**Structure serves causality, character, and consequence. Spectacle never replaces them.**
