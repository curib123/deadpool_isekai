# Red Jackal Overpowered Character / Power Retcon

**Project:** This Isekai Has a Bug. Unfortunately, It's Me.  
**Status:** AUTHORIAL RETCON — ACTIVE / FOUNDATION UPDATED  
**Applies To:** All volumes, chapters, scene plans, QA, power rules, and future production  
**Priority:** Overrides older Stage I / Stage II / mastery-ceiling language wherever conflict exists.

# 1. Character Direction

Red Jackal is an original overpowered role-player protagonist.

The desired high-level appeal is:
- hidden absurd strength;
- theatrical self-created personas;
- deliberate underestimation;
- playing with enemies rather than desperately trying to surpass them;
- extreme confidence backed by actual power;
- comedy from the gap between his private game and other people's serious interpretations;
- sudden terrifying simplicity when he stops playing.

He must not be written as a direct copy of any existing franchise character.

# 2. Power Truth

Red Jackal is already overwhelmingly powerful from the beginning.

There is no conventional power progression.

There is no:
- Stage I → Stage II → Stage III → Stage IV growth;
- level grind;
- class awakening;
- stat ceiling;
- final form;
- mandatory training arc to unlock raw strength;
- stronger enemy required merely to keep combat fair.

**Play Logic has no known intrinsic ceiling.**

The story may reveal increasingly outrageous things he can do, but those are reveals of existing capability, not upgrades.

# 3. What Can Still Improve

Red can improve:
- knowledge;
- judgment;
- local language;
- tactics;
- social understanding;
- understanding of institutions;
- understanding of magic/Fate/world history;
- relationships;
- creativity;
- discipline;
- ability to predict consequences.

None of these are raw-power unlocks.

# 4. Enemy-Play Rule

Against enemies Red often chooses not to end the fight immediately.

He may:
- match their apparent level;
- let them complete a technique;
- pretend a hit mattered;
- fight with a silly object;
- invent a self-imposed restriction;
- allow a false weakness to be discovered;
- retreat theatrically;
- stage a reveal;
- make the opponent believe they forced him to become serious.

This is entertainment and characterization.

It is not evidence that Red needed time to become stronger.

# 5. Serious Switch

When a line is crossed, Red may stop playing.

The transition is:

**playful restraint → silence → decisive overwhelming action**

No power-up animation is required.

No new form unlocks.

The fear comes from realizing:

**he could have ended it earlier.**

# 6. Story Tension Model

Do not ask:

**Can Red become strong enough?**

Ask:
- Does Red know the real problem?
- Is he playing with the wrong person?
- Will he stop playing before someone else is hurt?
- Can he protect several places at once?
- Does he understand the political or social consequence?
- Is the obvious enemy actually the important enemy?
- What happens because he deliberately hid his power?
- What does he choose when nobody can force him?

Raw combat is not the main source of uncertainty.

# 7. Supporting-Character Rule

Supporting characters remain necessary because competence is not identical to force.

A tracker may know where danger is going.

An engineer may know what structure matters.

An auditor may know which record proves the crime.

A commander may know where civilians must move.

Red could potentially destroy the physical obstacle.

He may still fail the larger objective if he does not possess the information.

Therefore:

**supporting cast matter through knowledge, judgment, access, relationships, logistics and consequences—not because the protagonist needs their DPS.**

# 8. Consequence Rule

Overpowered does not mean consequence-free.

Red's mistakes can still cause:
- deaths he did not prevent;
- political backlash;
- destroyed trust;
- evidence loss;
- people acting before he understands them;
- reputational damage;
- enemies escaping with information;
- cities suffering while he focuses somewhere else.

He may have the power to destroy almost anything and still make the wrong decision.

# 9. Regeneration Retcon

Ordinary lethal physical injury is not a meaningful long-term combat ceiling.

Existing scenes that show Red hurt may be retained if recontextualized as:
- genuine pain without lasting power loss;
- voluntary delayed restoration;
- ordinary treatment accepted for social reasons;
- Red allowing the injury to remain visible;
- narration from before he understood how little the injury truly constrained him.

Any manuscript that explicitly says exhaustion makes him unable to access higher raw power requires revision.

# 10. Reputation Retcon

Reputation progression is no longer:

**weak → stronger → terrifying**

It is:

**unknown → strange → underestimated → hidden monster → nobody knows the limit**

Rumors remain contradictory.

Some people may never believe the strongest stories.

# 11. Arc 1 Impact

Greywake's plot remains usable.

The central conflict becomes stronger if Red's restraint is intentional.

Arc 1 is not about Red becoming strong enough to save Greywake.

It becomes about:
- Red entertaining himself inside a local problem;
- gradually caring about people despite denying heroic motives;
- his games and bad judgment causing real consequences;
- deciding how much power to reveal;
- learning that overwhelming strength cannot retroactively fix every consequence;
- eventually dropping part of the act during the climax.

CH-023 may become a much larger spectacle because the old Stage I ceiling no longer applies.

The combined-cast requirement is reinterpreted:
others supply information, evacuation, evidence, logistics and local decisions while Red supplies overwhelming force when he chooses.

# 12. Arc 2 Impact

Merrowgate remains structurally compatible.

Its conflict is especially suitable because Cassian's danger is political/commercial rather than raw combat.

Red can be stronger than everyone in the port and still:
- take the wrong off-book job;
- fail to understand who profits;
- let Cassian manipulate his reputation;
- be unable to punch a missing evidence chain into existence;
- win a fight while losing the political narrative.

All previous Stage I use-count ceilings are superseded.

Future chapter planning should use **display/reveal choices**, not maximum-power allowances.

# 13. Supersession Rule

Where an older file says any of the following, treat it as legacy until revised:

- Stage I ceiling;
- Stage II unlock;
- manifestation maximum because of raw capacity;
- exhaustion blocks higher raw power;
- reality resistance is a routine hard ceiling;
- Red must become stronger to win;
- Red cannot solve a physical threat because his power is insufficient.

Older chapter events may remain canon if they can be reinterpreted as voluntary restraint, limited information, or role-play.

If not, that chapter requires Gate 9 retcon revision.

# 14. Production Priority

Before continuing new Arc 2 chapter production:

1. synchronize Volume 1/2 architecture with this retcon;
2. audit all canon manuscripts for explicit power-ceiling contradictions;
3. revise the affected chapters;
4. rerun Gate 9 where prose meaning changes;
5. rerun Volume 1 Gate 10;
6. regenerate/rerun Gate 11 for changed publication copies;
7. then resume CH-028.

# 15. Core One-Sentence Rule

**Red Jackal is not climbing toward overwhelming power; he is already there, and the story is about what he chooses to do while everyone else slowly realizes it.**
