# 1. New Main Character

**Name:** Luck Everhart

Luck Everhart replaces Red Jackal as the protagonist identity.

Luck is not a reality-warper, immortal, regenerator, time-stopper, chosen hero, or secret max-level fighter.

He has exactly **one supernatural ability**:

# 2. Fortune Distortion

Working in-world term: **Jackal Luck is retired.**
Canonical ability name: **Fortune Distortion**.

Fortune Distortion is passive probability distortion around Luck.

Luck cannot activate it on command.

He cannot choose the exact result.

He cannot create impossible outcomes.

Instead, chains of individually possible events become absurdly favorable around him.

Examples:
- an enemy's weapon breaks at the worst moment;
- a loose stone causes a stronger opponent to slip;
- a monster attacks the wrong target;
- two enemies collide;
- a falling beam blocks a killing blow;
- the right person arrives at the exact wrong/right second;
- Luck guesses and accidentally chooses the correct route;
- a document falls from a stack and exposes the one record everyone needed;
- a terrifying opponent mistakes Luck's silence for confidence and retreats;
- a rumor causes enemies to overestimate him and sabotage themselves.

# 3. Core Character Engine

Luck's comedy/aura engine is:

**Luck is nervous or improvising → he says/does something simple → coincidence makes it look brilliant → witnesses interpret hidden genius or terrifying confidence → Luck realizes the misunderstanding → he keeps a straight face and lets them believe it → the reputation creates a larger future problem.**

Important:
- Luck is not cowardly all the time.
- He can be clever, observant, shameless and opportunistic.
- He is physically ordinary-to-capable, not superhuman.
- He survives because probability refuses to let lethal sequences resolve cleanly.
- He can still be injured, exhausted, poisoned, arrested, embarrassed, trapped, or defeated in an objective.
- His luck does not automatically save other people.
- His reputation can become more dangerous than his real combat ability.

# 4. Personality

Public face:
- calm;
- dry;
- confident-looking;
- difficult to read;
- sometimes deliberately theatrical once he notices people are afraid of him.

Private reality:
- frequently improvising;
- often unsure what is happening;
- notices danger clearly;
- internally more expressive than he looks;
- willing to bluff when the misunderstanding is useful;
- enjoys taking credit after luck has already saved him;
- curious enough to enter trouble;
- self-centered enough to avoid becoming a conventional hero.

Luck is original. Broad inspiration may include accidental-legend and misunderstood-aura comedy, but no copied scenes, dialogue, powers, costume, or plot beats from existing works.

# 6. New Combat Law

Luck may face opponents much stronger and more skilled than he is.

Combat works through:
1. real opponent pressure;
2. Luck trying to survive or bluff;
3. small probability deviations;
4. an improbable chain forms;
5. Luck or an ally notices the opening;
6. the opening is converted into escape, victory, rescue, evidence, delay, or another objective;
7. witnesses misunderstand how much Luck planned.

Luck can win without throwing the strongest attack.

He may win because:
- the enemy defeats itself;
- terrain collapses;
- reinforcements arrive;
- an attack ricochets;
- a trap triggers early;
- a stronger ally misreads Luck's gesture as a signal;
- the enemy retreats from a threat that does not actually exist.

# 7. Aura / Misinterpretation Law

Luck gradually becomes famous as a hidden monster or master strategist.

The truth is simpler:
- he is lucky;
- he is good at keeping a straight face;
- he learns to exploit misunderstandings;
- people connect unrelated coincidences into a legend.

Reputation ladder:

1. Unknown outsider
2. Unclassifiable stranger
3. Suspicious survivor
4. “He planned that.”
5. “He knew that would happen.”
6. “Do not attack him first.”
7. “Nobody knows what happens if he gets serious.”
8. Late-series myth: people assume entire disasters were part of Luck's plan

Luck himself never receives a second supernatural power.

# 8. Greywake Volume 1 — New Story Spine

## Prologue — The Wrong Arrival
Luck dies in the mysterious Earth explosion and arrives in Veyr through Soul Drift. No inherited pop-culture persona and no power reveal.

## CH001 — Wrong Forest, Wrong World
Luck is hunted by a predator. He survives because a sequence of mud, broken stone, rotten timber, predator momentum and a falling branch creates an escape. He assumes he got absurdly lucky.

## CH002 — Undefined
Greywake's System tools cannot classify him. A translation token happens to become available at the perfect moment. His lack of records creates administrative suspicion.

## CH003 — The Things They Can Measure
Maelis tests him. Ordinary physical instruments work; System identity tools fail. A sequence of instrument glitches and coincidences begins the statistical mystery.

## CH004 — Off the Books
Luck gets hired because his unregistered status makes him useful for unofficial road work. He thinks he found easy money. Others think he deliberately chose the only job that bypasses System identity.

## CH005 — A Better Hammer
No conjured hammer. A real hammer breaks. Luck grabs an unsuitable spare tool; a chain of leverage, timing and material failure lets the repair succeed. Workers assume he saw the structural solution instantly.

## CH006 — The Road That Works
A caravan problem appears. Luck repeatedly stands in the “wrong” position, but those positions become correct seconds later as animals, wheels, terrain and traffic shift.

## CH007 — You Should Be Dead
The first major proof of abnormal luck. Luck should die in a predator attack, but several independent events prevent lethal contact. He is injured normally. Sela treats him normally. Tavian begins suspecting the pattern is statistically impossible.

## CH008 — Outside the Lanterns
Luck helps outer homesteads. No temporary reality-made footing. A failing cart, rope, root shelf and bridge geometry accidentally align after Luck makes a casual suggestion.

## CH009 — Wrong Tracks
Luck guesses the wrong trail. The wrong trail intersects the real movement pattern and exposes the hidden route manipulation. Tavian knows Luck did not have enough information to deduce it.

## CH010 — The Ledger Doesn't Balance
A dropped page, mistimed clerk arrival and duplicated record expose the casualty-accounting gap. Maelis starts tracking coincidence around Luck as data.

## CH011 — Broken North
No barrier manifestation. Luck survives a dangerous road collapse/monster pressure through terrain failure that happens to protect him while exposing evidence.

## CH012 — Someone Put This Here
Luck randomly notices the one ordinary detail that proves the lure was placed deliberately.

## CH013 — Where They Die
His presence accidentally causes a hidden pattern to become visible. The investigation proves outer groups are being sacrificed.

## CH014 — Necessary Losses
Luck's reputation begins altering negotiations. Hesk interprets his calm as hidden knowledge and reveals more than intended.

## CH015 — One Stake Too Many
A lure assembly fails at exactly the wrong moment for Hesk's plan. Luck is blamed/credited for sabotage he did not perform.

## CH016 — Backflow
Luck's “bad” route decision accidentally predicts the herd backflow. The settlement starts treating his guesses like warnings.

## CH017 — The Price of Being Right
Luck's earlier interference has consequences. Luck saved him, not everyone. This establishes the hard limit: probability advantage is not moral or strategic perfection.

## CH018 — No Acceptable Loss
Luck refuses Hesk's sacrifice logic for personal reasons, not heroic purity. A coincidence gives the group a third option but does not solve it for them.

## CH019 — Build the Wrong Road
No manifestation. Luck supports an apparently foolish diversion route. Rain/runoff/animal movement make it the correct defense.

## CH020 — The Last Lure
The plan to redirect the herd depends on timing. Luck's luck keeps the operation from failing cleanly, but every save creates another pressure point.

## CH021 — Before the Herd
Rumors now treat Luck as the mastermind. He privately knows he is improvising.

## CH022 — First Wave
Team battle. Luck's luck causes misses, collisions, jams and timing breaks; militia and specialists still do the real work.

## CH023 — Greywake Holds
Major battle. Luck becomes the center of enemy/monster misinterpretation while Brynn, Tavian, Kellan and others secure the actual victory conditions.

## CH024 — No One Gets Chosen
Hesk falls politically because evidence, witness timing and his own choices converge. Luck looks like he arranged the entire exposure.

## CH025 — What Greywake Counts
Greywake officially records the cost. Maelis presents the first serious statistical case that “coincidence” around Luck is not normal.

## CH026 — Before They Decide What I Am
Factions debate whether Luck is a hidden strategist, curse, blessing, weapon or threat. Luck realizes his reputation is becoming useful and dangerous.

## CH027 — Salt in the Air
Luck leaves Greywake for Merrowgate. By the time he departs, rumors describe a man who never needs to attack because disasters rearrange themselves around him.

# 9. Volume 1 Mystery

The reader understands what Luck never correctly identifies, including in the ending:

**His supernatural power is luck.**

The world should not name it too early.

Maelis approaches it statistically.

Tavian approaches it through repeated field impossibility.

Luck approaches it as:
“Things keep working out. Weird.”

## Permanent Character Lock — 2026-10-02

The author-directed permanent-clueless revision supersedes eventual-awareness language. Luck enjoys performing an enigmatic role from the prologue onward. He knows his bluff is a bluff, but he does not know Fortune Distortion is his power. Experts and opponents can produce elaborate theories; he attributes the results to accidents, ordinary improvisation, and a successful performance. This persists through the final scene. Ordinary skills and relationships develop without a power discovery or a personality replacement.
