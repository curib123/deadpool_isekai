# Soul-Drift / Story-Intruder Retcon

**Status:** LOCKED AUTHORIAL RETCON  
**Priority:** Overrides conflicting older power, POV, Red-development, fourth-wall, and origin language.  
**Preservation Rule:** Existing geography, world map, destination order, Greywake chapter order, Merrowgate chapter order, and major pacing beats remain unchanged unless explicitly listed here.

# 1. What Does Not Change

Preserve:
- Veyr geography and map;
- existing nations/cultures;
- Greywake as Volume 1 / Greywake Arc;
- Merrowgate as Volume 2 / Merrowgate Arc;
- Hesk as Greywake local antagonist;
- Cassian Venn as Merrowgate local antagonist;
- Greywake's investigation → lure crisis → climax → aftermath → departure pacing;
- Merrowgate's arrival → commercial pressure → off-manifest mistake → reversal → coalition/climax → aftermath pacing;
- existing major chapter order and destination sequencing;
- local supporting characters' independent lives and competencies.

# 2. Red Jackal Retcon

Red is:
- permanently overpowered from the opening;
- System UNDEFINED / NO RECORD / FAILED / UNAVAILABLE;
- without levels, classes, XP, stages, awakenings, or power progression;
- capable of automatic Play Logic body restoration;
- capable of unbounded imagination-based Play Logic;
- possesses passive Jackal Luck that bends coincidence/probability;
- an active fourth-wall breaker;
- the focal character of distant third-person limited narration;
- self-centered;
- theatrical;
- aura-farming;
- enemy-playing;
- chaotic;
- an anti-villain;
- fundamentally stable in personality from beginning to end.

There is no Red moral-development arc.

# 3. Origin Retcon

Earth:
- Evan Calder was an obsessive Deadpool fan and heavy consumer of meta/action media;
- he dies in a mysterious explosion;
- a natural far Soul Drift carries him to Veyr;
- Soul Drift erodes most autobiographical memory and ordinary personality organization;
- his deeply rehearsed meta-comedic cognitive groove survives unusually strongly;
- no god, villain, System, Exterior Needle, or Savael chooses him.

# 4. Fourth-Wall Retcon

Every genuine direct audience address invokes **Fourth-Wall Pause**:
- Veyran time stops;
- local motion and sound freeze;
- Red addresses the audience;
- native characters perceive no elapsed time;
- scene resumes from the same instant.

This is available from the start.

It is never unlocked later.

# 5. Combat Retcon

Every fight must assume:
**Red can already end the raw-power contest.**

Therefore fight tension comes from:
- Red choosing to play;
- voluntary restrictions;
- civilians;
- simultaneous objectives;
- hidden information;
- evidence;
- politics;
- timing;
- Red's curiosity;
- Red allowing the opponent too much stage time;
- native storylines continuing elsewhere.

Red may imitate weakness or use tiny effects.

That is performance, not limitation.

# 6. Red Character Stability

Red does not become:
- kinder as a series objective;
- sane;
- humble;
- law-abiding;
- a proper hero;
- responsible in the conventional coming-of-age sense.

He can have:
- attachments;
- temporary seriousness;
- specific regrets;
- tactical lessons;
- recovered memories;
- callbacks;
- running relationships.

Those enrich the stable character instead of replacing him.

# 7. Native Story Retcon

Veyr has a legitimate Hero-vs-Main-Villain storyline independent of Red.

Red is not the chosen protagonist of that structure.

He is the anomaly who keeps colliding with it.

The world must continue having:
- a System-recognized Hero;
- a Hero Party;
- a Main Villain;
- minor villains;
- local antagonists unrelated to the Main Villain;
- off-screen events that happen without Red;
- characters who never meet Red.

# 8. Story Intrusion Rule

When Red enters somebody else's important scene:
- let him recognize or joke about the trope;
- let the existing characters retain their motivations;
- allow Red to steal the visual spotlight;
- do not erase the original objective unless his action logically destroys it;
- reroute later beats around the consequences;
- preserve causal sequence.

Red can break the expected scene.

He should not break story continuity.

# 8A. False-Main-Character Rule

Red is the reader-facing focal character of this series, but he is not the native chosen Hero of Veyr.

When he enters a native Hero/Villain beat, he is allowed to dominate the *presentation* so strongly that witnesses or readers temporarily feel as if the scene belongs to him.

That effect is intentional.

However:
- the registered Hero remains legitimate;
- the Hero Party retains independent objectives;
- the Main Villain retains a campaign independent of Red;
- local villains do not all become Red's personal villains;
- off-screen events continue;
- Red does not inherit every prophecy, quest, rivalry, romance, or political obligation simply because he appeared.

Red can look like the main character of somebody else's scene without becoming the person that scene was originally about.

# 8B. Controlled Chaos / Unexpected Turn Rule

At least some Red-heavy chapters should contain an **earned unexpected turn** created by Red's choices.

The surprise must come from character logic, not arbitrary author intervention.

Valid causes:
- boredom;
- curiosity;
- ego;
- self-interest;
- protecting chosen people;
- wanting better theater;
- wanting to see the enemy's full act;
- refusing destiny or ownership;
- exploiting a trope he recognizes;
- misunderstanding the native situation.

Every unexpected turn must leave a causal footprint that future beats acknowledge.

# 9. Greywake Retcon Overlay

Greywake's external events stay in the same order.

Change Red's internal/behavioral framing:

- he does not “grow into responsibility”;
- early small Play Logic is chosen restraint / experimentation / comedy;
- severe injuries never imply a hidden stamina progression ceiling;
- he treats predators and hostile actors as toys until other people's danger makes the game inconvenient;
- his lure interference comes from curiosity, ego, dislike of imposed sacrifice, and wanting to see what happens—not a heroic awakening;
- after the backflow, he may acknowledge that his choice changed the board without becoming morally reformed;
- he stays because leaving would let Hesk own the story, because chosen people/problems are now “his,” and because the situation became interesting;
- climax cooperation does not mean Red became obedient; he chooses to follow Tavian/Brynn's information because they know the board better;
- his serious switch reveals that raw power was never the issue;
- CH-026 departure remains voluntary and preserves the same ending function.

# 10. Merrowgate Retcon Overlay

Merrowgate's chapter order and major events remain unchanged.

Change Red's framing:

- he enters looking for money, stimulation, rumors, transport, and a stage;
- reputation arriving before him becomes an aura-farming toy;
- Cassian interests Red because Cassian tries to buy/control the story told about Red;
- the off-manifest job remains Red's deliberate choice;
- CH-042 consequences remain real;
- Red does not stay because he has become morally better;
- he stays because Cassian used Red's name, blamed his mess on him, endangered people Red has chosen to keep around, and tried to control the narrative;
- Red plays with Cassian's political/commercial machinery rather than simply killing him;
- evidence matters because destroying Cassian physically would be easy and narratively boring;
- serious Red appears when the game threatens to take irreversible choices away from him or his chosen people;
- the witnessed-contract ending still fits because Red remains legally undefined.

# 11. Pacing Lock

Do not alter chapter count solely to support this retcon.

Do not move Greywake's climax later.

Do not move Merrowgate's major reversal or climax.

Rewrite **scene execution and character framing**, not the destination skeleton.
