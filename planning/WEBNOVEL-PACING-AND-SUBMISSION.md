# Webnovel Pacing and Submission Standard

**Revision:** 2026-10-07  
**Scope:** editorial direction for the entire Luck Everhart serial; prose implementation in this revision is CH001–CH003 and the synopsis only.  
**Authority:** subordinate to the Series Constitution, Writing Rules, Character Bible, Continuity Bible, and Luck Everhart Structural Story Bible. This document refines pacing; it does not replace canon.  
**Release status:** author-review revision, not a Webnovel acceptance, contract offer, or platform submission.

## 1. Product promise

An original, physically vulnerable outsider performs the part of a mysterious mastermind. Passive Fortune Distortion makes ordinary actions succeed improbably. Competent witnesses misunderstand his intention; their decisions create money, opportunities, suspicion, danger, and obligations. Luck enjoys the act and never identifies his supernatural power.

Keep the current title and original Luck Everhart identity. The repository's historical name is not the public book title. Do not market this as Deadpool fiction, import Marvel material, or claim that a renamed imitation is an original novel. No conscious probability control, regeneration, levels-to-grind progression, harem, or extra power is added to chase a market category.

## 2. Fast pacing means earlier change, not constant fragments

For each chapter, define a present objective, an obstacle, a consequential attempt, a payoff, and a new pressure. Open on that objective or pressure instead of summarizing the previous installment. Start paying off the hook within the chapter; do not repeatedly postpone the same answer behind cliffhangers.

Working editorial targets, NOT Webnovel requirements:

- Make the present problem clear in roughly the first 100–150 words. Put the signature attraction on the page early in the opening sequence.
- Usually build around one dominant problem and one or two closely connected scenes. Aim around 1,000–1,800 words when that gives a complete installment. A longer chapter is justified by earned payoff, not quota; a shorter one is not padded.
- Cut repeated questions, repeated witness praise, travel without change, explanations of accidents the reader just saw, and recaps of settled System results. Retain evidence needed for a different decision.
- Use connected narration, usually 2–5 sentences, with shorter paragraphs for danger, humor, or a turn. Keep dialogue separated by speaker. Short sentences and white space alone do not make a story fast.
- Require one active engagement: danger, contested negotiation, pressured investigation, a difficult test, escape, argument with consequences, or another concrete obstacle. Do not insert a meaningless fight merely to satisfy an action quota.

Do not cut geography, preparation, reaction time, injury treatment, character motivation, or emotional consequences just to reduce length. Introduce a hazard before it becomes a lucky solution. Preserve a short recovery beat when needed; end it on a decision, cost, or new problem.

## 4. Apply across the continuing story

Use the existing Greywake and Merrowgate chapter roadmaps, not a second competing outline. For every future installment, and every later revision, write an editor-only five-line card: objective; opposition; Luck's actual intention; visible outcome and witness interpretation; consequence and next decision. Revise a chapter only where the card exposes a failure.

Greywake's work chapters must earn pay, lose equipment, change trust, create a practical route question, or impose risk. Investigation must produce new evidence or an altered hypothesis, not repeat that the roads are suspicious. Political scenes must change an order, alliance, deadline, or available action. Crisis chapters must advance an evacuation, construction, battle objective, or cost. Aftermath must settle something and establish what still follows Luck.

Merrowgate must inherit the cost, identity liability, and reputation consequences without replaying the entire arrival tutorial. Preserve the current roadmap and the rule that CH028 onward has not yet been written. A faster serial is not permission to fabricate completed chapters or rush a protected revelation.

Alternate successes with complications. Supporting characters can reject Luck's pose, explain only what they observed, or correctly solve the practical problem while misreading him. Do not manufacture universal admiration. A lucky escape does not guarantee safety for bystanders or erase an obligation.

## 5. Public packaging

Use `published/SYNOPSIS.md` as the reader blurb. Sell the concrete mismatch—apparent mastermind, unaware lucky survivor—rather than promising a conventional leveling System. Use only categories/tags actually available in Inkstone and genuinely supported by the book, such as Fantasy, Action, Adventure, Comedy, Mystery, and Transmigration/Isekai where offered. Do not stuff unrelated tags.

Public reading starts at CH001. No separate prologue, lore dump, production status, QA badge, power encyclopedia, or agent instructions belong in a paid story chapter. Manuscript and publication prose must match. For this pass the paired opening files are byte-identical; revision metadata lives in QA/continuity documents.

## 6. Editor-facing application notes

These are preparation notes, not an application sent on the author's behalf.

**Premise:** A mortal outsider whose System identity cannot be registered survives through luck he does not understand. His performed confidence makes competent people credit him with plans he never made, and their belief draws him into dangerous work and institutional conflicts.

**Specific differentiation:** Reputation outruns actual competence. No conscious power mastery or normal leveling resolves the mismatch. Physical luck may save him while legal restrictions, collateral harm, promises, and other people's expectations remain.

**First-arc outline, with spoilers:** Greywake initially gives Luck restricted entry and irregular road employment. Work exposes a mismatch between safer profitable routes and danger borne elsewhere. Investigation reveals deliberate lure infrastructure. Hesk defends the policy as necessary survival; premature intervention creates backflow and real harm. A coalition develops a third option through field expertise, construction, evidence, and coordinated action. The local conflict resolves, while identity restrictions, responsibility, and reputation carry into his voluntary departure for Merrowgate.

**Long-term structure:** Each destination has its own independent problem, antagonist incentives, reversal, local payoff, and surviving costs. Merrowgate develops reputation, identity liability, and harbor interests rather than resetting the character. Do not submit unspecified later destinations as finished planning.

**Publication history:** This GitHub repository is public and contains earlier versions. Disclose that exposure wherever the application asks about prior publication or distribution. Verify whether the story is posted anywhere else; do not invent an exclusivity declaration.

**Update plan:** Before promising a schedule, count author-reviewed unpublished chapters, choose a cadence the author can actually maintain, and keep a buffer. A proposed starting cadence is five chapters per week with at least ten reviewed chapters held back. This is an editorial suggestion, not a platform requirement, scheduled automation, or claim that the buffer already exists.

## 7. Acceptance and authorship safeguards

Publishing, moderation approval, contract selection, and paid-program eligibility are different decisions. No word count, cliffhanger formula, internal QA score, or AI rewrite guarantees any of them. There is no evidence-supported approval percentage for this manuscript.

Check the live Inkstone application and current editor instructions before submission. Historical contest thresholds must not be presented as universal current contract requirements. The official Contract Application Guide cautions against rushing an application merely because a button appears and stresses specific differentiation and truthful publication history.

This revision used AI assistance, including rewritten prose. The official editorial article on cliffhangers advises against using AI to write whole chapters. That editorial advice is not proof of a universal ban, nor proof that AI-assisted prose is eligible. The author must personally review and revise the text, verify the applicable AI/disclosure requirements with Inkstone or an editor, and answer authorship questions truthfully. Human review does not erase AI involvement or guarantee eligibility. Do not use detector evasion or false human-only claims.

Any eventual publishing contract requires the author's separate review of rights, exclusivity, obligations, payment, and termination terms. Nothing in this repository accepts a contract or authorizes publication.
