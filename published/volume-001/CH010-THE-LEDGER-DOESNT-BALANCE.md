# Chapter 10 — The Ledger Doesn't Balance

Rook found the report on the attack because Maelis wanted a route description. He stayed because the report had misplaced him.

Four certified workers. One lower-leg injury. Predator contact during service travel. Assistance from irregular labor.

Maelis read each entry aloud beneath the charged Wayfarer Tongue Token. Rook looked at the paper, then at the bandage beneath his shirt.

"Where am I?"

"The irregular assistance is probably you."

"Probably?"

"There is no valid identity record to attach."

The room had three stacks of paperwork and no useful object to hit. Rook pulled his chair closer. His side hurt when he leaned across the desk. That seemed like something a report about his side ought to acknowledge.

"Does it record my injury?"

"No registered injury claim."

"Sela put stitches in me."

"I have her treatment note. The Guild incident and the treatment record do not reconcile through a recognized worker identity."

Rook sat back. The route existed. The pack existed. The man bitten below the knee existed. He had been reduced to assistance, as if a shovel had wandered over and saved everyone some effort.

Maelis set another sheet beside the report. Private labor expense, food issued, work completed. His claimed name appeared in an expense note. She pointed to the place where the identity field should have connected it to the incident.

"The assignment balances," she said. "The payment is accounted for. Your standard protections are not."

"So if I vanish, the silver still balances."

"An expense can close without resolving a missing person."

Rook put a hand over his entrant token. It was suddenly less funny that carpentry had solved the gate's problem while leaving his own intact.

"Hesk can send me out, pay me, lose me, and still have clean books."

"Balanced books," Maelis said. "That is a narrower claim."

She had not brought him here to discuss the attack. Tavian had reported layered tracks trending northeast, away from the Main Caravan Road and toward old northern routes. Maelis needed to know which observations Rook could independently confirm.

He described the marker by its shape and faded paint. He gave her the drainage bend, the track ages Tavian had distinguished, and the direction of the old trail. He could not read the labels, but he could identify where he had stood.

When she asked about the live crossing, he looked at the ceiling.

"I interfered."

"After Tavian told you to wait?"

"You two should start a club."

The pen remained above the page.

"Yes," Rook said. "I moved into the sightline. The animals ran."

Maelis put that observation in a separate box. His experiment had contaminated the fresh evidence. The older tracks remained usable. Suspicion was allowed to arrive before certainty, provided it did not sign certainty's name.

Rook told her about Kellan's warning marker: five predator sightings beneath an official low-warning symbol, all newer than the last patrol. She noted that an old sign alone did not prove deliberate suppression. He already knew Tavian would agree with her.

Then she opened the second stack.

Supply issue. Route assignment. Active certification. No completion. The same worker existed in one record set and vanished from another.

"Missing?" Rook asked.

"Here. Not in the certified casualty summary."

"Dead?"

"Unknown."

She followed the entries across three sheets. Most carried outer-route symbols he recognized. The gap between them looked much like the one his own injury had fallen through.

"Different cause," Maelis said before he asked. "But your case makes the separation visible. We need the Guild's internal filing categories."

Rook looked at the remaining stacks.

"There are more?"

She took the written request and stood. He went with her because the silver in his pocket had become less reassuring than it had been an hour ago.

The Road Guild remained busy. Workers carried rope and axle parts through the yard, and two caravans waited under the main-road marker. Nothing about the place looked like it had time to hide people. That was probably an advantage for anyone doing it.

Jessa read Maelis's request at the records counter. Then she read it again.

"You have the casualty summaries."

"Certified casualties," Maelis said. "These route incidents do not reconcile with them."

Jessa looked toward Hesk's office before touching a ledger. Rook noticed the glance. Maelis left the request on the counter and waited.

At last Jessa took them to the rear shelving. She brought down a dark cloth-bound book and a thinner packet tied with cord.

"Unresolved route loss," she said, setting down the book. "Irregular labor and incomplete identity. Those are separate."

"From casualties?"

Jessa nodded. Casualty certification needed a confirmed identity or recognized worker record. A missing person without a recovered body might stay unresolved. A local hire without full certification could remain an irregular incident. A report with no liability claim might never enter the certified total at all.

Maelis turned a page. Rook watched Jessa's hand instead of the writing. Her thumb kept rubbing the cloth edge.

"Even when the team believes the person died?" Maelis asked.

"Belief is not certification."

The answer sounded practiced. Rook looked toward the yard, where a worker was loading a new team onto a wagon. He wondered how many people had heard those words after somebody failed to come home.

Jessa showed corrections moving initial casualties to unresolved loss when identity could not be confirmed. Maelis asked who approved the final category.

The cloth stopped moving under Jessa's thumb.

"I process the forms."

"Jessa."

Hesk stood in the doorway. No guards, no raised voice. He read the request Maelis offered and said original unresolved-loss books had to remain in Guild custody. Certified copies could be arranged. Inspection needed a Guild clerk present.

Maelis indicated Jessa.

"She has caravan certifications due before departure," Hesk said. "Another clerk can supervise."

It sounded like work. Jessa went pale anyway.

"I am asking about categories she personally processes," Maelis said. "During assigned audit time."

Hesk looked at Jessa. She kept her hand on the book.

"I need this job," she said quietly. "My younger siblings depend on it."

"I know."

He reached for the ledger. Rook put his hand on it first.

Hesk stopped. So did everyone else.

"She hasn't said she's finished," Rook told him.

"That is not your decision."

"Exactly."

Maelis shifted half a step closer to the counter. She did not endorse the interruption, but she did not remove his hand either. Hesk looked from Rook to the wooden token beneath his coat.

"The Guild gave you work when nobody could process you. Remember the arrangement."

Rook remembered the advance. The bridge. The absence of ordinary protection. He had accepted a bad bargain because hunger offered worse ones. That was no reason to let Hesk improve it in retrospect.

"I took work," he said. "You didn't buy me."

Jessa looked down at the ledger. Rook took his hand off it and left the book where she had placed it.

"If you want to go, go," he told her.

Hesk waited. Rook disliked how easily a quiet room could make one person's answer feel like everyone else's property.

"Factual categories only," Hesk said at last. "No speculation. Originals remain here."

Maelis agreed to those conditions. As Hesk left, he reminded her that missing was not dead, unregistered was not certified, and merging unlike categories could manufacture the false picture she was trying to prevent.

"That is why I am keeping them separate," she said.

He gave her a small nod and went back to work. Beyond the doorway, the wagons were still being loaded.

Jessa did not reopen the ledger immediately. Rook moved away from the counter, giving her room to pick it up or carry it back to the shelf. Maelis waited with the unused request copy in her hand.

"One thing," Jessa said.

She opened to a narrow column. Unresolved cases in the Main Caravan Road summary were counted as route incidents, outside the headline certified casualty total. More unresolved notes stayed attached to the internal outer-route reports. Comparing the headline figures alone left out the people whose cases were harder to close.

"Written policy?" Maelis asked.

"Practice."

"Who taught you?"

Jessa closed the book.

"I said I would explain the category."

Maelis accepted the boundary. She copied the relevant entries with Jessa present, recorded which books they came from, and left the originals in Guild custody. Nothing had fallen open by accident. Nothing had forced the answer from the clerk. Rook had to wait while people chose what they were willing to risk.

Back in the Ledger room, Maelis arranged the certified copies by reporting period. Main Caravan Road, outer routes, unresolved and irregular losses. Rook recognized the route symbols even though the words remained beyond him.

She checked assignment volume. Weather. Comparable seasonal periods. One comparison could be coincidence. The same direction across several periods required more attention.

Main-road certified casualties fell. Outer unresolved and irregular losses rose.

Again.

Rook looked at the expense note containing his claimed name. The books could preserve the food he had eaten while failing to preserve what had happened to his body. It took little imagination to extend that problem to someone who could not walk into the office and complain.

"Intentional?" he asked.

"I don't know."

Maelis set the figures beside Tavian's route notes. Two kinds of evidence pointed away from the best-protected road. Neither explained what was moving the danger.

"Random clerical error is no longer enough," she said. "We need the physical cause before we accuse anybody of creating the pattern."

Rook followed the northbound line on Tavian's sketch. An old route, still receiving maintenance in places the public road reports treated as abandoned.

Outside, a caravan left Greywake under a low-warning marker. The driver smiled at the guard as he passed.

The ledger balanced.

The people didn't.
