# Chapter 14 — Necessary Losses

The council chamber was full before Luck arrived.

Representatives occupied every chair around the central table. Two benches had been dragged against the walls for observers. Three stacks of records waited in front of Maelis, arranged with enough precision to look accusatory.

Hesk sat opposite her.

Yesterday, he had stood through every argument.

Today, he looked tired.

Luck trusted that less.

Kellan had refused a chair entirely. He stood near the outer-route map with his arms folded. Tavian Rook remained at the same table, both hands braced beside a spread of field notes. Captain Brynn Alder had positioned herself where she could see the room and the door.

Nobody looked rested.

Maelis began before anyone could turn exhaustion into another argument.

"The evidence from yesterday remains unchanged. The diversion network exists. Multiple lure sites were maintained. The Main Caravan Road received protection while animal pressure increased elsewhere."

A council representative shifted.

"No one is disputing that now."

Kellan looked at him.

"You were yesterday."

"I said *now*."

"Comforting."

Brynn lifted one hand.

"Enough."

Mostly worked.

Maelis continued.

"Those facts remain recorded whether this council approves of the policy or not."

Hesk said nothing.

A councilwoman leaned toward the table.

"What happens if the lures stop today?"

Luck looked at her.

There it was.

Not *what happened?*

What happened next.

"So we're asking whether to keep doing it," he said.

Brynn turned.

"No. She asked what happens if it stops."

Luck leaned back against the wall.

"That distinction sounds expensive."

"It probably is."

Maelis folded her hands over the records.

"Evidence tells us what occurred. It does not automatically tell us how to reverse it safely."

Luck sighed.

"Deeply disappointing."

"I assumed you would feel that way."

Kellan stepped forward.

"Whatever happens next, the outer homesteads are in the room this time."

One representative nodded.

Another studied the table.

Brynn looked at Hesk.

"No lure moves without shared authorization. Militia watches the known sites. Route-risk information comes to us directly."

Hesk's gaze sharpened.

"The Guild retains operational control until another structure exists."

"No."

Brynn answered before he finished.

"You do not resume secret control because the council has not finished deciding what replaces it."

"I am the Roadmaster."

"You are also the person whose hidden policy created this dispute."

Nothing moved in Hesk's face.

That made the silence worse.

Brynn continued.

"Sites remain preserved. Tavian maps migration effects. Maelis controls the records. Kellan's people receive warning access."

"And the Guild?"

Brynn held his gaze.

"You answer questions."

For the first time, something almost like amusement touched Hesk's expression.

Almost.

The councilwoman who had asked about stopping the lures tapped two fingers against the table.

"If the network is suspended, I want the risk stated plainly."

Kellan turned on her.

"People are already dying under it."

"I know."

"Then what more do you need?"

"I need to know whether stopping it kills more."

The room went quiet.

Hesk looked at her.

"That is the first useful question this council has asked since yesterday."

Kellan's shoulders tightened.

"Careful."

"You wanted the truth."

"I got it."

"And now you want a simpler one."

Kellan took one step toward him.

Brynn moved no closer, but her attention sharpened.

"Explain," she said.

Hesk did not begin with the lures.

He looked toward the route map.

"Eleven years ago, the first snow came before the migration cleared."

Several people in the room went still.

Greywake remembered.

"The northern road failed after two days of freezing rain. The river crossing iced hard enough to stop cargo and not hard enough to trust. No caravan reached Greywake for weeks."

His voice remained even.

"Two supply wagons disappeared. One relief caravan turned back. Another never left because officials were still deciding whether reopening our route justified the cost."

Kellan stopped moving.

"The outer farms failed first," Hesk said.

Kellan's jaw tightened.

"Do not use them for this."

"I am telling you what happened."

"I know what happened."

"Then you know families came inside the walls carrying whatever they could."

Hesk placed both hands on the table.

"The storehouses emptied. Medicine ran short. Lamp oil ran short. Salt ran short."

No pause for effect.

"People froze."

Another breath.

"People starved."

Luck stopped leaning against the wall.

The story would have been easier if Hesk had sounded proud of it.

He did not.

"I was on the crews that reopened the road," Hesk said. "We cut through frozen debris. We buried two workers beside an old marker because the ground was too hard to bring them home."

His eyes moved across the council.

"When relief finally reached Greywake, the officials signing the release papers asked whether this settlement still justified maintaining the route."

No one interrupted.

"I learned something that winter."

Luck already knew he would hate the answer.

"A frontier does not survive by saving everyone."

Kellan's face hardened.

"It survives by deciding what cannot be allowed to die."

There it was.

The Main Caravan Road.

The records.

The outer routes.

People converted into logistics.

Luck folded his arms.

"So you decided the road mattered more than the people beside it."

Hesk looked at him.

"I decided the road was how everyone inside Greywake stayed alive."

"Very neat."

"No."

The word came sharper than anything Hesk had said all morning.

"It was not neat."

The room held still.

"There is no version of Greywake where every road receives equal protection."

Kellan stepped closer.

"That does not give you the right to feed mine to the forest."

"I did not say it did."

"You did it anyway."

Hesk met his eyes.

"Yes."

No excuse.

No denial.

That almost made it worse.

Hesk looked at Luck.

"You think I chose between good and evil."

Luck shook his head.

"I think you chose for people who never got asked."

"I chose between routes."

"Routes have people on them."

"Yes."

He knew.

That was the problem.

Brynn moved the discussion to the wall map before the room could become only anger.

Colored tags marked caravans, closures, work crews, expected arrivals, and supply paths. Luck could read none of the labels.

Naturally.

A Guild logistics clerk stood beside the map with a ledger held against his chest.

Maelis pointed to him.

"List Greywake's external dependencies."

The clerk swallowed.

"Grain and preserved food. Salt. Medicine. Metal stock. Tool replacement. Lamp oil. Cloth. Dry goods."

"Primary heavy route?"

"Main Caravan Road."

"Alternates?"

"Minor south road. River access when conditions allow. Outer connectors for local production."

Kellan looked at him.

"Local production."

The clerk nodded.

"Outer farms. Timber. Livestock. Repair stock. Local grain."

"Say that again the next time somebody calls us replaceable."

Hesk did not react.

Brynn pointed to the main road.

"If this closes?"

"Caravans reroute or stop."

"For how long?"

The clerk hesitated.

"I cannot give an exact time."

"Good," Tavian said.

Everyone looked at him.

He did not seem bothered.

"An invented number would not improve the decision."

Luck respected that.

Hesk gestured toward the map.

"This is why the road matters."

Kellan's expression sharpened.

"The outer roads matter too."

"Yes."

"Then stop speaking as though only one thing keeps Greywake alive."

Hesk turned toward him.

"The outer homesteads feed Greywake. Timber. Livestock. Labor. Repair crews. Local grain."

Kellan blinked.

Hesk continued.

"But if the Main Caravan Road fails, all of us become isolated."

Kellan laughed once without humor.

"While losing a few farms at a time remains affordable."

Hesk did not answer.

The silence answered for him.

Maelis opened another packet.

"The charter support request."

Hesk looked at her.

"What about it?"

"You used route-performance records in the application."

"Yes."

"Records now shown to omit or weaken some outer losses."

His jaw tightened.

"Yes."

Several representatives shifted.

Maelis did not.

"You intended to secure expanded Valedorn support using safety data that did not fully represent the cost of that safety."

"I intended to secure permanent funding before Greywake was abandoned the next time officials decided we cost more than we returned."

"That is not a denial."

"No."

Again.

No.

Hesk looked toward the route board.

"A stronger charter means road funding. Defensive support. Reliable patrol allocation. Better maintenance."

"And more Guild authority?" Kellan asked.

"Yes."

"The road has to remain viable long enough for anyone outside Greywake to care about strengthening it."

Maelis closed the packet.

"Manipulated reporting may invalidate the request entirely."

"I know."

For the first time, Hesk looked not controlled, but tired.

That almost made him easier to hate.

Almost.

Brynn turned to Tavian.

"We know why he wants the system maintained. What happens if we change it?"

Tavian moved several track stones aside and replaced them with markers for confirmed and suspected lure sites.

"Partly answerable."

He pointed to the nearest one.

"These devices do not command animals. They change choices."

He traced a route northeast.

"An animal avoids one pressure zone and enters another territory. Prey shifts. Predators respond. Herds move. Scavengers follow."

Another marker.

"Repeat the pressure long enough and routes adapt around it."

Kellan stared at the map.

"The movement stops being natural."

"It becomes shaped by the network."

Hesk spoke.

"Which is why shutting it down blindly is dangerous."

Tavian looked at him.

"That is not permission."

Hesk stopped.

Tavian tapped the map.

"It is a field problem. Several movement corridors now overlap where they should not. We have already seen predator and prey species using the same broad drift."

Luck remembered the road.

Wrong tracks.

Wrong direction.

Not madness.

Adaptation.

Tavian moved the Broken North marker.

"If one influence disappears, where do those animals go?"

A councilman answered.

"Back."

Tavian looked at him.

"Back to what?"

No one spoke.

"The old route now occupied by another predator population? The one blocked by changed territory? The one pushed against a service road?"

He let the questions sit.

"We do not know."

Luck looked at the map.

"So they spill somewhere else."

"Possibly."

"Toward Greywake?"

"Possibly."

"Toward the outer farms?"

"Possibly."

Luck exhaled.

"Terrible word."

"Correct word."

Hesk gestured to the map.

"Then the network must remain active."

"No."

Tavian's answer was immediate.

"You just explained—"

"I explained why removing it blindly is dangerous. That does not prove you were right to create and conceal it."

Professional disagreement remained beautiful.

Tavian pointed toward the unconfirmed sectors.

"We need the full deployment map."

Maelis shook her head.

"We do not have one."

"Then we build it."

Hesk folded his arms.

"And while you do?"

"No changes."

Kellan's head snapped around.

"You want us to leave the lures running?"

"I want nobody changing a system we do not understand while people are standing inside the consequences."

"My people already are."

"I know."

"Then every hour matters."

"I know."

"It does not sound like it."

Tavian did not flinch.

"If I tell you to break one now and the pressure moves into another homestead, did I help you?"

Kellan opened his mouth.

Stopped.

That silence hurt more than the argument.

Brynn stepped to the map.

"Temporary position. No lure altered without shared authorization."

Kellan's jaw tightened.

"Brynn—"

"Listen first."

He did.

"Guild servicing cannot change reservoir, strength, orientation, or placement without witnesses. Militia receives every route-risk report. Maelis gets copies of deployment and service records. Field teams map confirmed and suspected sites. Outer communities receive warnings."

"All of them?"

"All we can reach."

"Evacuated?"

"Where the risk justifies it."

Kellan hated the answer.

That did not make it wrong.

Maelis gathered the top packet.

"This temporary hold does not erase yesterday's findings."

A council member rubbed both hands over his face.

"No one said it did."

"I am saying it now so no one says otherwise later."

Very Maelis.

Hesk looked around the room.

"You are freezing the system in place."

"For now," Brynn said.

"And if migration shifts before mapping is complete?"

"We respond."

"You cannot respond everywhere."

"No."

His eyes moved from one representative to another.

"That is the part none of you want to say."

Luck pushed away from the wall.

"No. We simply do not enjoy saying it as much as you do."

Hesk looked at him.

"You still think there is a version where no one chooses."

"I think there is a difference between choosing under pressure and quietly deciding somebody else counts less."

"The forest does not care."

Luck's voice flattened.

"Neither do I."

Hesk's eyes narrowed.

Luck pointed toward the records.

"I care that you made your answer everybody else's problem."

"That will not move a migration."

"No."

Luck looked at Tavian's map.

"But neither will pretending your choice is the only possible one."

He wanted the lures broken.

Immediately.

The desire sat hot and simple in his chest.

Brynn saw it.

"So do I," she said.

Luck looked at her.

She pointed at the map.

"But if you destroy one and the pressure hits another settlement, what did you designate them as?"

That stopped him.

Not conscience.

Geometry.

Same result.

Maelis watched him.

Luck pointed at her.

"Do not make that evidence face."

"I have one face."

"Debatable."

Kellan exhaled.

"I hate this plan."

Brynn nodded.

"So do I."

"Not reassuring."

"It was not intended to be."

Then someone struck the chamber door hard enough to change the room.

A militia runner entered with mud up one leg.

"Captain."

Brynn turned.

"What happened?"

The runner crossed to the route board and pointed east of Kellan's bridge.

"Two patrol markers changed since morning. Fresh predator movement near the eastern outer homesteads. More than the local watch expected."

Kellan was moving before the sentence ended.

"Which homesteads?"

"Farms above the timber-road junction."

"How close?"

"Tracks on the upper approach. Livestock scattered. No confirmed attack yet."

Brynn looked at Tavian.

"Known lure sector?"

"One of Jessa's service entries was near that corridor."

"Confirmed site?"

"No."

Hesk stood.

"Send Guild handlers."

Brynn turned on him.

"No unauthorized servicing."

"The homesteads are already exposed."

"We warn them and send militia support."

"The lure may require adjustment."

"Then we confirm the site and the risk first."

Kellan struck the table with the side of his fist.

"My people are there now."

"I know."

"We do not have three days."

"I know."

Luck looked at the map.

Wait.

Map.

Confirm.

Meanwhile, somebody else's road grew teeth.

Tavian was already watching him.

"Luck."

"I have not done anything."

"Not yet."

Unfairly accurate.

Brynn issued orders.

"Warn the eastern homesteads. Two militia teams to the upper approach. Nobody touches a suspected lure without Tavian or an approved field team."

The runner nodded and left.

Kellan looked at Luck.

Not asking.

Not yet.

Hesk watched both of them.

That was worse.

Tavian stepped closer and lowered his voice.

"We still do not know the full network."

Luck said nothing.

"We do not know which pressure lines overlap there."

Still nothing.

"If you change one lure now, the animals may not go where you think."

"I heard you."

"I need you to understand me."

Luck met his eyes.

"Changing one lure before we map the network could drive everything backward through another corridor."

Tavian nodded.

"Backflow."

The word sat between them.

Outside the chamber, warning bells began to ring.
