# Chapter 22 — First Wave

The first mass did not charge the militia. It came out of the trees in layers: thin-legged prey, larger browsers, then more movement behind the brush. Luck stood beside a stack of spare guide panels with his coat arranged over the torn sleeve. From here he would look impressively calm. From here he also had the shortest route behind a stone marker if calm stopped being sufficient. "Not yet," Tavian said. His eyes stayed on the animals. Brynn held one hand up.

Nobody advanced. A militia recruit shifted his spear forward. "Back," she said. He obeyed. "Do not challenge the front," Tavian added. "Let them choose." Terrified animals still had choices. That was apparently the entire plan. Behind them, one final civilian cart rattled toward the inner fallback road. Two militia escorts ran beside it. A woman sat in the back holding a wrapped bundle to her chest while the driver fought to keep one damaged wheel straight.

*Too close. Still moving.* The first prey crossed Tavian's painted threshold marker. He raised two fingers. Nothing happened. More animals came. One group angled north. Another hesitated. A third tried the same line. "Wait."

Luck kept his hand off the spare guide rope. Pulling something at this point would look decisive, which was a poor reason to do it. Tavian watched until the first browsers reached the cleared approach toward Broken North. One lowered its head and slowed. A second took the same opening. Then a third. Luck quietly moved his boot away from a loose peg. Nobody needed a dramatic entrance caused by tripping.

The front began drifting north. Tavian closed his hand. "Signal." One horn sounded from the outer marker. Another answered farther up. Then a third. Across the approach, workers pulled light guide panels into their final positions. Militia stepped away from the center lane. Tavian watched the animals, not the people. The first sequence worked. Not beautifully. Nothing that large did anything beautifully. The lead prey committed to the wider northern approach. The mass behind them copied movement before understanding it. Smaller animals slipped between larger bodies. Ridge-hound calls followed from deeper in the trees. Brynn looked over her shoulder. "Final cart?" A runner answered. "Still clearing lower fallback."

"How long?"

"Moments."

Bad unit. Accurate enough. For half a minute, Greywake's plan looked competent. Then the first guide failed. A large browser struck the outer timber-and-rope frame sideways instead of following the opening. Wood snapped. One post folded. The animal stumbled through the gap. Everything behind it saw a new route. Three became six. Six became ten. The spill widened toward the fallback lane. Toward the cart. "South spill!" somebody shouted. Brynn's hand cut down. "Cart through! Workers off the lane! Militia give them space!" The driver whipped the draft animal forward. Wrong choice. The animal panicked harder.

The cart wheel bounced in the rut. Tavian pointed between the failed guide and the drainage ditch. "Low. Angle north. Spare panel!" Luck hauled at the nearest rope with a worker beside him, but the panel caught on its stacking peg. He pulled again. The peg snapped as a browser struck the fallen frame, knocking one end of the spare panel across the gap. The rope tightened around an upright still buried in sound ground. Instead of landing flat, the panel lodged at Tavian's angle. The first browser checked; the next crowded its shoulder and turned north. "Move!" Brynn shouted. The driver took the opening. A broken rail spun from the herd and struck Luck's shoulder hard enough to turn him.

Luck staggered into the marker behind him and stayed upright. "Clear!" Brynn called. A worker cut the spare line before it could catch the next group. The panel dropped flat and the last cart disappeared toward Greywake. The worker stared at the broken stacking peg. "You used the impact to set the angle," he said. Luck had used both hands to pull a rope unsuccessfully. "Discuss it after the animals," he said, pleased with the delivery and less pleased with his shoulder. A ridge-hound burst through the gap and snapped at a worker carrying rope. The worker dropped flat. A militia woman drove the hound sideways with her shield.

Another hound followed. Then a third. They were not hunting them. They were following prey and taking opportunities. That distinction did not help the worker on the ground. "Do not chase!" Tavian shouted. Brynn repeated him. "Hold the lane. Drive them off workers only." The militia formed around the retreating crew instead of turning the road into a hunt. One hound darted left. A spear turned it. Another crossed right. A homesteader with a road hook struck its shoulder and shoved it back into the open lane. The first worker got up.

Then the herd shifted again. A line of smaller prey cut too far east. Tavian's face changed. "That is wrong." Brynn looked at him. "Random spill?"

"Watch."

A second group followed the same angle. Then a third. Ridge-hounds used the opening behind them. Tavian pointed. "That route should be weakening by now."

"So something is pulling."

"Yes."

A runner came hard from the eastern marker. "Old service site!" Brynn turned. "What about it?"

"Active when the check team got line of sight."

Tavian stared at him. "Assigned team?"

"No."

"Changed by them?"

"No."

"Who has access?"

Nobody needed to answer. Hesk. Luck looked toward the eastern service route. Walking out of the battle and appearing in Hesk's doorway would make an excellent scene. He took one step before Tavian caught his sleeve. "Don't. You leave now, this line opens before you reach the site." Luck looked back at the workers hauling the remaining spare panels. They needed hands more urgently than Hesk needed an ominous visitor. A scream came from the lower detour. Brynn ordered two militia to remain with the guides and sent a runner to warn the eastern check team. Luck followed her toward the roadside bypass where the seasonal crossing had failed. Three workers were retreating from a collapsed light guide.

A fourth worker was below road level in the drainage cut, his leg pinned behind a broken rail. Ridge-hounds moved through the gap behind the prey. "Two with me. Keep the road open," Brynn ordered. Tavian pointed at the bank. "Do not drop more weight there." Luck grabbed a coil from a retreating worker and threw its loose end toward the trapped man. It missed his hand. The iron fastening ring struck a stone, bounced, and caught on the rail pinning his leg. The worker beside Luck pulled before Luck could apologize for the throw.

The line dragged the broken rail sideways. The trapped worker rolled clear and seized the rope. Two people hauled him up while Brynn's pair screened the gap. A ridge-hound glanced off a shield and slammed its shoulder into Luck's ribs, throwing him against stone. Breathing disappeared, then returned badly. The hound followed the prey. The rescued worker cleared the cut. "Fall back!" Brynn ordered. Nobody defended the bypass after it stopped being useful. Luck let another worker take the coil because his hands were shaking.

They retreated toward Broken North. The wrong pull had not stopped. Worse, it was compressing two flows against each other. Most prey still tried to follow the northern route. The unauthorized lure dragged a side branch east. Animals behind them saw both openings and hesitated. Hesitation became bunching. Bunching became pressure. Tavian stopped. "We are creating a crush point." Brynn looked across the lane. "Can we widen?"

"Only toward the homestead side."

"So no."

A horn sounded from Broken North. Three short notes. Repair pressure. They ran again. His ribs hurt. The pain shortened his stride. He fell behind the others before reaching Broken North. Broken North was already carrying more load than planned. Workers at the entrance fought to keep loose timber and stone out of the moving lane. Militia stood along the margins, not the center. One section of the upper guide bowed inward. Tavian pointed. "That outside branch. Turn only the front." Luck followed his line. A group of heavy browsers was drifting toward the weakest shoulder. If they broke it, the animals behind them would see another opening toward people. "What do you need?"

"Low guide. Here to here. Nothing higher."

Luck and a road worker dragged a spare timber guide toward Tavian's indicated angle. It was too heavy to set quickly. A fleeing animal clipped its trailing end and shoved it forward just as Luck lost his grip. The guide lodged against the piled repair stone. The lead browsers turned along it and the next bodies followed. Luck stepped out of the way, surprised to discover the job had finished without consulting him. Workers used the opening to clear broken material. Brynn moved two militia back three paces, keeping the exit visible.

They moved. A ridge-hound broke toward the workers. One militia guard intercepted it. Another hound came behind. A homesteader slammed a road hook into the dirt between them, forcing both to choose the open lane instead of the people. Nobody waited for him. Good. The guide had done its job. Two workers flattened it when Tavian signaled, restoring the open lane. The third pressure point was not the animals. It was the road itself. The repaired entrance groaned. Not failure. Warning. The foreman shouted from farther up. "Inside footing settling!" Tavian's eyes moved from the road to the herd. "Can you keep traffic light?" Brynn laughed once.

No humor. "Ask them." A new prey cluster entered. Large. Fast. The wrong-route pull tightened again. One group veered east hard enough to collide with another. Two animals went down. The rest split around them. Ridge-hounds behind stopped. Actually stopped. One backed away. Another abandoned the road entirely and climbed the slope. Tavian went still. Luck saw it a second later. Brush moved behind the prey. Not like a herd. Like something that did not care the trees were there.

A sapling bent, snapped, and knocked another aside. The nearest prey surged sideways. Workers retreated to marked lines while Brynn raised her hand. "Nobody closes center." Luck backed toward the equipment pile and tried not to make that movement look like backing away. The brush opened around a shoulder, then a head rising above the prey. Everything smaller made room. Luck had arranged his coat for the wrong audience.

Broken North groaned under the first wave. The unauthorized lure kept pulling east. And the largest territorial predator in the migration stepped into their line.
