Chapter 23 — Greywake Holds

The largest territorial predator in the migration had arrived. For one second, nobody moved. The animal filled the broken approach without needing to be monstrous in any supernatural sense. It was simply too large for the road, too close to frightened prey, and too angry at everything pressing around it. Thick shoulders forced through brush and young trees. Mud streaked its hide. One side of its face was marked by old scars that had nothing to do with us.

Then the prey behind it surged. The animal snapped its head around.

“Do not close on it!” Tavian shouted.

Three militia had already started inward with shields. They stopped hard.

“Open space on the north edge,” Tavian continued. “If you box it in, it comes through us.”

Brynn reacted before anyone could argue.

“North line back two steps. Spears low. Workers off the edge. Nobody advances unless it turns into the crew.”

The formation changed. Not perfectly. Fast enough.

The predator lowered its head and struck the remains of a guide frame with one shoulder. Timber cracked. The whole structure twisted away from the road and vanished under passing hooves.

Prey split around it. The animal turned after the larger opening, then stopped when another cluster cut across its path. Its tail slammed against the road edge hard enough to scatter loose stone.

I wanted to make something. A weapon. A wall.

Anything with a shape I could control. My hands were already trembling. Tavian looked at the animal, then at the road around it.

“Not yet.”

I did not argue. The predator took two heavy steps toward us. Militia shields came up.

It stopped again. Not because of us. Because the road behind it filled with more animals.

Tavian saw the change.

“It wants space.”

Brynn’s eyes stayed on the line.

“Where?”

“Not here yet.”

That was the problem. The migration had compressed too much. Every direction the predator could take was becoming another body, another fence, another slope, another person.

A work crew near the lower repair point had been waiting for a gap in the herd. One finally appeared.

“Move!” Brynn called.

Four workers ran toward the damaged guide edge carrying rope, timber, and a pry bar. They had maybe ten seconds. The predator turned toward them.

“Back!” Brynn shouted.

The crew reacted. Three made the marked line. The fourth slipped where the fill had broken loose.

He went down beside the road shelf. The predator moved. I was already running.

No power. Not yet. One militia man struck his shield with the butt of his spear.

Another shouted from the opposite side. The animal hesitated. I reached the fallen worker and grabbed the back of his coat.

“Leg?”

“Fine.”

Good. I pulled. He pushed.

The predator came closer. A shield hit its muzzle from the side. It jerked away.

I got the worker upright.

“Go.”

He went. I turned at the wrong time. The predator’s forelimb came across low and fast.

I saw the motion. Not enough to avoid it. Something hit my side.

The road disappeared. My back struck broken timber, then stone. For a moment there was no sound.

Then all of it returned at once. Hooves. Shouting.

Wood breaking. My own breathing. I tried to stand.

My left side failed first. Heat ran under my coat. Blood.

Not a shallow cut. The impact had opened something along my flank and driven the old rib pain deeper. I pressed one hand against it.

The wound was already tightening. It hurt enough to make my vision narrow.

“Red!”

Brynn. I pushed up. The leg I had damaged earlier almost folded.

A militia shield moved between me and the predator. Another spear point kept low near its chest. Nobody charged it.

Nobody tried to finish it. The animal struck at the shields, then backed from the noise. That gave me enough time to reach the protected side of the road.

Tavian was watching the predator. Not me. Good.

He needed to be watching the right problem.

“It keeps turning north,” he said.

Brynn glanced toward the brush lane above the road.

“The old service cut?”

“Too narrow right now.”

“It can be widened.”

“Not while the front is still crossing it.”

Another wave of prey came through. Smaller bodies this time. They moved faster and kept closer to the inside line.

The predator tracked them, then looked toward the less crowded brush above the old service cut. Tavian pointed.

“There. It wants that opening.”

Brynn raised her voice.

“Upper crew! Clear the service cut. Do not enter until I call it.”

Workers moved behind the militia screen. I followed. My side was closing.

Slowly. The bleeding had reduced from a stream to a steady line under my coat. My ribs still hurt.

My shoulder hurt. My leg hurt. My hands were worse.

The regeneration had kept me standing. It had not returned anything I had already spent. One worker shoved a coil of rope into my hands.

“Pull when we clear the post.”

I took it. Ordinary work. Good.

We dragged one broken marker aside. Two workers levered loose timber away from the service cut. The opening widened.

Not enough. The predator turned toward us again. Militia struck shields from the opposite side.

It faced them. Tavian watched the prey.

“Not yet!”

Brynn repeated it.

“Keep the cut closed!”

A worker looked at her.

“With what?”

“Your bodies not being inside it.”

He moved. The main migration was beginning to commit more strongly to Broken North. That was visible now.

The front groups were no longer testing every side route. More of them followed the line already established by the animals ahead. But the wrong pull still existed.

Downslope, another branch pressed toward the outer side. It forced more bodies into the same approach. Broken North shook under the extra load.

A sound came from beneath the road. Low. Stone grinding on stone.

Every worker nearby froze. Tavian looked toward the inner shelf.

“Again.”

The road moved. Not much. Enough.

A section of old stone under the inner edge settled lower. The timber support we had installed earlier still held, but one end shifted. Fill spilled into the drainage cut.

The usable lane narrowed. Tavian looked from the prey to the predator to the road. His decision took less than a second.

“That shelf goes, we lose the inside line.”

Brynn turned.

“Crew?”

A worker shook his head.

“Not with animals crossing.”

“How long?”

“Longer than we have.”

The road moved again. My hands were shaking. I knew what came next.

Tavian looked at me. Not at my face. At the failing shelf.

“Can you hold that?”

I followed his line. One section. Not the whole road.

Not the herd. Not the predator. Just stone that was trying to fall.

“Yes.”

Probably. Brynn pointed to the militia.

“Screen him. Repair crew ready behind.”

I stepped toward the shelf. The predator was still in the choke. Prey were still moving.

Nothing waited for me. I put one hand against the old stone. The pressure behind my eyes was already there.

I focused. The first shape tried to become too much. An arch.

Too wide. Too many points. The edges blurred before they had fully formed.

I stopped forcing detail. One job. Hold this section up.

The manifestation thickened beneath the shelf. Pale-grey material pushed between broken stone and the ground below, uneven at first, then settling into one brutal wedge of support. It was ugly.

It held.

“Now!” Brynn shouted.

The crew moved. Workers crawled into the protected side of the failure. Timber went under the shelf.

One man drove a wedge with a hammer. Another pulled rope through an old anchor point. Stone shifted above them.

My construct changed shape under the weight. Not intentionally. It compressed.

My hand shook against the road. The wound in my side pulled tighter. Pain moved under my ribs every time I breathed.

The pressure behind my eyes became a pulse. The construct tried to split. No.

One job. Hold. A crack formed along the outer face.

“Timber set!” someone shouted.

“Second brace!”

The predator struck a shield line behind me. Militia boots scraped over stone. Brynn shouted something I could not make out.

I kept looking at the shelf. The workers were still under it. That was enough.

The pale support bent. I simplified it again. Less surface.

More weight directly under the failure. The shape lost the cleaner edge it had briefly formed. It became thicker.

Cruder. Still one thing. Still holding.

“Rope locked!”

“Stone pack!”

Two workers shoved loose rock behind the timber. Another hammered. My vision darkened at the edges.

The construct cracked deeper. Then the worker closest to me shouted, “Load transferred!” I did not release it immediately.

“Again.”

He checked.

“Transferred!”

I let go. The pale-grey reinforcement collapsed inward and vanished. The road settled.

Stopped. Ordinary timber, rope, stone, and old masonry carried the weight. Not comfortably.

Enough. The workers crawled out. I nearly went down with them.

A militia man caught my shoulder. I stayed upright. No one said anything about what I had done.

They moved to the next problem. That was right. Tavian was already looking at the service cut.

The predator had turned toward it again. The main prey flow was now passing more consistently through Broken North. The gap at the upper cut widened as workers cleared the last brush and loose frame.

Tavian watched three groups pass. Then four. He pointed.

“Now.”

Brynn raised her arm.

“Open the cut! Upper line back! Lower shields forward two!”

Workers dragged the final timber aside. Militia shifted. The predator reacted instantly.

Space appeared where there had not been space before. It moved toward it. Not calmly.

It charged three steps, stopped when a shield line struck from the opposite flank, then swung hard enough to smash one abandoned post.

“Hold!” Brynn shouted.

Nobody closed the lane. The predator turned again. One worker stumbled too close to the opening.

I moved without thinking. Not power. My body.

I caught the worker and pulled him behind the marked line. The predator came through the space where he had been. For one moment its shoulder passed close enough that I could smell mud, blood, and animal heat.

Then it saw the open cut. Tavian shouted, “Give it the lane!” Militia pressure shifted.

Spears lowered on the wrong side. Shields opened on the right. The predator took the only path that did not lead into bodies.

It went through the service cut. Brush snapped. Young trees bent.

The animal disappeared uphill. A militia man started after it. Brynn’s voice stopped him.

“No chase!”

He stopped. The predator kept going. The main choke remained open.

Nobody celebrated. Prey were still moving. Workers went back to the road.

Militia reset their positions. Tavian stood in the center of all of it and watched the migration as if the large predator had already become history. The physical line had held.

Broken North looked worse than before. The inner shelf had fresh cracks. The repair timber was already taking weight.

Loose stone covered part of the drainage edge. The road would need real reconstruction after this. If there was an after this.

I leaned against a post. My side had mostly stopped bleeding. The pain had not.

My hands were shaking hard enough that I closed them into fists to hide it. That did not help. Tavian looked down the corridor.

“Main body is committing.”

Brynn followed his sightline. More prey were taking Broken North now. Not all.

Enough. The road had become the path of least resistance because we had spent people, timber, rope, blood, and one impossible support making it stay that way. For the first time since the first wave appeared, the choke was not losing ground.

Then Tavian turned toward the lower fork.

His expression changed.

Brynn saw it.

“What?”

He pointed.

At first I saw only movement.

Then I saw the angle.

A trailing branch of prey was not following the main body.

It was bending outward.

Toward the outer homestead route.

Another group followed.

Then another.

The physical line was holding.

Hesk’s lure was pulling them away from it again.
