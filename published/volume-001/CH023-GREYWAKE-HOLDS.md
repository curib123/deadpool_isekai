# Chapter 23 — Greywake Holds

The predator stepped into Broken North. For one second, nobody moved. Too large for the road. Too close to frightened prey. Too territorial to care. Then the herd behind it surged. "Do not close on it!" Tavian shouted. Three militia had already started inward. They stopped. Brynn's voice hit immediately after. "North line back two steps. Spears low. Workers off the inner edge."

The formation changed around the animal. It swung its head between the damaged road, workers, militia, and frightened prey. A browser struck the outer guide and snapped a timber. The predator flinched toward the sound, compressing the animals behind it. Luck took half a step toward a stout repair cart. He had chosen it because it offered the best available cover. A recruit saw the movement and followed him; Luck pointed the recruit back to Brynn before his supposed confidence spread any farther.

"Where does it want to go?" Luck asked Tavian. It was an honest question. Knowing the answer might improve his chances of standing somewhere else.

"Anywhere with less pressure."

"Helpful."

"It is."

A work crew near the lower repair point got a gap. Brynn saw it. "Move! Timber team through!" Four workers ran with rope and a bracing beam. The predator turned. "Back!" Tavian shouted. Three workers reached the marked line. The fourth slipped beside the road shelf. The predator moved.

Luck caught the slipping worker's coat and dragged him toward the repair cart. The predator lunged. Luck's boot slid in spilled gravel, dropping him against the cart shaft. His shoulder struck the brake lever. The loaded cart rolled from its shallow stop and the animal's forelegs hit the axle before its jaws could reach either man.

The cart lurched sideways, driving one shaft beneath the predator's jaw and turning its head. The loose stone load spilled over its front paws. It stumbled, snapped at the empty space where Luck had been, and recoiled from the grinding wheel. Luck pulled the worker behind the retaining marker. He had done nothing worth repeating, so he chose a sentence instead. "No." Tavian shouted over him. "Do not corner it!"

"I was moving away."

Tavian looked at Luck. Luck held the stare. "Fine. I was considering options." Tavian pointed uphill. "North service cut. That's its exit." Brynn turned. "Upper team! Clear the service cut. Everything loose out of the lane." Two workers ran uphill. One militia pair followed, shields facing the road. The predator watched them move.

*Good. Bad. It changed objectives.*

Instead of lunging at Luck again, the animal turned toward the workers at the service cut. Luck ducked behind the cart and caught his sleeve on a tool strap. The strap jerked a shovel from the load; it struck the metal wheel rim with a loud clang. The predator flinched away from the noise and checked its stride. Two militia raised their shields across the workers' retreat. "Leave it room to turn!" Tavian shouted. Luck tugged himself free and agreed privately with the principle.

"Why?"

"Because if we close it in, the herd behind it cannot read the exit."

The militia widened the gap instead of closing it. The predator backed toward the center, where prey were already bunching. Luck left the caught strap alone. His sleeve could survive a hole; his ribs had become less negotiable. The road made a deep grinding sound. The foreman pointed at the inner shelf as fill spilled into the drainage and a permanent timber brace shifted. "Workers clear!" Brynn ordered. Tavian looked between the animal and the road. "If that shelf drops, the inside lane closes."

The foreman crouched beside the crack. "Primary timber is still carrying. We need the cross-brace under it before the footing goes." Brynn sent four workers to him and pointed Luck toward a rope on the unloaded cart. "Help them." Luck took it beside two others. The brace hung at the wrong angle, pinned beneath spilled repair stone. They hauled while the foreman guided the lower end with a road hook.

The road lip sagged farther. Luck slipped backward and lost his pull. At the same instant, a browser struck the empty cart, jerking the line tight. The dragging brace swung against the old retaining masonry. One corner caught in a recess exposed by falling fill; the opposite end landed beneath the primary timber. The load settled onto it with a crack. "Clear!" the foreman shouted. Nobody stayed under it. They waited through another grinding sound before he approached from the side and checked both bearings.

"Temporary seat!" the foreman called. "It is carrying. Set the permanent wedges from the outside." Workers crawled along the protected edge with stone, timber, and mauls. The brace creaked each time the herd loaded the road. Luck could hear exactly how little he wanted to stand near it. A worker looked from the cart to the rope in his hands. "He timed the load transfer," the man said. The foreman did not bother deciding who deserved credit. "Then he can keep the spare line clear. Nobody under the shelf." Brynn assigned four militia to screen the repair crew and another pair to the service cut.

A runner arrived from the lower line. "East branch still pulling." Tavian did not turn. "How many?"

"Growing."

"Compared with five minutes ago?"

"Twice."

That was bad enough to qualify as information. Hesk's lure was still working. They were now holding three problems at once. The predator. The road. The wrong-route pull. Good climax design. Terrible morning. The territorial predator charged again. This time not at him. At the militia pair beside the service cut. It had learned something. Luck was the bad direction. So it chose another. One militia guard planted his shield.

The shield would not stop that weight. Luck shouted before finding anything clever to say. "Here!" The predator turned at the sound. One paw struck the broken cart shaft, which rolled across the gravel and sent the charge sideways into the unloaded frame. Luck jumped behind the marker as the frame splintered. The shield guard scrambled clear. "Keep it facing north!" Tavian shouted. Luck was facing north himself, with every intention of increasing the distance.

"I am trying."

"You are antagonizing it."

"Also true."

The upper workers cleared the final brush bundle from the service cut. "Open!" one shouted. Tavian watched a prey cluster crossing the approach. "Not yet." The predator lunged toward the moving bodies. Luck reached for the cart rope to avoid falling over it; his pull dragged a bent iron rim out of the wreckage. The rim struck the marker with another metallic crash. The animal checked and veered away from the noise. Behind Luck, the herd struck a guide frame and snapped its rope. "Outer spill!" Brynn shouted.

A cluster of smaller prey broke toward the repair crew. Luck grabbed a spare panel. Tavian stopped him. "Do not close them. Open their exit!" Brynn ordered two workers to cut the broken guide loose. The panel fell flat, the prey spread into open ground, and pressure dropped. Luck set his spare down before it became one more obstacle. The predator snapped toward him again, but a fleeing browser crossed its path and shouldered it away. Luck pressed himself behind the marker and took whatever help the animal was offering.

The foreman called that the first permanent brace was set. Another worker brought the second. The temporary cross-brace shifted under the changing load, but the first new wedge kept it seated. Luck helped tension the cart rope while the crew set a stone bearing behind it. Different jobs kept the same battle moving: Tavian read the herd, Brynn held the margins, the foreman watched the shelf, and Luck tried to remain where someone had actually asked him to stand. A ridge-hound cut after a militia runner. The runner tripped beside the guide line while the repair crew kept working.

Another hound came behind it. Brynn moved herself. Sword flat. Not trying to kill. She struck the first across the muzzle, kicked the second off the runner's path, and hauled the runner upright by his collar. "Back to signal line." He ran. Brynn turned immediately. No pose. No applause. That was command. The predator finally stopped trying to reach him. It looked at the service cut again. Tavian saw. "Almost."

"Define almost."

"Two more prey clusters."

"I hate ecology."

"No, you hate waiting."

"Also true."

The next cluster crossed. Then another. Tavian raised one hand. "Now." Brynn shouted. "Upper line open! Lower shields forward! Give it the lane!" Militia moved as one. The predator saw space. This time nobody occupied it. It lunged north. Luck did not stop it. It tore through the service cut, clipped one sapling, and vanished uphill. One militia man started after it. Brynn's voice stopped him. "No chase!" He froze. Correct. The predator was never the objective. Greywake was. The road still was. The herd still was. The wrong lure still was. The foreman called: "Load transfer!" Workers prepared to ease the rope and test the permanent timber bearings.

The foreman signaled a trial transfer. Workers eased the cart line, watched the first bearing, then the second. The inner lip settled without dropping. "Primary holds. Keep the temporary cross-brace until the stone wedge is set." Luck held the spare rope beside the worker assigned to him. His arms ached and he had no opinion on which timber should come out first.

The stone wedge went in. The foreman struck it twice and ordered the cart rope slackened. Timber groaned as the load transferred into the permanent bearings. It held. Luck let out the breath he had been saving and pulled the loose line out of the walking lane. A worker caught another by the belt before he slipped over the edge. There were rescues happening all around Luck that needed nothing from him. Then four ridge-hounds broke into the worker line.

Two militia intercepted the first pair. The other two came through the gap toward Luck. He raised the loose rope, intending to look larger. One end remained hooked around the smashed cart rim. A hound stepped inside the loop as it lunged; Luck fell backward and the tightening rope turned the animal north. The second struck the first's flank and recoiled. Tavian advanced with his polearm while Brynn closed the route toward the workers. All four took the open uphill lane. One claw had opened Luck's forearm on the way past. He wrapped it with a strip from his already damaged sleeve.

The nearest worker stared at the loop. "You kept that rope loose until the hounds committed. And the cart hit at the exact moment the shelf needed the brace. You were using their momentum for both jobs." Luck had wanted the rope out from under his feet. He stood slowly so his knees would not contradict the explanation.

"Momentum is difficult to argue with," he said.

The worker shouted to the foreman that Luck should direct the next load transfer. Luck pointed at the man already checking the bearings. "He has it." A successful mysterious sentence had very nearly acquired him a bridge.

The main prey body kept flowing. Broken North held because its workers repaired the footing, militia kept the margins open, and Tavian gave the animals somewhere to go.

The repairs were still ugly, and the temporary timber had taken damage that would matter tomorrow. Luck's sleeve was wet with blood. He leaned against the marker until Tavian looked toward the lower fork and went still. A trailing branch of prey was bending toward the outer homestead road: one group, another, then another. Brynn followed his gaze. "The physical line is holding." Tavian nodded. "Yes."

"Then why are they leaving it?"

He pointed toward the wrong branch. "The lure." Greywake had won the physical battle. Hesk was still trying to decide who paid for it.
