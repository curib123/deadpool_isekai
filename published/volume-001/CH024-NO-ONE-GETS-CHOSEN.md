# Chapter 24 — No One Gets Chosen

The physical line was holding. Hesk's pull was not. Tavian pointed toward the lower fork. The trailing branch peeling away from Broken North had grown since the last report. Not the main body. Too large to ignore. Browsers at the front took the same outer angle. Smaller prey followed. Ridge-hounds used the movement because predators were apparently loyal to bad logistics.

That route led toward the outer homesteads. Brynn asked how long they had; Tavian watched three groups cross before answering. "Not long enough." Behind them, workers and militia were holding Broken North without Luck beside the repair rope. Maelis and Jessa arrived with two militia witnesses, copied records, and the old-service route sheet. Brynn did not waste time. "Site?"

Jessa pointed downslope. "Same housing. Same sector code from the reserve records." Tavian took the sheet. He compared it with the wrong-moving branch. "That is the pull." Maelis lifted her packet. "Custody entries, service references, Jessa's signed explanation. Originals remain secured."

*Good. Evidence had survived the battle. Now they had to survive the evidence owner.*

The old-service housing sat inside a shallow stone recess beside the fork. Hesk stood beside it. The Roadmaster key was already inserted. The vent assembly had been opened farther than the coalition setting allowed. One directional plate had been turned toward the outer road. Jessa set her service-tool case beside the recess and pointed at the plate. "He changed the output." Tavian looked through the brush. "Yes." Brynn stepped forward. "Hands away from the housing." Hesk looked at her. "Captain."

"Now."

He did not move. The migration kept moving behind them. That mattered. This was not a courtroom. It was a route fight with paperwork. Maelis came to the edge of the recess. "We have the reserve records, issue entries, witnesses, your storehouse acknowledgment, and Jessa's signed explanation." Hesk looked at the packet. "Then you have paperwork."

"We have enough that destroying this housing changes nothing."

"I am not destroying it."

"No," Maelis said. "You are using it."

Another prey group took the outer angle. Tavian raised one hand. "Pull increasing." Brynn's voice hardened. "Hesk." He looked toward the migration. "Broken North is damaged."

"It is holding."

"For now."

"That is what roads do. They hold until they need repair."

"That is not a settlement policy."

"No. This is."

She pointed toward the outer road. "Stop sending the cost there." Hesk's expression tightened. "The Main Caravan Road is Greywake's lifeline."

"We know."

"Food."

"We know."

"Medicine."

"We know."

"Winter stores."

"We know."

"Then act like you know."

His voice finally sharpened. "The corridor you built is a gamble. Broken North is old stone, emergency timber and optimism."

"Mostly timber," Luck said.

Nobody appreciated it. Fair. Hesk looked at him. "You understand cost better than they do." Luck said nothing. "You walk out of consequences other people pay for. You act as if you can afford to be wrong." Luck felt the pull of the fresh wrap on his forearm. Hesk had not seen how closely those consequences had followed him. He continued. "Do not pretend you are different from me." Luck looked at the housing. The outer road. His bloodstained sleeve from the ridge-hounds. "I'm not."

His eyes narrowed. "But there is a difference."

"What?"

Luck pointed toward the homestead route. "You keep turning your choice into everybody else's problem. Eventually that became my problem. I hate being volunteered." It was a good line. He almost stopped to appreciate it. Then Hesk reached for the key. "He's rotating the plate," Jessa said. Luck lunged for his wrist and missed. His boot struck the case Jessa had placed beside the recess; the case slid beneath Hesk's heel. Hesk fell against the stone lip. The inserted key jammed at the half-turn. Luck caught his sleeve to keep him from striking the housing and found himself standing over him.

"Let go."

"No."

Brynn stepped in before Hesk recovered. "Release the key." Hesk shoved Luck away hard enough to send pain through his ribs. Luck stumbled backward and took the caught sleeve with him. A seam tore. Hesk lurched away from the mechanism just as Brynn seized his forearm, turned beneath it, and pinned his wrist against the stone lip. Luck steadied himself on the wall. The plan, such as he had possessed one, had ended when he missed the wrist.

Jessa moved to the housing. Hesk tried to hook Brynn's leg; she changed her stance and put him down. He was a road official with years of frontier experience, but she was trained for precisely this kind of physical resistance. "Key," she said. Luck stayed out of her way. An ominous silhouette could be useful, provided it did not obstruct the arrest.

Hesk twisted again. One of the militia witnesses moved to help. Brynn snapped: "Watch the road." Good. Everyone kept their job. Tavian was already at the fork. "Branch still widening!" That mattered more than Hesk's pride. Jessa reached for the key. Hesk's free hand closed around her wrist. She froze. Not because he was stronger. Because he was Hesk. Employer. Roadmaster. The person who had signed her job. His voice dropped. "Jessa." Her face changed. "You know what happens if this road fails."

"I know."

"Your brothers live here too."

"I know."

"Then stop helping them gamble with Greywake."

For one second, she did not move. Maelis stepped closer. Did not touch her. Did not answer for her. Jessa looked at Hesk. Then at the route sheet. Then at the key still waiting in the housing. "My brothers also live on roads you counted around." She pulled free. Brynn forced Hesk's arm behind him. Good. Jessa's choice stayed hers. Maelis pointed to the housing. "Document current position first." Jessa nodded. She read the plate mark aloud. Maelis recorded it. Another prey group bent outward. Tavian shouted. "Thirty seconds before the lead edge commits too far." Probably not a literal number. Close enough to be terrifying. Brynn looked at him. "Go." Hesk looked up. "You leave this site and she changes that wrong—" Luck stopped. Tavian shouted again. "Luck!"

There it was. The emotionally satisfying option: Stay. Make Hesk understand. Maybe break something he valued. Maybe his face. The useful option: Leave him to the people whose job this actually was. Annoying. Luck looked at Hesk. "You keep assuming I need to win against you." Then Luck turned his back on him. That felt better than hitting him. Mostly. The fork was chaos. Prey at the front had begun choosing the outer road before reaching the main split. Once enough bodies committed, momentum would make the lure unnecessary.

Tavian pointed to the inside edge. "Here." Luck looked. "Low?"

"Low."

"How long?"

"Until the front changes."

Tavian pointed at a portable guide panel lying beside the fork. Luck hauled one end while a militia worker took the other. They were late. A browser reached them before the panel was seated and struck its outside corner. The impact knocked it against the old marker exactly as the worker drove the first peg. The panel caught low across the unwanted opening. The animal turned north, bumped the next, and pulled three smaller prey after it. "Hold," Tavian said. Behind them, Jessa worked the housing while Maelis recorded each setting.

The branch pressed against the panel. Its rope stretched and one peg lifted. Luck braced beside the marker with the militia worker, pushing the timber back whenever an animal struck. It was not going to last long. Tavian watched the movement. "Not yet." Jessa called that the vent was closed. The animals already moving did not change direction immediately. Hesk laughed behind them. "You think turning one plate fixes years?"

Nobody answered. Tavian pointed at Jessa. "Neutral next. Wait for my signal." She kept one hand on the service plate. Another herd segment reached the fork. The front saw north-moving bodies. Some followed them. Others still took the outer line. Tavian watched. "Now." Jessa rotated the plate toward neutral. Click. No light. No explosion. No convenient magical confession. Just mechanism. The outer branch weakened. Not enough. A ridge-hound pack came behind the prey and chose the open outer angle. Four animals. Then six. Tavian swore. That was new. "Predator momentum." Brynn looked over while keeping Hesk down. "Problem?"

"If they take outer, prey behind may follow."

Luck looked at the hounds and stepped back from the panel. A stone shifted under his boot. His hand caught the loose end of a guide line and drew it tight across the outer approach as the lead hound entered. The animal's forepaw struck the line. It stumbled against its packmate; both recoiled north, toward the wider opening. The following hounds copied the turn before reaching the obstruction. Luck let go as soon as the line began pulling him forward. Tavian drove the last hound away from the homestead angle with his polearm.

Hesk stared from under Brynn's grip. "The case. The key. You made me stop turning it, then waited for Jessa to close the vent before changing the pack's direction. You came here knowing how it would end."

Luck had come hoping to deliver one good sentence. The rest of the accusation contained rather more preparation than he had brought. He straightened his torn coat.

"You had your turn," he said.

Maelis looked at the scraped case and marked the interruption separately from the settings. Hesk kept talking as if the mechanism had been Luck's trap. Luck left the explanation to the people who had actually studied it.

Tavian looked at the dropped line, then at Luck. "That was reckless."

"That was ecology."

"Do not say that."

Fair. The hounds cleared the fork. Prey behind them followed the visible movement. The branch narrowed again. Tavian raised his hand. "Keep neutral." Jessa did. Maelis checked every step against the service record. "Locking pin." Jessa found it. Brynn shifted Hesk to one knee. He stopped resisting physically. His argument was still alive. "You are choosing the outer road now." Brynn looked at him. "No."

"You are choosing Broken North."

"We are choosing a route with no inhabited settlement assigned as the sacrifice."

"It can fail."

"Yes."

"People can die."

"Yes."

"And you call that better?"

Brynn's expression did not change. "I call it shared." That silenced him longer than anything Luck had said. Jessa set the service catch. Removed the key. Passed it to Brynn. Then drove the locking pin through the housing. The lure stopped being Hesk's private decision. Tavian watched the split. "Hold." More prey took Broken North. The outer angle weakened. Another group approached. Followed north. Another. North. He lowered his hand. "Release." Luck and the militia worker lowered the panel flat and pulled its loose rope out of the lane. The herd kept turning. That mattered.

No new obstruction. No fresh pull from Hesk's lure. Momentum through a road Greywake had chosen together. The wrong branch emptied by degrees. Tavian waited another full cycle of movement. Then: "Committed." Brynn bound Hesk's wrists. "Rovan Hesk, you are under emergency detention pending council and Ledger review." No victory speech. No dramatic execution. No one needed him to stand over him. Maelis sealed the current-setting record.

Jessa kept the service key out of Guild custody. Tavian kept watching the migration because unlike the rest of them, he understood that winning an argument did not make animals stop moving. Luck looked toward Broken North. The main body continued through. The outer homestead road stayed clear of the mass Hesk had tried to send there. Greywake had not solved every future road problem. Kellan still could not walk. The seasonal bridge was still gone. Trade would still pay for damaged infrastructure.

The migration would still leave ecology confused. Getting through the battle had changed none of those costs. But the final choice was visible. Recorded. Shared. No private ledger. No hidden acceptable loss. No one had been chosen to lose.
