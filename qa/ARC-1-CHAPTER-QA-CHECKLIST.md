# Arc 1 Chapter-by-Chapter QA Checklist

**Applies to:** Every chapter in Arc 1 / Greywake, CH-001 through CH-026  
**Genres:** Dark Fantasy + Comedy + Isekai + Overpowered MC + Action + Adventure + Shounen  
**Status:** STUDIO STANDARD — CURRENT FIRST-PERSON ARC 1  
**Separate from:** `qa/VOLUME-001-GREYWAKE-OFFICIAL-ARC-1-QA.md`, which governs arc-level QA

## Status rules

- **PASS** — the section's applicable checks are satisfied by this chapter.
- **WEAK** — the section exists but needs a targeted improvement.
- **MISSING** — a required applicable element is absent.
- **N/A** — the section or conditional element is intentionally unnecessary for this chapter.
- Every `WEAK` or `MISSING` result must have an immediately adjacent `FIX:` line using: `Problem → Cause → Best Chapter → Smallest Effective Fix → Continuity Check → Re-QA`.
- Conditional sections do not require every chapter to contain a battle, joke, dark scene, power reveal, mystery, travel, emotional beat, or hype moment. Do not force genre material into a chapter only to turn a checkbox green.
- **Mandatory engagement overlay:** every chapter must separately pass `qa/CHAPTER-ENGAGEMENT-QA-STANDARD.md`. Combat is optional, but an active engagement event is not.
- A chapter that passes these 35 sections but fails the engagement overlay cannot be marked FINAL.

## 1. Chapter Purpose — MANDATORY

- [ ] Clear purpose exists.
- [ ] Something changes before the chapter ends.
- [ ] Chapter advances plot, character, relationship, mystery, worldbuilding, conflict, adventure, power system, or emotional development.
- [ ] Chapter is not present only to increase chapter count.
- [ ] Removing it would noticeably affect the story.

If MISSING: merge, rewrite, shorten, or remove the chapter.

## 2. Opening Hook — MANDATORY

- [ ] First paragraph immediately places the reader in a scene.
- [ ] Opening contains movement, emotion, curiosity, danger, humor, or tension.
- [ ] Opening avoids unnecessary exposition.
- [ ] Reader quickly understands where the MC is and what is happening.
- [ ] Opening does not repeat the previous chapter unnecessarily.
- [ ] First page creates a reason to continue.

## 3. Immediate Chapter Goal

- [ ] Someone wants something.
- [ ] Preferably the MC has an immediate objective.
- [ ] Objective is understandable.
- [ ] Something interferes with the objective.
- [ ] Objective can change when an unexpected event occurs.

## 4. MC Presence — MANDATORY

- [ ] MC personality is visible.
- [ ] MC makes at least one meaningful decision.
- [ ] MC is not simply dragged through the chapter.
- [ ] Reactions are consistent with previous chapters.
- [ ] Thoughts/dialogue are recognizable.
- [ ] MC remains entertaining without his strongest ability.
- [ ] MC does not become generic in serious scenes.

## 5. Character Voice

- [ ] Important characters sound different.
- [ ] Dialogue reflects personality.
- [ ] Characters are not exposition machines.
- [ ] Reactions feel natural.
- [ ] Interruption, hesitation, disagreement, teasing, misunderstanding, or silence appears where appropriate.
- [ ] MC's dialogue remains recognizable.

## 6. Character Chemistry

If multiple characters are present:

- [ ] Interaction feels alive.
- [ ] Characters do not merely take turns giving information.
- [ ] Relationship changes slightly or reveals something.
- [ ] MC behaves differently with different people.
- [ ] Banter is not forced.
- [ ] Serious moments are allowed to breathe.

## 7. Comedy Check

If comedy appears:

- [ ] Joke fits character or situation.
- [ ] Joke is not copied without development.
- [ ] Comedy does not interrupt an important emotional beat.
- [ ] Other characters react naturally.
- [ ] Joke does not make real danger meaningless.
- [ ] Chapter does not force every paragraph to be funny.
- [ ] Some humor comes from personality rather than punchlines.

## 8. Dark Fantasy Check

If dark material appears:

- [ ] Danger feels real.
- [ ] World feels hostile, strange, tragic, or morally complicated.
- [ ] Consequences are not erased by humor.
- [ ] Monsters/threats behave like actual threats.
- [ ] Violence has appropriate weight.
- [ ] Darkness serves plot, character, atmosphere, or worldbuilding.
- [ ] Darkness is not present only for shock.

## 9. Isekai Check

When relevant:

- [ ] MC's outsider perspective matters.
- [ ] Something about the world feels unfamiliar.
- [ ] MC interprets something differently because of his previous life.
- [ ] Earth knowledge does not automatically solve everything.
- [ ] Cultural misunderstandings occur naturally where appropriate.
- [ ] Isekai premise has not disappeared.

Not every chapter needs this section to be applicable.

## 10. OP MC Check

When MC uses power:

- [ ] Ability feels impressive.
- [ ] Power use matches established rules.
- [ ] MC uses it creatively where appropriate.
- [ ] Other characters react appropriately.
- [ ] Power does not remove every source of tension.
- [ ] Power use demonstrates personality as well as strength.
- [ ] MC does not gain exactly the required ability without setup.

## 11. Power-System Consistency

- [ ] Established rules remain consistent.
- [ ] Limitations are respected.
- [ ] Counters remain possible.
- [ ] New information expands rather than contradicts the system.
- [ ] Reader can approximately understand why an ability succeeds.
- [ ] If MC breaks rules, the story recognizes that break.

## 12. Mystery Check

If mystery appears:

- [ ] Chapter gives information, clue, contradiction, discovery, or question.
- [ ] Mystery progresses.
- [ ] Characters notice meaningful clues.
- [ ] Reader can form theories.
- [ ] Not every question is immediately answered.
- [ ] New mystery does not replace older mysteries without progress.

## 13. Worldbuilding Check

- [ ] Worldbuilding appears naturally.
- [ ] Information is relevant to the current scene.
- [ ] Reader learns through action, environment, dialogue, objects, customs, danger, or consequences.
- [ ] Proper nouns are controlled.
- [ ] Chapter does not stop for a history lecture.
- [ ] Location feels physically present.
- [ ] NPCs make the world feel inhabited.

## 14. Adventure Check

When applicable:

- [ ] Characters discover something.
- [ ] Location contributes to the scene.
- [ ] Environment creates opportunity or danger.
- [ ] Travel does more than move characters from A to B.
- [ ] Exploration reveals worldbuilding naturally.
- [ ] Chapter increases curiosity about the larger world.

## 15. Conflict — MANDATORY

- [ ] Some resistance exists.
- [ ] Something prevents an easy outcome.
- [ ] Conflict may be physical, emotional, social, environmental, strategic, mysterious, or internal.
- [ ] Characters must respond to the problem.
- [ ] Situation becomes different because of that response.

## 16. Battle Checklist

If combat occurs:

- [ ] Fight has a clear reason.
- [ ] Combatants have objectives.
- [ ] Battlefield is understandable.
- [ ] Characters use different tactics.
- [ ] Opponent reacts intelligently.
- [ ] Environment matters where appropriate.
- [ ] Injury/damage affects behavior.
- [ ] Fight develops rather than repeating attack → dodge → attack.
- [ ] Something changes during the battle.
- [ ] Fight reveals personality.
- [ ] Fight reveals information or creates consequences.
- [ ] Reader understands why the fight ends.

## 17. Battle Variety

Check that the fight is not too similar to the previous one. Mark only structures that naturally apply:

- [ ] 1v1
- [ ] 1vMany
- [ ] 2v1
- [ ] team battle
- [ ] monster hunt
- [ ] ambush
- [ ] chase
- [ ] rescue
- [ ] defense
- [ ] survival
- [ ] capture mission
- [ ] protecting someone
- [ ] enemy trying to escape
- [ ] temporary alliance
- [ ] enemy vs enemy
- [ ] environmental hazard during combat

## 18. OP Battle Tension

If MC is far stronger:

- [ ] Tension is not falsely based on whether MC can win physically.
- [ ] Another objective matters: protect someone, stop a ritual, capture alive, prevent escape, avoid destruction, protect identity, gather information, meet a deadline, save multiple people, or understand an unknown power.

## 19. Supporting Character Check

- [ ] Side characters do something useful.
- [ ] They make decisions.
- [ ] They do not only praise MC.
- [ ] Someone may disagree with or misunderstand MC.
- [ ] Actions reflect their goals.
- [ ] At least occasionally, they solve problems without MC.

## 20. Emotional Beat

- [ ] Character emotion fits what happened.
- [ ] Important events receive reactions.
- [ ] Previous consequences are remembered.
- [ ] Emotion is not repeatedly explained.
- [ ] Important feelings appear through action/dialogue when possible.
- [ ] Humor does not erase meaningful emotion.

## 21. Shounen Energy

When appropriate:

- [ ] Determination is clear.
- [ ] Loyalty, friendship, or rivalry matters.
- [ ] Characters make bold decisions.
- [ ] Someone pushes beyond expectations.
- [ ] Teamwork or courage matters.
- [ ] Chapter contains a satisfying hype moment when appropriate.

Not every chapter needs hype.

## 22. Scene Structure

For each major scene:

- [ ] Scene has a purpose.
- [ ] Character wants something.
- [ ] Something interferes.
- [ ] Scene develops.
- [ ] Something changes.
- [ ] Scene exits without overstaying.
- [ ] Transition to next scene is understandable.

## 23. Pacing

- [ ] Chapter does not drag.
- [ ] Important moments receive enough space.
- [ ] Minor transitions stay concise.
- [ ] Exposition does not dominate.
- [ ] Action is not rushed.
- [ ] Emotional scenes are not rushed.
- [ ] Repetition is removed.
- [ ] Chapter has natural rhythm.
- [ ] Paragraph sizes are readable.

## 24. Description

- [ ] Important locations are visualizable.
- [ ] Important characters/actions are clear.
- [ ] Description supports atmosphere.
- [ ] Sensory details are selective.
- [ ] Description does not freeze the story.
- [ ] Setting remains present during dialogue and battles.

## 25. Dialogue QA

- [ ] Dialogue sounds natural aloud.
- [ ] Characters do not explain what everyone already knows.
- [ ] Dialogue has purpose.
- [ ] Subtext appears where appropriate.
- [ ] Physical reactions support dialogue.
- [ ] Dialogue tags are not distracting.
- [ ] Conversation changes information, emotion, relationships, or decisions.

## 26. Prose QA

- [ ] POV remains consistent.
- [ ] Narration voice matches the novel.
- [ ] Internal thoughts sound like MC.
- [ ] Repetitive words/phrases are minimized.
- [ ] Sentences vary naturally.
- [ ] Action is easy to follow.
- [ ] Emotional writing is not unintentionally melodramatic.
- [ ] Prose feels alive rather than mechanical.

## 27. Continuity — MANDATORY

- [ ] Timeline is correct.
- [ ] Character location is correct.
- [ ] Injuries are remembered.
- [ ] Inventory/items are consistent.
- [ ] Character knowledge is consistent.
- [ ] Powers are consistent.
- [ ] Relationships reflect previous chapters.
- [ ] Names/titles remain consistent.
- [ ] Weather/time/travel remain sensible.
- [ ] No contradiction with previous canon.

## 28. Setup and Payoff

- [ ] Previous setups are remembered.
- [ ] Chapter may pay off an earlier detail.
- [ ] New setup has a reason to exist.
- [ ] Foreshadowing is not too obvious.
- [ ] Important future developments are not introduced from nowhere.

## 29. Chapter Progression Test — MANDATORY

Answer: **What is different now?** At least one must be true:

- [ ] MC knows something new.
- [ ] MC made a decision.
- [ ] Character relationship changed.
- [ ] Threat became greater.
- [ ] Threat was reduced.
- [ ] New location was reached.
- [ ] Mystery progressed.
- [ ] Character gained/lost something.
- [ ] Enemy made a move.
- [ ] New consequence appeared.
- [ ] Goal changed.
- [ ] Arc moved closer to climax.

If nothing changed, revise the chapter.

## 30. Chapter Ending — MANDATORY

- [ ] Ending feels intentional.
- [ ] Final paragraph has impact.
- [ ] Reader wants to continue.
- [ ] Ending is not abruptly cut.
- [ ] Ending does not repeat the previous chapter's cliffhanger.

Possible endings: revelation, danger, decision, emotional realization, comedy callback, unexpected arrival, new clue, victory with complication, defeat, ominous discovery, destination reached, or an answered question revealing a larger question.

## 31. Next-Chapter Test

Ask: **If I were a reader, would I click Next Chapter immediately?**

- [ ] Yes because I want to know what happens.
- [ ] Yes because I care about the characters.
- [ ] Yes because of a mystery.
- [ ] Yes because conflict is escalating.
- [ ] Yes because the chapter itself was enjoyable.

Stopping in the middle of a sentence/action is not sufficient.

## 32. No-Filler Test

- [ ] Chapter contributes to Arc 1.
- [ ] Quiet scenes still develop something.
- [ ] Comedy scenes reveal character, relationship, or world.
- [ ] Travel scenes have purpose.
- [ ] Training scenes produce meaningful progression, when present.
- [ ] Repeated information is removed.

## 33. Arc Position Check

Mark the chapter's actual phase:

**Early Arc:** hook, MC, world, relationships, first conflict, mystery, power demonstration.  
**Middle Arc:** deepen relationships, increase mystery, expand world, escalate opposition, complicate goals, midpoint change.  
**Late Arc:** converge threads, increase consequences, reduce detours, accelerate mysteries/payoffs, major battles, emotional pressure, climax preparation.  
**Final Chapters:** climax, consequences, emotional payoff, Arc 1 resolution, Arc 2 hook.

## 34. Memorable Moment Test

Ideally every chapter has at least one memorable element: funny moment, creepy moment, character interaction, powerful line, battle moment, discovery, mystery clue, emotional moment, world detail, OP moment, or shocking development. It does not need to be huge.

## 35. Final Chapter Gate

Before marking a chapter FINAL:

- [ ] Clear purpose.
- [ ] Strong opening.
- [ ] MC feels like himself.
- [ ] Something meaningful happens.
- [ ] Conflict exists.
- [ ] Character/world/plot progresses.
- [ ] Dialogue feels alive.
- [ ] No major pacing issue.
- [ ] No continuity error.
- [ ] Ending creates momentum.
- [ ] Chapter contributes to Arc 1.
- [ ] No obvious filler.
- [ ] Genre tone remains consistent.

## Mandatory Engagement Overlay

Before `Chapter Result` is finalized, verify the chapter's primary engagement event in:

`qa/CHAPTER-ENGAGEMENT-QA-STANDARD.md`

Allowed final overlay results:
- PASS — COMBAT ENGAGEMENT
- PASS — NON-COMBAT ENGAGEMENT
- PASS — HYBRID ENGAGEMENT

WEAK/FAIL blocks final chapter approval.

## Chapter Result

- **PASS** — chapter can move forward.
- **PASS WITH REVISION** — story works, but targeted improvements remain; the affected checklist sections must be `WEAK` with adjacent `FIX:` lines.
- **FAIL** — mandatory structural problem exists; revise before publication.
- **N/A** — specific genre element is intentionally unnecessary in this chapter.

The governing rule is: **Every chapter must either move the story forward or deepen why readers care about the story—and the strongest chapters do both.** Individual chapters may specialize; forcing every chapter to contain a battle, joke, dark scene, power reveal, or cliffhanger would make the arc repetitive.
