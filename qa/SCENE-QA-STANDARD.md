# 1. Scene Purpose Rule

Every scene must change something.

A scene must advance at least one:
- objective;
- conflict;
- relationship;
- knowledge;
- mystery;
- world understanding;
- tactical position;
- institutional position;
- physical location;
- emotional pressure;
- consequence;
- setup/payoff.

If removing the scene changes nothing meaningful, **CUT OR MERGE IT**.

# 2. Required Scene Card

Every scene plan must identify:

- **Scene ID**
- **Chapter ID**
- **Location**
- **Time / sequence position**
- **Present characters**
- **Opening state**
- **Primary objective**
- **Secondary objective**
- **Who wants what**
- **Immediate obstacle**
- **Hidden pressure**
- **Information available at opening**
- **Information forbidden at opening**
- **Worldbuilding purpose**
- **Character purpose**
- **Red stable-character expression**
- **Dialogue function**
- **Comedy opportunity**
- **Serious-beat requirement**
- **Action/battle requirement, if any**
- **Play Logic use, if any**
- **Fourth-Wall Pause use, if any**
- **Mystery / F-ID / M-ID state**
- **Choice**
- **Scene turn**
- **Consequence**
- **Ending state**
- **Why the next scene must happen**

# 3. Character Presence QA

Every present character needs a reason to be there.

FAIL if:
- a character exists only to explain lore;
- a character appears only to praise Red;
- a specialist is present but never uses their specialty;
- a character knows something they should not know;
- a character is conveniently absent only because their competence would solve the scene;
- everyone automatically agrees with Red.

Supporting characters must retain:
- independent goals;
- independent reactions;
- their own knowledge limits;
- their own decisions.

# 4. Red Jackal Scene QA

Every Red-centered scene must preserve the stable story engine.

Check:
- close first-person limited;
- Red remains self-authored and freedom-focused;
- no conventional moral-development lesson is imposed;
- useful actions may have selfish/personal motives;
- theatricality appears when appropriate, not as a quota;
- serious mode means dropping performance/restraint, not powering up;
- mistakes can come from curiosity, ego, boredom, role-play, incomplete information or refusal of control;
- Red may learn facts/tactics without becoming morally normalized.

# 5. Dialogue QA

Dialogue must have function.

At least one of:
- reveals intent;
- withholds intent;
- negotiates;
- threatens;
- challenges;
- creates subtext;
- changes trust;
- transfers necessary information;
- creates conflict;
- creates comedy;
- establishes voice;
- forces a decision.

Avoid:
- exposition tennis;
- every character speaking in the same rhythm;
- speeches that explain what everyone already knows;
- repeated restatement of the previous scene;
- dialogue that exists only to tell the reader the plot.

Allow:
- interruption;
- silence;
- unfinished answers;
- callbacks;
- refusal to answer;
- people ignoring Red's joke;
- practical speech under pressure.

# 6. Comedy / Serious Beat QA

Comedy is optional and situational.

A scene may be:
- comedic;
- mixed;
- mostly serious;
- completely serious.

Do not force a joke after:
- severe injury;
- death;
- civilian harm;
- betrayal;
- major revelation;
- emotionally exposed confession;
- irreversible consequence.

Red may still think like Red, but timing matters.

# 7. Mystery / Knowledge QA

Before prose, verify:
- what Red knows;
- what each supporting character knows;
- what the reader may infer;
- what remains writer truth only;
- whether an F-ID is PLANNED or already PLANTED;
- whether the scene actually contains the clue before marking it planted;
- whether an M-ID reveal level changes.

No protected truth may enter through convenient exposition.

# 8. Worldbuilding QA

Worldbuilding must enter because the scene needs it.

Prefer:
- procedure;
- work;
- travel;
- architecture;
- food;
- law;
- money;
- equipment;
- environment;
- customs;
- conflict;
- consequences.

Avoid encyclopedic blocks unless the scene itself is a formal explanation with a reason to exist.

# 9. Action / Battle Trigger

If meaningful hostile action occurs, the scene must also pass:

**qa/BATTLE-SCENE-QA-STANDARD.md**

A fight scene cannot pass Scene QA while failing Battle QA.

# 10. Play Logic / Regeneration QA

If Play Logic appears:
- define the immediate intended role;
- do not infer a maximum from the chosen expression;
- do not add Stage/progression;
- do not add finite stamina/cooldown;
- preserve collateral/information consequences;
- explain through context why Red chooses that expression.

If regeneration appears:
- pain may remain;
- damage to clothing/equipment/environment remains;
- others do not inherit Red's recovery;
- no finite regeneration resource is invented;
- no medical expert learns protected writer truth without evidence.

# 11. Fourth-Wall Pause QA

For every genuine external-audience address:
- local time freezes;
- physical motion/sound freezes;
- Red comments;
- Red returns to the same instant;
- native characters do not notice or remember it.

No free tactical repositioning or hidden-information exploitation.

# 12. Pacing / Length QA

Every scene should enter as late as practical and leave after its turn/consequence.

Check:
- no repeated setup;
- no repeated conclusion;
- no unnecessary arrival/departure business;
- no explanation after the reader already understands;
- no artificial cliffhanger created by withholding something the POV character plainly knows.

Scene length follows function, not quota.

# 13. Scene Turn QA

Every scene requires a turn.

Examples:
- new evidence;
- objective changes;
- character refuses;
- authority shifts;
- danger appears;
- plan succeeds but creates a new cost;
- joke fails and exposes tension;
- Red makes a choice;
- ally reveals a boundary;
- opponent adapts;
- route closes;
- clue recontextualizes earlier information.

A scene without a turn is likely summary or filler.

# 14. Scene Handoff QA

The ending must create the next scene through causality.

Good handoffs:
- discovery requires verification;
- injury requires treatment;
- argument requires a decision;
- evidence requires confrontation;
- failed route requires alternate route;
- battle consequence requires cleanup;
- choice creates pursuit;
- unanswered question creates investigation.

Avoid:
- “and then later” with no causal bridge;
- next scene happening only because the outline says so.

# 15. Repetition QA

Across adjacent scenes, compare:
- location;
- participant mix;
- objective;
- conflict type;
- dialogue rhythm;
- Red behavior;
- information function;
- ending shape.

If two scenes perform the same function, merge or differentiate them.

# 16. Scene-Level Pass Standard

A scene passes only when:

- purpose is necessary;
- participants are justified;
- knowledge boundaries are correct;
- character voices are distinct;
- dialogue has function;
- comedy timing is appropriate;
- action geography is clear if relevant;
- mystery state is correct;
- no power/progression drift exists;
- the scene turns;
- the consequence carries;
- the next scene is causally earned.

# 17. Gate Integration

Scene QA is mandatory at:
- **Gate 7** — roadmap scene-function preview;
- **Gate 8** — full Scene Card validation;
- **Gate 9** — manuscript execution validation;
- **Gate 10** — arc repetition / scene-variety audit.
