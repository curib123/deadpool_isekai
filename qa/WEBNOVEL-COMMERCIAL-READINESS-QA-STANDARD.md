# WebNovel Commercial Readiness QA Standard

**Project:** This Isekai Has a Bug. Unfortunately, It's Me.  
**Purpose:** Evaluate reader-acquisition, early-retention, and contract-submission readiness for a pay-by-chapter web-novel environment.  
**Applies to:** Public title, synopsis, prologue if used, opening chapters, chapter hooks/endings, early-arc pacing, and the advertised genre promise.  
**Relationship to canon:** This QA does **not** decide canon quality. A chapter can be canonically correct and still fail commercial-readiness QA.  
**Relationship to Gate 11:** Gate 11 asks whether the publication copy is correct. This standard asks whether the publication package is commercially strong enough to submit or promote.  
**Guarantee rule:** Passing this QA does not guarantee a WebNovel contract, profitability, ranking, or reader growth.

## Source Principle

This standard incorporates the commercial criteria described in the WebNovel editorial guidance supplied by the author: attractive title/synopsis, dramatic opening, strong early inciting incidents, memorable leads, suspense/expectation, good pacing, and avoidance of plain openings, slow pacing, information dumps, and low-tension chapter progression.

The cited editorial article was written from a female-lead/editorial perspective. Therefore:

- romance is **not** forced into this male-led action/comedy isekai;
- "strong romantic conflict" becomes **strong central conflict, relationship friction, rival/antagonist pressure, or another genre-appropriate emotional engine**;
- romance/harem expectations are checked only if the public metadata actually markets the book that way;
- market fit never overrides the project's locked genre identity or originality.

## Status Rules

- **PASS** — strong enough for the intended commercial-reader function.
- **WEAK** — present, but likely to reduce click-through or early retention.
- **FAIL** — a critical commercial-readiness problem is present.
- **N/A** — legitimately not required by this project's marketed genre.
- **PASS WITH TARGETED REVISION** — the package is fundamentally viable, but one or more WEAK items should be corrected before contract submission.

Every WEAK or FAIL must include:
**Problem → Reader Risk → Smallest Effective Fix → Canon/Continuity Check → Re-QA.**

---

# 1. Title / Click Promise — MANDATORY

- [ ] Title is easy to remember.
- [ ] Title communicates genre, premise, tone, or curiosity.
- [ ] Title does not depend on copyrighted character names or misleading fan-fiction branding.
- [ ] Title is not so generic that it disappears beside comparable books.
- [ ] Title promise is actually delivered by the opening chapters.

# 2. Synopsis / Storefront Conversion — MANDATORY

- [ ] First 1–2 sentences contain the strongest premise.
- [ ] MC, anomaly/advantage, central problem, and genre promise are understandable.
- [ ] Synopsis creates questions instead of answering the whole series.
- [ ] Synopsis avoids lore-listing and internal terminology overload.
- [ ] Synopsis is concise enough to scan on mobile.
- [ ] Synopsis does not summarize multiple future arcs when a sharper hook would be stronger.
- [ ] Final lines leave a concrete reason to open Chapter 1.

# 3. Opening Event — CRITICAL

The first public reading unit (prologue if present, otherwise Chapter 1) must quickly deliver at least one of:

- danger;
- impossible event;
- conflict;
- strong mystery;
- comedy with consequence;
- dramatic reversal;
- immediate emotional disruption.

It must not spend its opening pages on neutral history, world explanation, or routine setup.

# 4. Prologue Necessity / Duplication — CRITICAL WHEN A PROLOGUE EXISTS

- [ ] Prologue provides material Chapter 1 does not simply repeat.
- [ ] Prologue creates a stronger first impression than beginning directly at Chapter 1.
- [ ] Prologue does not replay the same death, awakening, exposition, or reveal almost immediately.
- [ ] Removing the prologue would noticeably reduce reader understanding, anticipation, mystery, or emotional impact.

If the prologue duplicates Chapter 1, either remove it from the public sequence or revise it into a genuinely different promise.

# 5. First-Three-Chapter Retention Funnel — CRITICAL

By the end of CH-003, the reader should understand:

- who Red Jackal is as a personality;
- what makes him abnormal/overpowered;
- what immediate problem he faces;
- why Veyr cannot process him normally;
- what practical consequence the anomaly creates;
- what larger danger, mystery, opportunity, antagonist pressure, or destination conflict is beginning.

The first three chapters must not feel like three versions of the same setup.

# 6. Inciting-Incident Strength — MANDATORY

Check both levels:

**Series inciting incident**
- death/explosion;
- Soul Drift arrival;
- undefined System status;
- abnormal powers/fourth-wall behavior.

**Current-arc inciting incident**
- a concrete local problem pulls Red into the destination's conflict.

A strong series incident does not excuse an arc that takes too long to begin.

# 7. Genre-Promise Delivery — MANDATORY

For this project, the opening must begin delivering:
- Isekai;
- Comedy;
- Overpowered MC;
- Dark Fantasy danger;
- Action/Adventure;
- mystery/anomaly;
- Shounen-style momentum where appropriate.

Not every chapter needs every element, but the reader must see enough evidence early to trust the advertised genre blend.

# 8. Memorable MC — CRITICAL

- [ ] Red has a recognizable voice within the first page.
- [ ] His choices, not just powers, distinguish him.
- [ ] Comedy emerges from character/situation.
- [ ] Serious moments remain credible.
- [ ] He is not merely a collection of references to other characters.
- [ ] The reader can describe his personality without naming an inspiration.

# 9. Supporting-Cast / Relationship Engine

For an ML action/comedy fantasy, the commercial equivalent of FL romantic tension can be:
- strong banter/chemistry;
- distrust;
- rivalry;
- reluctant partnership;
- authority conflict;
- mystery partnership;
- ideological clash;
- attraction if organically present.

- [ ] At least one recurring relationship gives readers an interpersonal reason to continue.
- [ ] Supporting characters have agency and do not exist only to praise Red.
- [ ] Important relationships change or deepen across the early arc.

# 10. Early Antagonist / Pressure Visibility

- [ ] The reader feels an opposing force before the early arc becomes routine.
- [ ] The pressure may be person, institution, environment, monster ecology, mystery, or consequence.
- [ ] The pressure escalates rather than remaining abstract for too many chapters.
- [ ] The book does not rely only on "Red is undefined" after that premise is already established.

# 11. Information-Dump Firewall — CRITICAL

- [ ] System/world information enters through action, conflict, procedure with consequences, dialogue, discovery, or environment.
- [ ] No early chapter stops to explain the world at length.
- [ ] Proper nouns are controlled.
- [ ] Repeated explanations of Red's anomaly are compressed.
- [ ] A second test/procedure must reveal a materially new consequence, not merely restate the first.

# 12. Early Pacing / Repetition Firewall — CRITICAL

- [ ] Consecutive opening chapters perform clearly different functions.
- [ ] Two quiet/procedural chapters in a row require escalating stakes, relationship tension, or a sharp new consequence.
- [ ] Repeated onboarding, registration, testing, explanation, or travel is compressed.
- [ ] The first major external conflict does not arrive so late that the opening feels like setup-only fiction.

# 13. Suspense and Reader Expectation — MANDATORY

Every opening chapter should create at least one forward expectation:
- What is Red?
- Why is he missing from the System?
- What can Play Logic actually do?
- What will happen when institutions cannot classify him?
- What local problem is he entering?
- Who benefits from the problem?
- What happens when Red collides with the world's intended Hero/story?

The next chapter should answer, complicate, or redirect at least one active expectation.

# 14. Chapter-Ending / Next-Click QA — MANDATORY

- [ ] Ending is intentional.
- [ ] Ending produces curiosity, consequence, decision, danger, reveal, relationship turn, or strong comic reversal.
- [ ] Ending does not rely on an artificial cut.
- [ ] Reader has a specific reason to click Next.
- [ ] Three consecutive chapters do not use the same kind of ending.

# 15. First-Five / First-Ten Promise Ladder

## By CH-005
The book should have delivered:
- Red's core voice;
- System anomaly;
- visible abnormal power;
- meaningful consequence of being undefined;
- recurring supporting-cast friction/chemistry;
- a concrete local objective/conflict.

## By CH-010
The book should have delivered:
- an escalating arc mystery or antagonist pressure;
- at least one strong action payoff beyond the opening;
- a clearer destination-level conflict;
- evidence the setting has its own life beyond Red;
- at least one expectation whose payoff creates a larger expectation.

# 16. Mobile Readability / Serial Rhythm

- [ ] Chapter lengths feel intentional rather than inflated.
- [ ] Long procedural scenes contain turns, reactions, and visible progress.
- [ ] Paragraphs are mobile-friendly.
- [ ] Each chapter contains one or more memorable beats.
- [ ] The reader is not asked to wait many chapters for the advertised premise to begin.



# 16A. Chapter-by-Chapter Engagement Continuity — CRITICAL

Every chapter in the commercial release must pass:

`qa/CHAPTER-ENGAGEMENT-QA-STANDARD.md`

The book must not rely only on strong CH-001/CH-005/climax action while allowing intervening chapters to become passive.

Each chapter needs either:
- combat/hostile action; or
- an equally strong confrontation, discovery, danger, countdown, risky task, failure, political collision, consequence, or irreversible choice.

A quiet chapter may pass.

An exposition-only or filler chapter may not.

# 17. Trend Awareness Without Copying

- [ ] Book uses recognizable market language/tropes where helpful.
- [ ] It still has a distinct identity.
- [ ] Trend-fit does not require copying plot, characters, scenes, dialogue, or branded identities.
- [ ] Public metadata sells the actual book rather than its inspirations.

# 18. Originality / Reference Safety — CRITICAL

- [ ] Public title, synopsis, cover copy, and chapter marketing do not present the story as a Deadpool, Marvel, Eminence of Shadow, or other franchise derivative.
- [ ] Red's voice, origin, powers, visual identity, and story engine remain independently developed.
- [ ] References remain transformed, brief, and unnecessary for comprehension.
- [ ] The book remains understandable to readers who do not know the inspirations.

# 19. Commercial-Readiness Decision

A package may receive:

**PASS — WEBNOVEL COMMERCIAL READINESS**
when no CRITICAL item is WEAK/FAIL and the title/synopsis/opening/first-three funnel all pass.

**PASS WITH TARGETED REVISION**
when the core premise and writing are viable but specific early-retention issues remain.

**FAIL — RETURN TO FRONT MATTER / EARLY CHAPTER REVISION**
when the public opening is plain, duplicative, exposition-heavy, slow, or fails to create a concrete next-click reason.

## Governing Rule

The goal is not to turn the story into a generic trend copy. The goal is to make the existing Red Jackal story reveal its strongest promise early, clearly, and repeatedly enough that a new reader has reasons to begin and continue.
