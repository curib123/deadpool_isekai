# 1. Chapter Purpose Lock

CH-004 must move Red Jackal from:

**being unable to use normal registered guild work**

to:

**knowingly accepting a useful but structurally bad private Road Guild arrangement because it gives him money, supplies, local access, and immediate freedom.**

The chapter must establish:

1. Rovan Hesk's first direct appearance;
2. Hesk as competent, calm, locally respected, and materially useful rather than obviously villainous;
3. the Greywake Chartered Road Guild's public economic importance;
4. the difference between a registered guild contract and privately arranged off-book labor;
5. the protections Red Jackal gives up by accepting the private arrangement;
6. Red Jackal's immediate material need for money, food, lodging, supplies, and local information;
7. Jessa Vale's procedural competence and cautious discomfort around irregular orders;
8. Captain Brynn Alder's brief independent warning without exposing Hesk's hidden scheme;
9. Red Jackal knowingly choosing the arrangement because it benefits him now;
10. the exact practical handoff into CH-005's first road assignment.

# 4. Language / Literacy Lock

Red Jackal still cannot read Veyran writing and does not permanently understand local speech.

The Chartered Road Guild maintains ordinary **Wayfarer Tongue Tokens** at its caravan/contract desks because it routinely deals with foreign and regional travelers.

A charged Guild token must support spoken negotiation when needed.

It does not:

- translate written contracts;
- grant literacy;
- reveal hidden intent;
- detect lies;
- create legal identity.

Because Red Jackal cannot read the written paperwork:

- Jessa must explain relevant terms aloud;
- route instructions must be given verbally and/or through obvious physical map markers/pictograms;
- Red Jackal cannot be treated as having knowingly read fine print;
- any accepted arrangement is based on spoken terms and private Guild records, not a normal identity-bound registered contract.

# 5. Payment / Contract Mechanism Lock

Valedorn/Avarran trade currency already supports:

- **Crown** — high-value coin;
- **Mark** — silver trade coin.

CH-004 should use **Marks** for ordinary local road-work payment unless later manuscript context requires otherwise.

Do **not** invent a full price table.

The chapter may establish purchasing power comparatively:

- meals;
- basic lodging;
- simple travel supplies.

Exact numerical pay may remain unstated unless the manuscript benefits from locking a rate.

The private arrangement is:

- paid by the Road Guild / Hesk's discretionary authority;
- recorded locally as private labor or an irregular service expense rather than a registered guild contract;
- not linked to a valid System identity;
- not eligible for ordinary registered-worker protections;
- not covered by standard guild injury guarantees, traveler guarantees, or formal contract appeal;
- usable by Hesk because Red Jackal's lack of registration makes the normal contract path unavailable.

This arrangement must be useful enough that Red Jackal has a rational reason to accept it.

It must also be bad enough that the later exploitation is causally earned.

# 6. Deadpan Voice Lock

CH-004 manuscript must follow the permanent series standard:

**dry/deadpan internal humor + restrained fourth-wall presence.**

The natural humor source is:

- Red Jackal negotiating money without knowing local prices;
- Hesk speaking in practical terms while Red Jackal notices what is missing;
- Jessa translating legal/administrative consequences into plain language;
- Brynn refusing to let Red Jackal mistake “private” for “protected.”

Do not turn the negotiation into a comedy routine.

Red Jackal can posture, but his internal narration should remain understated.

## SC-004-01 — The Roadmaster

**Approximate Time:** Same day as CH-003, later afternoon.  
**Location:** Greywake Chartered Road Guild hall, public contract/caravan desk.  
**Characters Present:** Red Jackal, Jessa Vale, Road Guild staff, Rovan Hesk enters during the scene.

### Scene Purpose

Introduce the Road Guild as a functioning public institution and Rovan Hesk as someone whose authority comes from actual frontier usefulness.

### Red Jackal's Immediate Goal

Find out whether Hesk's invitation can solve his immediate money problem without creating a new custody or registration problem.

### Opposing Goal

Hesk wants to assess whether Red Jackal is useful, manageable, and willing to work outside normal certification.

He does not reveal that he also values Red Jackal's lack of documentary protections.

### Conflict

Red Jackal expects an unusual offer because his situation is unusual.

The Guild treats the surrounding road business as ordinary work:
- caravan schedules;
- bridge maintenance;
- route postings;
- supply movement;
- escort certification.

Hesk's offer therefore enters through a credible working institution rather than a secret back room.

### Knowledge Entering Scene — Red Jackal

He knows Hesk's name and title only from Jessa's invitation.

He knows the Road Guild can potentially give him work when ordinary registration cannot.

### Supporting Character Knowledge

**Jessa knows:**
- Red Jackal's standard contract file cannot be opened;
- Hesk authorized the meeting;
- private expense/work records can be created without pretending Red Jackal has a valid registry identity;
- routine Guild procedure.

**Hesk knows:**
- the observable facts listed in the Chapter Knowledge Lock;
- Red Jackal needs access to work;
- he survived the north-road wilderness.

### What Red Jackal Discovers

The Guild is not merely paperwork.

Greywake visibly depends on it to:
- schedule caravans;
- maintain roads and bridges;
- assign escorts;
- move supplies;
- keep traffic flowing.

Hesk is treated by staff as an established authority, not an obvious criminal.

### Reader-Talk Opportunity

Optional and restrained.

Red Jackal may quietly note that the man offering irregular work appears to be one of the most legitimate people in the building.

### Self-Talk Opportunity

He assesses:
- how much authority Hesk has;
- whether the invitation looks like a trap;
- whether walking away leaves him with any practical alternative.

### Emotional State

Alert, curious, materially motivated.

### Aura-Farming Opportunity

Red Jackal presents himself as though Hesk requested a specialist rather than an undocumented stranger who needs money.

Hesk does not challenge the framing because it costs him nothing.

### Comedy Opportunity

Deadpan only: Red Jackal notices that confidence is easier when nobody asks for a price yet.

### Dialogue Purpose

- establish Hesk's public role;
- establish why the meeting exists;
- establish that Red Jackal cannot enter ordinary guild certification;
- allow Hesk to begin evaluating him.

### System Interaction

None.

No new appraisal.

No attempt to register Red Jackal.

### Play Logic Restrictions

No attempt, manifestation, demonstration, or discussion of mechanics.

Hesk does not know about the CH-001 manifestation.

### Regeneration Continuity

No regeneration test or discussion.

Hesk may know Red Jackal survived the north-road wilderness but cannot infer a healing ability.

### Worldbuilding Introduced Naturally

- Road Guild hall;
- public contract/caravan desks;
- bridge/road schedules;
- escort certification;
- Greywake's economic dependence on road traffic.

### Continuity Dependencies

Direct continuation of Hesk's invitation at the end of CH-003.

### Knowledge Restrictions

Do not reveal:
- march-lures;
- falsified casualties;
- outer-sacrifice policy;
- Hesk's scapegoat plan.

### Mystery / Foreshadowing Role

Antagonist setup only.

## SC-004-02 — The Offer

**Approximate Time:** Minutes later.  
**Location:** Road Guild hall, Roadmaster's working office / private contract table.  
**Characters Present:** Red Jackal, Rovan Hesk, Jessa Vale.

### Scene Purpose

Define the private work arrangement and make its usefulness immediately obvious.

### Red Jackal's Immediate Goal

Secure:
- paid work;
- enough immediate resources to eat and sleep somewhere;
- access to local supplies;
- useful knowledge of Greywake's roads;
- freedom from further identity testing.

### Opposing Goal

Hesk wants a capable worker he can deploy without normal certification constraints or Guild liability.

He tests Red Jackal without openly treating him as disposable.

### The Offer

Hesk proposes privately arranged provisional road work under his discretionary Guild authority.

The first assignment is preparation/support for a **main-road repair and supply movement** that will lead directly into CH-005's damaged bridge/worksite.

The assignment may include:
- travel with or ahead of a small repair/supply crew;
- basic protection of workers and materials;
- assistance at a damaged road/bridge site;
- returning with the crew or reporting completion.

Do not begin the actual assignment in CH-004.

### What Hesk Offers

- payment in ordinary local Marks;
- access to basic job supplies;
- route information necessary for the assignment;
- the possibility of additional private work if Red Jackal proves useful;
- no further identity test as a condition of this particular arrangement.

A small advance or supply allowance may be offered if needed so Red Jackal can function before first payment.

### What Hesk Does Not Offer

- normal guild certification;
- registered-worker legal status;
- guild injury guarantee;
- identity-bound traveler guarantee;
- formal contract appeal;
- automatic compensation if the job goes wrong;
- institutional responsibility equivalent to a registered worker.

### Conflict

The deal solves exactly the problems Red Jackal currently cares about while shifting almost all risk onto him.

### Knowledge Entering Scene — Red Jackal

He knows he needs money and cannot access normal guild jobs.

He does not yet understand the practical value of a Mark.

### Supporting Character Knowledge

Jessa understands exactly which normal protections are missing.

Hesk understands that those missing protections are useful to him.

Neither may reveal hidden conspiracy truth.

### What Red Jackal Discovers

“Off the books” does not mean invisible.

The Guild can still record:
- that money was disbursed;
- that supplies were issued;
- that a private service occurred.

What is missing is the registered identity-bound contract structure that would protect him.

### Reader-Talk Opportunity

Optional: Red Jackal may acknowledge that “no paperwork” was not actually what he received; he received different paperwork he could not read.

### Self-Talk Opportunity

He recognizes the bad terms but compares them against:
- no income;
- no local purchasing knowledge;
- no registered alternatives.

### Emotional State

Interested, skeptical, increasingly pragmatic.

### Aura-Farming Opportunity

Red Jackal treats Hesk's invitation as if he is deciding whether the Guild deserves his time.

Hesk lets the posture stand and returns to practical terms.

### Comedy Opportunity

Pay negotiation begins before Red Jackal knows what the amount can buy.

Keep it understated.

### Dialogue Purpose

- establish assignment category;
- establish private/off-book mechanism;
- establish payment form;
- expose missing protections;
- show Hesk's calm practicality.

### System Interaction

None.

### Play Logic Restrictions

None.

### Regeneration Continuity

None.

### Worldbuilding Introduced Naturally

Difference between:
- certified guild work;
- registered contract protection;
- discretionary/private labor;
- Guild supply issuance.

### Continuity Dependencies

Red Jackal's Unverified Entrant status remains active.

### Knowledge Restrictions

Hesk must not indicate that he already intends to scapegoat Red Jackal.

The assignment must sound genuinely useful and plausible.

### Mystery / Foreshadowing Role

No F-ID.

This is antagonist-method foreshadowing through labor structure, not a mystery clue.

### What Changes

### Exit Condition

He refuses to agree until someone explains what the payment and missing protections mean in practical terms.

### Conflict

- whether there is an advance/supply allowance;
- what the Guild supplies;

- basic lodging;

## SC-004-04 — Private Does Not Mean Protected

**Approximate Time:** Late afternoon.  
**Location:** Edge of Greywake market / Road Guild caravan yard.  
**Characters Present:** Red Jackal, Captain Brynn Alder, Rovan Hesk briefly, Jessa Vale.

### Scene Purpose

Use Brynn as an independent civic check so Red Jackal cannot later claim he did not understand the institutional risk.

### Red Jackal's Immediate Goal

Finish the arrangement and move on to food, lodging, and preparation.

### Opposing Goal

Brynn wants to ensure:
- the Guild is not quietly treating temporary admission as full certification;
- Red Jackal understands that private Guild work does not create normal worker protections;
- the militia is not being made responsible for an arrangement it did not authorize.

Hesk wants the matter treated as ordinary discretionary labor rather than a political argument.

### Conflict

Brynn does not forbid the work.

She makes the missing protections explicit.

Hesk remains calm and technically correct:
Red Jackal is choosing private work because registered work is unavailable.

### Knowledge Entering Scene — Red Jackal

He already understands the broad risk.

Brynn confirms that the risk is institutionally real, not merely contract wording.

### Supporting Character Knowledge

**Brynn knows:**
- Red Jackal remains an Unverified Entrant;
- normal guild certification is unavailable;
- Hesk is offering private work;
- the militia does not control ordinary Road Guild hiring.

She does not know the march-lure conspiracy.

**Hesk knows:**
- Brynn distrusts irregular arrangements;
- she lacks grounds to forbid a voluntary private job.

### What Red Jackal Discovers

No institution is going to rescue him from the choice.

If he accepts:
- he gets resources and access;
- he owns more of the risk.

### Self-Talk Opportunity

He recognizes that this is exactly the sort of freedom he claims to want.

The inconvenient part is that freedom does not come with insurance.

### Reader-Talk Opportunity

Optional and very brief.

### Emotional State

Resolved rather than pressured.

### Aura-Farming Opportunity

Red Jackal accepts Brynn's warning as though he had already accounted for every risk.

Brynn does not argue.

### Comedy Opportunity

Dry only: the person least protected by paperwork is receiving unusually thorough explanations of paperwork.

### Dialogue Purpose

- preserve Brynn's independent civic role;
- preserve Hesk/Brynn tension;
- make Red Jackal's acceptance informed;
- avoid making Brynn omniscient.

### System Interaction

None.

### Play Logic Restrictions

None.

### Regeneration Continuity

None.

### Worldbuilding Introduced Naturally

- private labor does not transfer liability to Greywake militia.

### Continuity Dependencies

Brynn previously admitted Red Jackal as an Unverified Entrant.

### Knowledge Restrictions

Brynn cannot accuse Hesk of the hidden migration policy.

Hesk cannot reveal it.

### Mystery / Foreshadowing Role

No F-ID.
