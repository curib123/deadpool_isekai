Its job is no longer to prove what Hesk did.

**what happens if the system is stopped before anyone understands what years of manipulation have done to the migration?**

4. give Hesk his strongest credible argument without absolving him;

10. make Brynn confront competing public-safety obligations rather than choosing a simple side;

15. make Tavian insist the migration geometry must be mapped before any lure is altered;

18. keep exact backflow behavior uncertain;

# 3. Hesk Sympathy Boundary

CH-014 is the chapter where Hesk becomes most understandable.

That does **not** mean he becomes correct.

The manuscript may reveal:

- eleven years earlier, an early-winter migration and road collapse isolated Greywake;
- relief arrived late;
- caravans vanished;
- outer farms failed;
- people froze or starved;
- distant officials debated whether Greywake's route was still economically worth maintaining;
- Hesk helped reopen the road;
- he concluded that someone on the frontier must make survival decisions when distant authorities will not;
- he believes the Main Caravan Road is Greywake's lifeline;
- he believes losing reliable trade can kill more people than localized frontier losses;
- he has been seeking stronger long-term Valedorn charter support, funding, and defensive support.

His worldview may be expressed in substance:

**A frontier does not survive by saving everyone. It survives by deciding what cannot be allowed to die.**

The chapter must preserve the sympathy boundary:

- Hesk genuinely protects Greywake;
- Hesk does not enjoy killing people;
- Hesk knowingly accepted sacrificing weaker communities;
- he concealed the policy and distorted reporting;
- understandable fear does not erase agency or responsibility;
- the chapter must not imply that secrecy and victim selection were the only possible choices.

# 6. Network / Ecological Infrastructure Lock

CH-013 proved coordinated diversion.

CH-014 may now establish the operational danger of treating the lure system as isolated switches.

Writer truth already establishes:

- years of manipulation have compressed predator and prey routes into an abnormal corridor;
- the network is beginning to fail;
- removing/disabling part of it can release accumulated pressure toward previously protected corridors.

Character-level CH-014 knowledge may reach:

- the network has operated long enough to reshape current movement patterns;
- animals now route around repeated pressure zones;
- several species' paths overlap abnormally;
- different lure sectors are interacting indirectly through animal movement;
- removing one influence point may not restore a natural route;
- instead, pressure may seek another available corridor.

Preferred term:
**backflow risk**

Meaning in CH-014:

**a sudden change at one lure can cause displaced migration pressure to rebound or spill into routes that were previously screened.**

Do not establish:

- exact route a backflow will take;
- exact timing;
- exact species response;

- complete network geometry.
