# Native Story Framework

**Status:** LOCKED SERIES FRAMEWORK / DEPLOYMENT TIMING PROVISIONAL  
**Purpose:** Establish the real Hero/Villain story that exists independently of Red Jackal.

# 1. Core Rule

Veyr has its own protagonist-shaped conflict.

Red Jackal is not its chosen hero.

The native storyline continues when Red is absent.

# 2. HERO-001 — The Registered Otherworld Hero

**Working Name:** Adrian Hale  
**Origin:** Earth  
**Status:** PROVISIONAL NAME / LOCKED ROLE  
**System State:** fully recognized after legitimate summoning/transfer  
**Narrative Function:** the actual conventional isekai hero contrast to Red

Adrian retains:
- coherent Earth memory;
- a registered name;
- a race;
- a Hero-class path;
- measurable level/progression;
- System-recognized skills;
- a Fate-supported quest;
- an official reason to be in Veyr.

He is everything an institution expects an otherworld hero to be.

He is not a parody and not incompetent.

He can be genuinely brave, skilled, conflicted, and important.

Red's existence does not make Adrian fake.

The comedy comes from contrast:
- Adrian has the quest;
- Red has no quest;
- Adrian has a Hero Party;
- Red keeps walking into their set pieces;
- Adrian levels;
- Red cannot;
- Adrian is explainable;
- Red breaks the explanation.

# 3. Hero Party

The Hero Party must have independent relationships that do not revolve around Red.

Locked role slots:
- HERO-001 — registered otherworld Hero;
- PARTY-001 — native martial protector / tactical authority;
- PARTY-002 — native mage/scholar / world-lore authority;
- PARTY-003 — healer/faith or support specialist with independent beliefs;
- PARTY-004 — scout/rogue/diplomatic specialist who notices narrative inconsistencies first.

Names and exact national origins may be finalized through Character/World QA without changing these roles.

The party may:
- admire Red;
- hate him;
- distrust him;
- find him funny;
- mistake him for a villain;
- mistake him for a secret master;
- become tired of arriving after he has already disrupted their target.

No universal reaction is allowed.

# 4. MAIN-VILLAIN-001 — The Crownless Sovereign

**Working Title:** The Crownless Sovereign  
**True Name:** PROVISIONAL / later Character QA  
**Status:** LOCKED ROLE / IDENTITY DETAILS PROVISIONAL  
**Narrative Function:** principal antagonist of the native Hero storyline

The Main Villain:
- has a real campaign that would exist without Red;
- is opposed by the Hero for reasons unrelated to Red;
- commands or influences some minor villains but not every local antagonist;
- can threaten nations, systems, people, secrets, or world order;
- does not need to exceed Red in raw power;
- may initially mistake Red for a lieutenant, rival villain, hidden god, secret final boss, or irrelevant lunatic;
- eventually realizes Red is not part of the expected board.

The villain's danger comes from:
- scale;
- planning;
- distributed objectives;
- manipulation;
- timing;
- things happening where Red is not;
- consequences that cannot be punched backward in time.

# 5. Minor Villain Rule

Use three categories:

1. **Native Main-Plot Villains** — connected to MAIN-VILLAIN-001.
2. **Destination Villains** — local antagonists such as Hesk or Cassian who may have no connection to the main villain.
3. **Independent Threats** — criminals, monsters, political rivals, cults, opportunists, or anomalies with their own motives.

Do not force every bad event into one conspiracy.

# 6. Red Intrusion Patterns

Preferred recurring patterns:

- Red reaches a crisis before the Hero Party and accidentally takes the dramatic position.
- The Hero Party discovers a defeated villain who insists the “real monster” was someone else.
- Red mistakes a serious prophecy scene for theater and comments during a Fourth-Wall Pause.
- A minor villain prepares specifically for the Hero and has no plan for Red.
- Red lets a villain perform because the speech is entertaining, causing the Hero Party to think Red is allied with them.
- Red steals an entrance, then leaves before anyone understands why he was there.
- Adrian attempts a conventional heroic solution while Red solves a completely different problem.
- Red deliberately refuses to finish the Hero's opponent because “that one isn't my boss fight.”
- Red finishes the opponent anyway because they annoyed him.
- The Hero Party's quest survives, but its next step changes because Red moved a piece nobody expected.

# 6A. The Scene-Thief Contract

Red Jackal may repeatedly create a **false-main-character effect**.

The native scene belongs causally to Adrian, the Hero Party, the Main Villain, a destination cast, or another local actor.

Red enters.

For a few beats, everything may visually and socially reorganize around him because:
- his entrance is absurdly strong;
- his reputation precedes him;
- he behaves as though the scene was waiting for him;
- Play Logic creates impossible stage control;
- enemies fixate on the unknown anomaly;
- witnesses misunderstand his role.

Then planning must remember who actually had the original objective.

The pleasure is not replacing the native protagonist.

The pleasure is watching an uninvited anomaly **steal the scene and damage the script**.

# 6B. Independent-Story Minimum

For every major volume, planning must maintain at least one meaningful development that:
- happens without Red present;
- matters to the Hero/Villain campaign or another independent character line;
- can later collide with Red.

This prevents Veyr from feeling frozen whenever Red leaves the page.

# 7. Sequence Preservation

The Hero storyline is allowed to be disrupted.

It is not allowed to disappear without causal replacement.

Whenever Red breaks an expected beat, planning must answer:

1. What objective still exists?
2. Who notices the interference?
3. What does the villain change?
4. What does the Hero Party now have to do differently?
5. What consequence becomes the next beat?
6. Did Red accidentally become more important to somebody else's story than he intended?

# 8. Current Arc Placement

Greywake and Merrowgate retain their locked destination plots.

The Hero Party does **not** need to physically enter either current arc.

Their story may progress off-screen in parallel.

References, rumors, aftermath, or later callbacks may establish that another major story was moving while Red dealt with Greywake and Merrowgate.

This preserves current pacing and avoids retroactively forcing the Hero Party into completed chapter structures.
