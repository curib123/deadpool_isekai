# 2. Series Engine

Luck Everhart is a self-authored mysterious traveler whose only supernatural ability is passive Fortune Distortion.

Series progression is measured through:
- knowledge of Veyr;
- ordinary competence;
- relationships;
- reputation;
- legal/social access;
- carried injury and resources;
- accumulated evidence;
- consequences of past choices;
- how native storylines reroute around him.

It is not measured through:
- levels;
- power stages;
- awakened forms;
- new supernatural abilities;
- conscious probability control.

# 4. Greywake Arc — Structural Outcome

Greywake must remain a complete local story.

Core progression:
1. Luck arrives without usable identity.
2. He chooses the name Luck Everhart after surviving the forest.
3. Greywake cannot classify him.
4. He accepts off-book Road Guild work because he needs money and freedom.
5. Main-road competence contrasts with neglected outer routes.
6. Tavian identifies abnormal migration.
7. Maelis finds documentary contradictions.
8. Hidden lure infrastructure proves deliberate intervention.
9. Hesk's sacrifice logic becomes explicit.
10. Luck's impatient intervention contributes to backflow.
11. Consequences force a third option rather than a clean moral answer.
12. Greywake survives through evacuation, engineering, route work, evidence, militia coordination, and lucky openings.
13. Hesk's policy is exposed.
14. Luck leaves with injury, reputation, money pressure, and unresolved identity.

Greywake's question:
**Who gets counted when survival becomes logistics?**

# 5. Merrowgate Arc — Structural Outcome

Merrowgate is not a second Greywake.

It expands the story into:
- port law;
- contracts;
- cargo;
- insurance/assurance;
- debt;
- warehouses;
- cranes and bridges;
- private security;
- rumor as commercial information.

Core progression:
1. CH027 brings Luck to Merrowgate through ordinary travel and cost.
2. Merrowgate independently fails to classify him.
3. Reputation from Greywake arrives before he fully understands it.
4. Luck finds irregular work because his missing registry identity is commercially useful.
5. Cassian Venn's public legitimacy is established before guilt is proven.
6. Luck knowingly accepts an off-manifest job because it pays well and flatters his exemption from rules.
7. Later sabotage reveals that his choice helped move altered equipment.
8. Investigation follows money, contracts, technical evidence, and political beneficiaries.
9. A deadly infrastructure failure makes Luck a useful public suspect.
10. He can leave but chooses to remain because disappearing would shift his consequence onto others and strengthen Cassian's narrative.
11. Iria, Samir, Nessa, Dalen, workers, and Luck build a coalition where each owns a necessary function.
12. The climax combines harbor survival with evidence preservation.
13. Cassian's network is exposed without pretending all port problems vanish.
14. Merrowgate creates a local witnessed-contract workaround for Luck's chosen name without granting System identity.
15. Luck leaves with changed reputation and practical access, still unaware of Fortune Distortion.

Merrowgate's question:
**Who owns a free port when control can be purchased through fear?**

# 6. Reputation Roadmap

Luck's reputation grows through consequence, not power tiers.

- Greywake opening: unknown outsider.
- Early Greywake: suspiciously capable.
- Mid Greywake: conflicting local theories.
- Greywake climax: locally notorious.
- Merrowgate arrival: rumors precede him.
- Merrowgate midpoint: his name becomes commercially and politically useful.
- Merrowgate reversal: he becomes both suspect and asset.
- Merrowgate aftermath: competing legends exist simultaneously.

No single rumor becomes omniscient truth.

# 7. Native Story Roadmap

Veyr's registered Hero, Hero Party, Main Villain, wars, prophecies, and large-scale conflicts continue independently.

Luck may:
- arrive before or after an expected Hero beat;
- accidentally alter staging;
- attract the wrong antagonist's attention;
- cause a local faction to misidentify him;
- force native actors to adapt.

He does not replace the Hero's System role.
