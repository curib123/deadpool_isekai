# Power-Model Retcon Override

The authorial retcon in `planning/RED-JACKAL-OVERPOWERED-RETCON.md` supersedes all Stage I / progression-ceiling language in this architecture.

Volume 1 now treats Red as already overwhelmingly powerful.

Any prior statement that he “cannot” solve a physical problem because of raw power must be reinterpreted or revised as one of:
- voluntary restraint;
- lack of information;
- simultaneous objectives;
- concern for collateral;
- social/political/evidentiary constraints;
- Red playing with the situation too long.

Supporting characters remain necessary for knowledge, logistics, evidence, evacuation, judgment, and local authority—not because Red lacks enough force.

# 1. Volume Purpose

Greywake Arc introduces Red Jackal, Veyr's everyday frontier reality, the World System's inability to define him, his deliberately under-revealed unbounded Play Logic, abnormal self-restoration, and the consequence difference between someone overwhelmingly hard to stop and everyone around him who is not.

The volume's local story must stand on its own.

The arc is **not** secretly about Savael, the Exterior Needle, the Eidrathi, the Great Design, or the final cosmology.

Its primary question is local and human:

**How much of the frontier is Greywake willing to sacrifice in order to keep Greywake alive?**

Red Jackal's personal version of that question is:

**What happens when someone decides his usefulness gives them the right to assign him a role?**

# 2. Opening State

## 2.1 Red Jackal's Arrival Situation

Red Jackal physically manifests in the Hollow March wilderness within roughly one day's dangerous travel of Greywake.

He arrives:
- with Earth memories substantially intact;
- without a valid World System identity;
- with an unfamiliar but physically functional Veyran embodiment;
- aware that his body behaves strangely;
- only beginning to understand that imagination can affect reality;
- without local money, legal identity, guild registration, social standing, or reliable knowledge of Veyr.

The exact reason his translocation resolves near Greywake is **not explained or investigated in Volume 1**.

His first hours establish practical survival pressure rather than cosmological exposition.

A monster encounter and the road network naturally point him toward the nearest fortified settlement: Greywake.

## 2.2 First Contact With Greywake

Greywake's gate, guild, or civic intake procedures attempt ordinary identification.

The result is not hidden data.

It is failure:
- Name: UNDEFINED
- Race: UNDEFINED
- Class: UNDEFINED
- Level: UNDEFINED
- Progression: UNAVAILABLE
- Registry: NO RECORD

The settlement cannot process him normally for guild work, travel registration, lodging guarantees, or standard frontier liability.

He is not immediately declared evil.

He is treated as:
- suspicious;
- administratively impossible;
- potentially dangerous;
- potentially useful.

This tension drives his first relationship with Greywake.

# 3. Why Red Jackal Becomes Involved

Red Jackal does not begin by deciding to save Greywake.

He becomes involved for mixed, self-centered reasons:

1. he needs food, equipment, local knowledge, money, and freedom of movement;
2. he resents attempts to detain or classify him;
3. dangerous frontier work sounds more interesting than waiting for officials;
4. proving useful lets him control the terms of his presence;
5. he enjoys the opportunity to make an impression;
6. he becomes personally offended when he learns someone has been treating him and other people as expendable pieces.

The Greywake Chartered Road Guild offers him **off-book provisional work** precisely because it can exploit an unregistered person without placing him in normal guild records.

Red Jackal initially accepts because the arrangement benefits him.

He later understands that the same lack of identity he uses to escape rules also makes it easy for institutions to deny him protections.

# 4. Exact Local Conflict

Greywake depends economically on one reliable caravan road and associated river access.

Seasonal monster migrations through the Hollow March normally make the northern approaches dangerous but survivable.

Over several years, the migration pattern has shifted.

The shift is **not natural**.

The Greywake Chartered Road Guild has secretly installed and maintained a chain of illegal **march-lures**: alchemical/magical scent-and-mana stakes originally designed for short-term predator diversion.

The Guild has been using them at unsafe strength and duration to push monster movement away from:
- the main caravan road;
- Greywake's core walls;
- profitable supply depots.

The diverted migration increasingly crosses:
- outer homesteads;
- minor roads;
- unchartered camps;
- poorer settlements with less political influence.

The policy has kept Greywake commercially alive.

It has also caused deaths that the Guild classifies as ordinary frontier losses.

The current season has become unstable because years of manipulation have compressed multiple predator and prey routes into one abnormal migration corridor.

The march-lure network is beginning to fail.

If nothing changes, the resulting surge can hit both the outer communities **and** Greywake.

# 5. Main Antagonist

## ANTAG-GW-001 — Roadmaster Rovan Hesk

**Species:** Human  
**Age:** 48  
**Origin:** Greywake / Hollow March  
**Affiliation:** Greywake Chartered Road Guild  
**Position:** Roadmaster and voting member of Greywake's settlement council  
**Status:** Volume 1 local antagonist

## 5.1 History

Rovan Hesk was born in Greywake and spent most of his life on frontier roads.

Eleven years before the current story, an early-winter migration and road collapse isolated Greywake.

Relief arrived late.

Caravans vanished.

Outer farms failed.

People froze or starved while central officials argued over whether the route remained economically worth maintaining.

Hesk survived and helped reopen the road.

He concluded that Greywake survives only when someone is willing to make decisions distant authorities refuse to make.

He eventually rose through the Chartered Road Guild and gained a council seat.

## 5.2 Worldview

**A frontier does not survive by saving everyone. It survives by deciding what cannot be allowed to die.**

Hesk believes:
- the main road is Greywake's lifeline;
- if the road dies, the settlement dies;
- outer homesteads are replaceable;
- trade infrastructure is not;
- central authorities will abandon Greywake if losses make the route look unprofitable;
- moral purity is a luxury purchased by people who do not live on the frontier.

He does not enjoy killing people.

He has accepted sacrificing them.

## 5.3 Goal

Keep the main caravan road open long enough to secure a stronger long-term Valedorn road charter, funding, and permanent defensive support for Greywake.

## 5.4 Methods

Hesk uses:
- illegal high-strength march-lures;
- falsified road-loss reports;
- selective guild contracts;
- bribes and favors;
- control of route information;
- expendable unregistered labor;
- political pressure on the settlement council.

## 5.5 Resources

- Road Guild personnel;
- route maps;
- storehouses;
- lure compounds;
- wagons and draft animals;
- hired guards;
- council influence;
- knowledge of old roads and monster movement.

## 5.6 What He Protects

Hesk genuinely protects:
- Greywake's main road;
- core food supply;
- trade access;
- families inside the central settlement;
- the settlement's long-term economic viability.

## 5.7 What He Sacrifices

He is willing to sacrifice:
- outer homesteads;
- unregistered travelers;
- politically weak camps;
- inconvenient evidence;
- people he believes Greywake cannot afford to prioritize.

## 5.8 Weaknesses

- overconfidence in controlled systems;
- decades of tunnel vision;
- assumes every crisis is ultimately logistical;
- underestimates people he cannot place inside a ledger;
- has convinced himself that because his objective is survival, his choices are automatically necessary.

## 5.8A Antagonist Independence

Hesk's march-lure policy predates Red Jackal by years.

If Red Jackal never arrived:
- Hesk would still manipulate the migration;
- Maelis's audit could still threaten the records;
- Tavian could still identify unnatural route behavior;
- the compressed migration would still become unstable;
- outer homesteads would still face deliberate sacrifice.

Red Jackal changes how the crisis resolves; he does not create the antagonist's motive, plan, or local conflict.

## 5.9 Relationship to Red Jackal

Hesk initially sees Red Jackal as ideal off-book labor:
- powerful;
- undocumented;
- disposable on paper;
- useful for dangerous tasks;
- easy to blame if something goes wrong.

Red Jackal initially sees Hesk as a practical bastard whose deal is useful.

Their conflict becomes personal when Red Jackal realizes Hesk has silently assigned him the same category used for outer settlements:

**acceptable loss.**

## 5.10 Win Condition

Hesk wins if:
1. the core road remains open;
2. the largest migration surge is diverted into outer territory;
3. evidence linking the Road Guild to the lures is destroyed or discredited;
4. Greywake's official losses remain low enough to secure the new road charter;
5. Red Jackal can be blamed as an anomalous disruption if necessary.

## 5.11 Consequence If Victorious

Greywake survives economically in the short term.

Several outer communities are destroyed or abandoned.

The Guild gains more authority.

The lie becomes institutional policy.

## 5.12 Consequence If Defeated

The manipulation becomes public.

Greywake must survive the migration without sacrificing designated communities.

The Road Guild loses political legitimacy.

The settlement must accept a less profitable but more honest reconstruction of its frontier routes.

## 5.13 Sympathy Boundary

Hesk's fear and logic are understandable.

His deliberate concealment of deaths, repeated sacrifice of weak communities, and willingness to use unregistered people as disposable assets cross the line into antagonism.

# 6. Secondary Opposing Force — The Migration Surge

The monster migration is not evil.

It is an ecological force made dangerous by years of human manipulation.

The surge includes multiple species rather than one single “boss monster.”

The exact species mix may be refined at Chapter Roadmap stage using the Bestiary, but the architecture requires:
- prey animals driven off normal routes;
- pack predators following them;
- at least one larger territorial predator displaced into human travel zones;
- escalating environmental damage as the migration compresses.

The climax cannot be solved only by killing one monster.

The route itself must be changed.

# 11. Monster and Wilderness Architecture

The arc uses ecology rather than random encounters.

Required threat layers:

## Layer 1 — Opportunistic Predators

Small/medium threats establish frontier danger.

Purpose:
- teach combat;
- demonstrate Stage I Play Logic;
- show that Red Jackal can survive injuries ordinary people cannot.

## Layer 2 — Displaced Packs

Predators appear where experienced locals say they should not.

Purpose:
- investigation clue;
- Tavian's expertise;
- prove migration distortion.

## Layer 3 — Large Territorial Predator

A major creature is driven toward roads or homesteads by compressed migration.

Purpose:
- substantial action set piece;
- demonstrate that killing one strong creature does not solve the ecological problem.

## Layer 4 — Full Migration Surge

Multiple species and panicked movement become the climax's environmental threat.

Purpose:
- force cooperation;
- make logistics and terrain matter;
- prevent the final solution from being “Red Jackal punches the boss harder.”

# 12. Action Escalation

The volume escalates through **type**, not just stronger enemies.

1. individual survival danger;
2. frontier job danger;
3. route/patrol encounters;
4. evidence-gathering under threat;
5. major territorial predator;
6. multiple communities at risk;
7. full migration surge plus human sabotage/political interference.

Each step increases consequences for people other than Red Jackal.

# 13. Stage I Play Logic Plan

Red Jackal remains at **Stage I — Improvised Manifestation**.

Approved demonstration categories:

## PL-1 — Immediate Improvised Tool

A short-lived object solves a concrete frontier problem:
- brace;
- wedge;
- hook;
- cutting tool;
- temporary ladder/step;
- simple shield-like object.

Purpose:
Establish that imagination can produce useful physical answers.

## PL-2 — Absurd Combat Object

A visually excessive blunt or defensive object appears during danger.

Purpose:
- establish personality;
- create comedy if the situation supports it;
- demonstrate that the effect is real but unstable.

It must not erase the whole encounter.

## PL-3 — Improvised Terrain Assistance

A temporary physical construct helps cross, block, redirect, or stabilize a small environmental obstacle.

Purpose:
Foreshadow creative problem-solving needed later.

## PL-4 — Climax Utility, Not Solo Victory

During the migration crisis, Play Logic contributes one temporary component to the group solution:
- reinforcing a failing structure;
- creating a temporary barrier/guide;
- buying time;
- redirecting a small part of movement.

The entire migration cannot be rewritten or deleted by imagination.

# 14. Regeneration Architecture

Volume 1 must establish four truths through observation:

1. Red Jackal can recover from injuries that would incapacitate normal people.
2. Regeneration hurts.
3. Repeated damage and exhaustion reduce his precision and judgment.
4. Regeneration does not protect anyone standing beside him.

## Consequence Rule

At least one major turning point must force Red Jackal to understand that choosing a reckless solution because **he** can survive can injure someone who cannot.

This is character development, not a moral conversion.

# 15. Aura-Farming Architecture

Approved opportunities include:

- deliberately theatrical arrival into a tense social situation;
- unnecessary mystery when a straightforward answer would work;
- dramatic positioning before a monster confrontation;
- pretending a failed/accidental manifestation was intentional;
- using a visually excessive object because the practical version would be boring;
- attempting a cool exit or line that is interrupted by frontier reality.

Required balance:

- at least one aura-farming attempt succeeds strongly;
- at least one receives little reaction;
- at least one backfires or becomes embarrassing;
- aura farming must never be the reason civilians die.

Exact instances belong to Chapter Roadmap.

Comedy sources:

# 17. Character Architecture

Treats Veyr as dangerous, strange, and exciting.

Uses performance to maintain control.

# 18. Volume Movement Architecture

These are **movements**, not chapters.

## Movement I — The Undefined Stranger

Functions:
- Red Jackal arrives in Hollow March;
- reaches Greywake;
- System identity failure becomes immediate social problem;
- Hesk offers off-book frontier work;
- Red Jackal accepts for practical/selfish reasons;
- Maelis and Tavian enter through their independent assignments.

Tone:
adventure, confusion, danger, comedy, curiosity.

Mystery ceiling:
M-002 surface only.

## Movement II — Frontier Work

Functions:
- Greywake culture and local factions become concrete;
- Red Jackal takes dangerous jobs/patrols;
- Stage I Play Logic and regeneration are demonstrated;
- monster routes feel increasingly wrong;
- outer communities report disproportionate danger;
- Maelis finds record inconsistencies;
- Tavian identifies ecological inconsistencies.

Tone:
adventure-forward with increasing unease.

## Movement III — The Road Is Lying

Functions:
- lure evidence is discovered;
- Guild route choices become suspicious;
- Red Jackal learns his off-book jobs overlap the manipulated danger zones;
- outer-homestead losses become personal rather than statistical;
- Hesk's policy becomes visible without yet exposing every detail.

### Midpoint Shift

The story changes from:

**“Greywake is suffering a dangerous migration.”**

to:

**“Someone in Greywake has been choosing where the migration kills people.”**

This is the locked midpoint revelation.

## Movement IV — Necessary Sacrifices

Functions:
- Hesk's history and rationale become clear;
- council politics fracture;
- evidence threatens Greywake's road charter;
- Red Jackal is pressured to accept that Hesk's method “works”;
- one lure site becomes the immediate focus.

### Red Jackal Error

Red Jackal, acting from anger, confidence, or impatience, disrupts part of the lure network before the full migration geometry is understood.

The act is not stupid.

It is understandable and insufficiently responsible.

### Major Reversal

Removing/disabling part of the lure network releases accumulated pressure.

The migration begins redirecting toward Greywake itself.

Hesk can now argue, with evidence, that exposing his scheme has put the entire settlement at risk.

At least one non-Red-Jackal character suffers a real consequence from the worsening crisis.

No death is structurally required, but the consequence cannot be comedic or immediately erased.

## Movement V — No Acceptable Loss

Functions:
- Greywake prepares for impact;
- outer homesteads cannot simply be abandoned;
- Hesk attempts to restore the sacrificial route;
- Red Jackal rejects becoming Hesk's disposable anomaly;
- Maelis makes the documentary truth actionable;
- Tavian develops the viable migration redirection plan;
- militia, homesteaders, caravans, and available workers must cooperate.

The solution requires:
- terrain;
- road infrastructure;
- militia discipline;
- monster behavior knowledge;
- temporary barriers;
- evacuation;
- Red Jackal's improvisation.

## Movement VI — Greywake Holds

### Climax

The full migration surge reaches the Greywake corridor.

The climax has two simultaneous conflicts:

1. **Physical:** redirect the migration without sacrificing the outer homesteads or allowing the core settlement to collapse.
2. **Human:** prevent Hesk from reactivating the final sacrificial lure plan and destroying/controlling the evidence.

Red Jackal confronts Hesk, but defeating Hesk physically is not sufficient.

The migration solution succeeds because multiple people contribute competencies Red Jackal does not have.

Red Jackal deliberately uses only a bounded-looking portion of Play Logic while the local plan unfolds. He could apply vastly greater force, but doing so would not automatically solve evacuation, evidence, route choice, or the consequences already in motion.

### Serious Red Jackal Beat

A real consequence caused by his earlier recklessness or Hesk's attempt to treat people as expendable removes the performative layer temporarily.

The seriousness is emotional, not an automatic power boost.

## Movement VII — What Greywake Counts

Functions:
- immediate aftermath;
- casualties and damage remain real;
- Hesk's manipulation becomes publicly undeniable;
- Road Guild authority is stripped/restructured;
- outer communities gain formal representation or enforceable warning rights;
- Maelis's audit preserves the evidence;
- Greywake's road remains damaged and less profitable;
- the settlement survives without proving Hesk completely wrong about the difficulty of frontier survival.

Red Jackal is not crowned a hero.

Public opinion remains mixed:
- some grateful;
- some frightened;
- some angry;
- some fascinated;
- some convinced he caused part of the disaster.

# 19. Local Resolution

The Greywake Arc must resolve its central local conflict.

By the end:

- the illegal march-lure network is dismantled or placed under transparent emergency control;
- Hesk loses the ability to secretly designate expendable communities;
- the immediate migration surge is survived and redirected;
- outer homesteads are not deliberately sacrificed;
- Greywake's main road is damaged enough that recovery has a cost;
- the settlement remains viable but cannot return immediately to the previous comfortable lie.

Hesk's final legal fate may be imprisonment, removal, or transfer for trial, but he cannot remain Roadmaster with hidden control.

The Chapter Roadmap may choose the exact legal result without changing the local resolution.

# 20. Consequences Left in Greywake

Permanent local consequences:

1. **Political:** Road Guild loses unilateral control of route safety data.
2. **Civic:** Outer Homestead Compact gains formal representation in emergency-route decisions.
3. **Economic:** Greywake's trade capacity temporarily falls.
4. **Infrastructure:** One or more major road/bridge sections require reconstruction.
5. **Institutional:** Ledger of Measures receives proof of serious reporting fraud.
6. **Social:** Red Jackal becomes a known local anomaly whose reputation is contradictory rather than universally heroic.
7. **Ecological:** Migration routes need seasons to normalize; Greywake cannot simply “reset” afterward.

# 21. Departure Condition

Red Jackal leaves Greywake only after:
- the immediate migration crisis is resolved;
- Hesk no longer controls the sacrificial policy;
- Red Jackal is no longer being held under Greywake's temporary anomaly concerns;
- he has gained enough local knowledge/resources to travel independently;
- staying would mean accepting a role other people increasingly want to assign him.

His departure motivation is therefore partly practical and partly personal:

**Greywake is the first place in Veyr that tries to decide what he is after seeing what he can do. He leaves before that definition becomes ownership.**

The destination after Greywake is **not selected in Phase 5**.
