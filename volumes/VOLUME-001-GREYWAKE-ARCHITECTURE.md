# Volume 1 Architecture — Greywake Arc

**Destination:** Greywake  
**Range:** CH001–CH026  
**Status:** CURRENT STRUCTURAL AUTHORITY — 2026-10-03  
**Story authority:** `planning/LUCK-EVERHART-STRUCTURAL-STORY-BIBLE.md`

# 1. Volume Purpose

Greywake introduces Luck Everhart, establishes his identity failure and accidental-legend engine, then tests the difference between **keeping a system working** and **deciding who is allowed to absorb its cost**.

Local question:

**How far may a frontier institution shift danger onto weaker communities to keep the settlement alive?**

Luck's personal question:

**Can he remain free when institutions and rumors keep assigning him a role?**

# 2. Opening State

Luck arrives:
- without a usable Earth identity;
- unable to read Veyran;
- physically mortal;
- with only passive Fortune Distortion;
- unaware that his luck is supernatural;
- already fond of theatrical presentation.

His immediate needs are food, shelter, information, money, and freedom.

# 3. Local System

Greywake depends on:
- the Main Caravan Road;
- outer farms/homesteads;
- road crews;
- militia;
- supply traffic;
- local production.

The Road Guild has practical power because road survival is settlement survival.

# 4. Antagonist

## Rovan Hesk

Hesk is not a cosmic villain.

He is a competent Roadmaster shaped by a past supply catastrophe. He believes Greywake survives only by prioritizing routes that cannot be allowed to fail.

His hidden lure policy redirects migration pressure away from the Main Caravan Road and toward politically weaker outer corridors.

His flaw is not caring about survival. It is deciding the acceptable victims in secret and then manipulating records to protect that choice.

# 5. Structural Movements

## I — The Undefined Stranger (CH001–CH003)
- forest survival;
- Luck chooses the name Luck Everhart;
- Greywake intake fails to classify him;
- physical measurements work while identity fields fail;
- Hesk notices an irregular worker opportunity.

## II — Frontier Work (CH004–CH008)
- Luck accepts bad but useful off-book work;
- main-road competence is established;
- Tavian's ecological expertise enters;
- Luck is injured and learns recklessness affects others;
- outer-route neglect becomes visible.

## III — The Road Is Lying (CH009–CH013)
- field evidence contradicts migration records;
- ledger evidence contradicts official safety claims;
- Broken North reveals hidden lure infrastructure;
- the team proves danger is being redirected deliberately;
- Hesk's policy becomes an open political conflict.

## IV — Necessary Losses (CH014–CH017)
- Hesk gives the strongest argument for maintaining the network;
- immediate outer danger pressures the group;
- premature intervention changes the system;
- backflow creates broader harm;
- Luck cannot treat the consequence as intentional genius.

## V — No Acceptable Loss (CH018–CH020)
- coalition builds a third option;
- specialists design a safer migration corridor;
- crews rebuild Broken North under pressure;
- Jessa exposes Hesk's retained lure resources.

## VI — Greywake Holds (CH021–CH024)
- evacuation and preparation;
- migration wave;
- infrastructure and predator crisis;
- Hesk's final lure threatens the sacrificial route;
- climax requires Tavian, Brynn, Maelis, Jessa, crews, and Luck.

## VII — What Greywake Counts (CH025–CH026)
- casualties, damage, evidence, and responsibility are counted;
- Road Guild authority is restructured;
- outer communities gain representation;
- Hesk's argument survives even after his defeat;
- Luck leaves before Greywake can define him permanently.

# 6. Fortune Distortion Use

Luck's supernatural role is limited to passive probability.

When a major lucky sequence occurs:
- the variables must already exist;
- a specialist may recognize mechanics better than Luck;
- Luck or another person must act on the opening;
- injury/property/political costs remain.

No scene uses:
- constructs;
- supernatural healing;
- time manipulation;
- raw super-strength;
- conscious probability control.

# 7. Supporting Character Functions

- **Tavian Rook:** ecology, tracking, migration geometry, rescue-first judgment.
- **Maelis Orra:** records, evidence, procedural truth, knowledge limits.
- **Brynn Alder:** militia command, public safety, lawful authority.
- **Kellan Dorr:** outer-community perspective, road/bridge knowledge, cost of policy.
- **Sela Arven:** injury consequence, medical reality, refusal to romanticize survival.
- **Jessa Vale:** Guild procedure, complicity, testimony, internal access.
- **Rovan Hesk:** survival logic, institutional secrecy, political antagonist.

None exists primarily to admire Luck.

# 8. Midpoint / Reversal / Climax

**Midpoint evidence shift:** hidden lure infrastructure and route overlays prove the migration pattern is engineered.

**Major reversal:** a premature lure intervention contributes to backflow and proves that being morally right about Hesk does not make every intervention safe.

**Climax:** Greywake survives through a prepared corridor, specialist coordination, ordinary infrastructure, and passive luck openings—not a solo power display.

# 9. End State

At CH026:
- Greywake survives;
- Hesk loses operational authority;
- the Road Guild is restructured;
- outer communities have enforceable voice;
- damage and injuries remain;
- Luck's identity remains unresolved;
- his reputation is contradictory;
- he leaves voluntarily and alone.

# Final Lock

Greywake is an investigation/logistics/political survival arc with accidental-legend comedy inside it. It is not a raw-power showcase.
