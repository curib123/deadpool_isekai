# 1. Volume Purpose

Merrowgate Arc expands the series from frontier survival into dense commercial, maritime, and urban pressure.

Greywake asked whether a frontier could justify sacrificing people to survive.

Merrowgate asks a different local question:

**Who owns a free port when every service that keeps it free can be bought?**

Red Jackal's personal version is:

**If nobody can officially define me, who gets to profit from the story people tell about me?**

The arc must stand as a complete Merrowgate story.

It is not secretly about Savael, the Exterior Needle, the Great Design, the final cosmology, or a hidden world-ending conspiracy.

# 3. Merrowgate Identity

Merrowgate is an independent Brasswater trade city positioned between Avarran and Kharadi influence.

Its volume identity must remain visible through:

- layered docks;
- chain bridges;
- dry docks;
- cargo cranes;
- warehouses;
- market towers;
- guild flags;
- foreign quarters;
- mixed Valic and Serekh customs;
- shipwright and mercenary subcultures;
- migrant labor;
- merchant reputation;
- private security;
- port bureaucracy;
- constant movement of goods and people.

Merrowgate is commercially independent rather than noble-centered.

People care less about pedigree than:
- contracts;
- solvency;
- reliability;
- cargo;
- reputation;
- who guarantees whose risk.

# 4. Why Red Jackal Becomes Involved

Red does not arrive intending to save Merrowgate.

He becomes involved because:

1. he needs money after ordinary travel costs;
2. he wants access to wider routes and passage options;
3. normal traveler and employment verification still fails on him;
4. off-book dock work pays well precisely because he is difficult to place inside standard liability systems;
5. rumors from Greywake make some people curious, skeptical, or willing to hire him;
6. he enjoys discovering that his reputation can sometimes open doors;
7. he underestimates how quickly a commercial city can turn reputation into a commodity;
8. a profitable irregular job connects him to the central scheme before he understands it.

He is not motivated by civic duty.

His involvement begins as convenience, money, curiosity, ego, and resistance to being commercially categorized.

# 5. Exact Local Conflict

Merrowgate's independence depends on many competing institutions balancing one another:

- Harbor Council;
- merchant guilds;
- shipwright houses;
- Open Hand Caravans;
- independent warehouse operators;
- smugglers;
- private security companies;
- foreign commercial agents.

No single institution is supposed to own the port's movement, inspections, security, debt, and emergency closures.

Over the past year, a sequence of seemingly unrelated incidents has made that decentralized model look increasingly unsafe:

- selective warehouse theft;
- cargo disappearances;
- forged inspection results;
- damaged moorings;
- privateer information leaks;
- false risk notices;
- targeted sabotage of smaller independent operators.

The incidents are real.

The pattern behind them is engineered.

A powerful merchant-security house has been creating and amplifying instability, then profiting from the resulting fear.

After each incident, the same network gains:
- distressed cargo;
- debt claims;
- insurance leverage;
- warehouse contracts;
- private guard contracts;
- political arguments for centralized control.

The scheme's political objective is the proposed **Unified Harbor Security Charter**.

If adopted, the charter would place emergency inspections, berth closures, major warehouse security, and crisis enforcement under one private commercial authority.

Merrowgate would remain legally independent.

In practice, one merchant house would gain the ability to decide what moves, what waits, what is considered dangerous, and who absorbs commercial loss.

# 6. Main Antagonist

## ANTAG-MG-001 — Cassian Venn

**Species:** Human  
**Age:** 47  
**Origin:** Merrowgate  
**Affiliation:** Venn Maritime House  
**Position:** Maritime assurance magnate, private-security patron, Harbor Council member  
**Status:** Volume 2 local antagonist

## 6.1 History

Cassian was raised inside a Merrowgate shipping family that lost vessels, warehouse access, and political standing during repeated foreign pressure and privateer crises.

He rebuilt the family position by combining:
- cargo assurance;
- debt purchasing;
- warehouse investment;
- convoy protection;
- private security.

He genuinely believes Merrowgate's independence survives only because larger powers find the port useful but difficult to control.

He sees fragmented harbor authority as a historic weakness waiting to be exploited.

## 6.2 Worldview

**A free port is only free while nobody outside it can close it. If the city will not centralize enough to protect itself, someone competent must do it for them.**

## 6.3 Goal

Secure passage of the Unified Harbor Security Charter and place Merrowgate's critical crisis infrastructure under Venn Maritime House control.

## 6.4 Methods

Cassian's network uses:

- selective sabotage;
- purchased informants;
- manipulated risk certification;
- deniable smugglers;
- false security intelligence;
- debt acquisition after manufactured losses;
- private guards;
- Harbor Council lobbying;
- commercial pressure;
- planted evidence;
- legitimate rescue/security work used to build public trust.

He does not control every criminal in Merrowgate.

Smugglers, rivals, and foreign agents have their own goals and sometimes disrupt his plans.

## 6.5 Resources

- merchant wealth;
- warehouses;
- insurance/assurance contracts;
- private security personnel;
- council access;
- legal advocates;
- shipping schedules;
- information brokers;
- commercial debt holdings;
- influence over some inspectors and dock supervisors.

## 6.6 Sympathy Boundary

Cassian is not lying about every danger.

Merrowgate genuinely faces:
- foreign economic pressure;
- privateers;
- smuggling;
- maritime monsters;
- trade sabotage;
- fragmented crisis authority.

He has funded real rescues and real port defenses.

His failure is that he now manufactures danger to prove only his preferred system can solve it.

## 6.7 Relationship to Red Jackal

Cassian initially sees Red as:

**a commercially useful irregular person with no stable registry identity and a growing frontier reputation.**

He does not know why Red is UNDEFINED.

He recognizes that Red's lack of ordinary identity makes him useful for:
- deniable labor;
- off-book movement;
- blame;
- spectacle;
- controlled rumor.

As Red becomes harder to direct, Cassian shifts from trying to use his reputation to trying to define it publicly.

## 6.8 Win Condition

Cassian wins if:

- enough port instability becomes publicly undeniable;
- Red or another irregular outsider can absorb blame for key sabotage;
- the Harbor Council invokes emergency authority;
- the Unified Harbor Security Charter passes;
- Venn Maritime House gains practical control over inspections, closures, and security.

## 6.9 Consequence If Victorious

Merrowgate stays open and nominally independent, but a private commercial monopoly gains the power to decide who may move through the port and who carries the cost of "security."

## 6.10 Consequence If Defeated

The scheme is exposed, the emergency charter fails or is revoked, Venn authority is broken, and Merrowgate must reform security without surrendering the port to one house.

Cassian is a local antagonist.

He has no protected cosmology knowledge.

# 8. Faction Architecture

## Harbor Council

Goal:
Keep Merrowgate independent, profitable, and governable.

Internal split:
Some members favor Venn's charter because they fear outside pressure and port disorder.
Others fear private capture more than temporary instability.

## Merchant Guilds

Goal:
Predictable trade and profit.

They are not one bloc.

Large houses may favor consolidation.
Smaller houses fear being priced out.

## Shipwright Houses / Independent Yards

Goal:
Maintain technical and commercial independence.

They become important because physical port safety cannot be solved by contracts alone.

## Open Hand Caravans

Goal:
Keep cross-border trade routes open without surrendering to a single local monopoly.

They provide a natural bridge between Greywake rumors and Merrowgate commerce.

## Smugglers

Goal:
Profit from fragmented enforcement.

They are neither heroic resistance nor a single villain faction.

Some unknowingly serve Cassian.
Some knowingly take his money.
Some oppose him because monopoly enforcement is bad for business.

## Private Security Companies

Goal:
Paid authority.

Some compete with Venn.
Some are absorbed by him.
Some resist because monopoly ends competition.

## Foreign Agents

Goal:
Advance outside commercial/political interests.

They complicate the conflict but are not the secret authors of the main scheme.

# 9. Red Jackal's Meaningful Mistake

Red must make one causally important mistake that reflects his established flaws rather than making him suddenly foolish.

He accepts a high-paying off-book port job because:

- normal verification is inconvenient;
- the money is useful;
- the irregularity amuses him;
- he likes proving that rules cannot easily stop him;
- he believes he can survive if the job becomes dangerous.

The job involves moving sealed commercial material through a route designed to avoid ordinary manifest scrutiny.

Red recognizes that the job is suspicious.

He does not know it is connected to Cassian's broader scheme.

His mistake is choosing **not to ask enough questions because being outside the paperwork feels like an advantage.**

The moved material later enables or protects one important sabotage operation.

The exact object, route, and chapter placement belong to Gate 7.

The consequence must affect people other than Red.

Regeneration cannot erase it.

# 10. Midpoint Shift

The midpoint revelation is local, evidentiary, and commercial:

**The apparently unrelated port incidents are producing a consistent pattern of profit, debt acquisition, and security influence for the same network.**

The cast does not yet possess complete proof that Cassian ordered every incident.

The story changes from:

**"Merrowgate is chaotic and somebody is exploiting it."**

to:

**"The chaos itself is being shaped into an argument for private control."**

Red realizes his irregular work may have been part of that system.

# 11. Major Reversal

Cassian's network exposes enough evidence of Red's off-book involvement to make him a credible public suspect in a major sabotage event.

The reversal must achieve all of the following:

- Red's Greywake reputation becomes a liability as well as an asset;
- Harbor Watch loses political room to treat him casually;
- Nessa's broker ambitions suffer because she helped route the job;
- an independent dock/crew suffers a serious material and human consequence;
- Cassian gains temporary political momentum;
- emergency consolidation becomes more likely;
- Red cannot solve the problem by leaving without accepting that his choice helped create the opening.

This is not a false accusation in which Red did nothing wrong.

He did not plan the sabotage.

He did choose the suspicious work that enabled part of it.

# 12. Non-Human / Environmental Pressure

Merrowgate must remain physically maritime.

The arc includes real environmental danger through:

- Brasswater storms;
- tides;
- unstable decks and cranes;
- ship collisions;
- water exposure;
- maritime monsters outside or near the port approaches where appropriate.

Cassian does not control the sea.

Natural danger creates pressure his scheme exploits but does not fully command.

Monster use must follow existing ecology and may not become random combat filler.

# 13. Action Escalation

Action escalates by type:

1. travel and arrival pressure;
2. dock work / urban physical hazards;
3. pursuit or confrontation in warehouses and crowded port routes;
4. sabotage investigation under commercial pressure;
5. shipyard or cargo-crane crisis;
6. public-security escalation after Red is implicated;
7. coordinated harbor emergency where infrastructure, weather, ships, guards, workers, and evidence all matter.

The climax is not a single duel.

# 14. Play Logic Architecture

**Volume 2 public-display plan: deliberately restrained utility/support manifestations unless a larger reveal is specifically authorized. This is not a capability ceiling.**

No power stage unlocks in this volume because Red has no power stages.

Any increase in precision, repeatability, or restraint is a **choice of presentation/application**, not growth in power or personality.

## 14.2 Approved Volume 2 Development

### PL-MG-1 — Familiar Utility Repeat

Red can recreate a small category of previously understood simple tools with slightly less conceptual drift.

This is improvement, not guaranteed precision.

### PL-MG-2 — Motion-Environment Utility

Red attempts Stage I constructs on moving decks, cranes, bridges, or unstable harbor surfaces.

Motion, noise, fear, and divided attention increase failure risk.

### PL-MG-3 — Sequential Coordination

Red can contribute several separate simple manifestations across a larger group plan.

### PL-MG-4 — Climax Support

During the harbor emergency, Play Logic may:

- brace;
- bridge;
- catch;

- redirect one small immediate hazard;
- create time for trained workers or guards.

It cannot:

- calm the whole sea;
- permanently repair the harbor;
- erase all ships or monsters;
- overwrite trade law;

# 15. Regeneration Architecture

Volume 2 continues the Greywake truth:

- Red heals abnormally;
- healing hurts;
- energy is finite;
- drowning, restraint, repeated impact, exhaustion, poison, magic, and other counters can still matter;
- regeneration does not restore judgment or precision;
- regeneration does not heal anyone else.

The port environment creates new ways survivability can fail to equal victory.

Water, restraint, falling loads, and enclosed spaces must remain credible threats.

# 16. Reputation / Aura Architecture

Merrowgate is the first destination where Red's reputation can arrive ahead of him through trade networks.

Required reputation outcomes:

- one Greywake story helps him;
- one exaggerated version creates fear or commercial interest;
- one person dismisses the story entirely;
- one aura-farming attempt succeeds;
- one is ignored because port workers are busy;
- one backfires because a dramatic action increases his perceived liability.

The arc must show that reputation is a resource other people can trade.

# 18. Character Movement / Stable Red Contrast

## 18.1 Red Jackal

Red does not undergo a moral-development arc.

- he likes the freedom of informal port work;
- he likes being a rumor people trade;

Moves from:

**procedure protects the port**

toward:

**procedure is only legitimate when public authority can see who benefits from it.**

## 18.3 Samir Kesran

## 18.4 Nessa Quill

## 18.5 Dalen Marr

Moves from trying to price every risk toward accepting that some forms of concentrated control create risks no contract can safely externalize.

# 21. Local Resolution

By the end of the Merrowgate Arc:

- the Unified Harbor Security Charter is defeated, revoked, or stripped of monopoly powers;
- Cassian loses practical control and enters local legal/commercial consequence;
- Venn Maritime House remains economically real but cannot retain the engineered emergency authority;
- some collaborators are exposed while unrelated smugglers/criminals remain unresolved local problems;

- smaller shipwright/warehouse operators gain stronger oversight access;
- the port suffers real repair and trade costs;
- Merrowgate remains messy, competitive, and independent rather than becoming purified.

# 22. Red Jackal Identity Consequence

Merrowgate does not solve Red's System identity.

A narrow local civic workaround may emerge:

A person whose System identity cannot validate may still enter a witnessed local contract under a declared name if living witnesses accept legal liability for the record.

This does not:
- create System registration;
- grant a class;
- prove his origin;
- bind other nations;
- make him a citizen automatically.

It gives Merrowgate a practical way to acknowledge agency without pretending the System has an answer.

Red may accept the practical benefit while refusing any claim that the city has defined what he is.
